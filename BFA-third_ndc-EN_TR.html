Foreword

Burkina Faso’s Nationally Determined Contribution (NDC) reflects its commitment to the global mitigation effort and its determination to strengthen its resilience, an absolute priority for its development, which is highly dependent on climate-sensitive sectors such as the environment, agriculture, livestock, water resources, energy, health and housing.

This NDC demonstrates the country’s consistency in raising its greenhouse gas (GHG) emission reduction ambitions in accordance with Article 4 of the Paris Agreement. From an initial ambition of 18.2% relative to the reference scenario in its 2015-2020 NDC, Burkina Faso moved to 29.42% in its 2021-2025 NDC. This momentum is maintained in NDC 3.0 at 30.87% by 2030. The GHG emission reduction potential of the adaptation and resilience-building actions in this NDC, relative to the reference scenario, is also estimated at 41.94% by 2030. The overall cost of meeting the commitments is USD 7 758 044 430.7, of which USD 4 956 913 742.1 has been mobilised and USD 2 801 130 688.7, i.e. 36.1%, remains to be sought.

The participatory and inclusive approach adopted for the preparation of NDC 3.0 strengthened the involvement of stakeholders, notably State institutions at central and deconcentrated levels, local authorities, civil society organisations, the private sector, technical and financial partners, local communities, women and young people. It also made it possible to take into account innovations such as (i) gender-transformative actions, (ii) youth and childhood, (iii) land tenure, (iv) climate security, (v) the WASH-climate nexus and (vi) cooling.

This NDC is in line with the country’s priorities as set out in the “Vision 2050 for low-carbon, climate-resilient development of Burkina Faso (LT-LEDS)”. It is also aligned with the country’s determination to strengthen the transparency, accountability and coherence of its climate policies.

In formulating its NDC 3.0, the country benefited from the support of technical and financial partners including the United Nations Development Programme (UNDP), the Global Green Growth Institute (GGGI), the Belgian development agency (ENABEL) and the NDC Partnership. I would like to express the Government’s gratitude to them. My thanks also go to the civil society organisations, the private sector, the members of the technical monitoring group, the consultants and all the actors whose involvement was necessary for the finalisation of NDC 3.0.

I remain convinced that it is through international mobilisation to finance the implementation of NDCs that we will, together and in time, achieve the key goal of the Paris Agreement, which is to limit the temperature increase to below 2°C by 2100. For its part, Burkina Faso will play its role in meeting its commitments under the legal instruments on climate.

Executive summary

Burkina Faso, a Sahelian country vulnerable to the adverse effects of climate change, has chosen to contribute to the global effort to combat climate change. As a Party to the United Nations Framework Convention on Climate Change, the Kyoto Protocol and the Paris Agreement, Burkina Faso has regularly translated this commitment into actions to reduce greenhouse gas (GHG) emissions at national level, with a view to holding the increase in global temperature well below 2 °C by 2100 relative to pre-industrial levels and pursuing efforts to limit this increase to 1.5 °C.

To this end, the country has prepared and submitted two Nationally Determined Contributions: the first in 2015, with a GHG emission reduction target for 2030 of 21 574.63 Gg CO2eq, i.e. 18.2% relative to the reference scenario, and the second in 2021, aiming to reduce GHG emissions by 31 632.85 GgCO2eq in 2030, i.e. 29.42%. The evaluations of these two NDCs show that the projected ambitions were not achieved in terms of GHG emission reductions because of (i) the lack of financing for most of the conditional projects and (ii) the late mobilisation of resources, which delayed the start of some unconditional projects.

In order to strengthen its commitments and comply with the requirements of the Paris Agreement, Burkina Faso has prepared this third Nationally Determined Contribution (NDC 3.0) for the period 2026-2030. This NDC is intended as a crucial step to consolidate the gains made since 2015 and to raise national ambition.

The preparation of NDC 3.0 was a structured process, led by the Permanent Secretariat of the National Council for Sustainable Development (SP/CNDD) and the NDC Partnership, with technical and financial support from UNDP, GGGI and ENABEL. The approach adopted was resolutely participatory and inclusive, involving State institutions at central and deconcentrated levels, local authorities, civil society organisations, the private sector, technical and financial partners, local communities, women and young people. Stakeholder consultations took the form of launch workshops, dialogues, data collection at national and regional levels, and multi-stakeholder validation workshops.

NDC 3.0 significantly raises the GHG emission reduction targets for 2030 and 2050 and is aligned with the long-term low-carbon, climate-resilient development strategy (LT-LEDS). Under the mitigation component, the country commits to reducing its greenhouse gas emissions by 30.87% by 2030 relative to the reference scenario, i.e. 26 241.81 Gg CO2 eq. This effort consists of 22.37% unconditional actions and 8.50% conditional actions. The contributing sectors are (i) Agriculture, (ii) Forestry and Other Land Use (FOLU), (iii) energy, (iv) transport, (v) Industrial Processes and Product Use (IPPU), (vi) waste and WASH.

For the adaptation component, a GHG emission reduction of 41.94% (i.e. 35 651.32 Gg CO2 eq) is planned by 2030, with 39.67% unconditional actions. The sectors targeted by adaptation measures are Agriculture, FOLU, water resources, livestock, waste and WASH, housing and infrastructure.

Beyond 2030, the overall GHG emission reduction ambition reaches 55.23% in 2035 and 75.55% in 2050 for mitigation, and 70.31% in 2035 and 84.40% in 2050 for adaptation.

In addition to quantified GHG reduction ambitions, NDC 3.0 incorporates major innovations, notably a paradigm shift in structuring its actions and projects as “areas of action” rather than sectoral projects. It also incorporates essential cross-cutting themes such as gender transformation and social inclusion, children and youth, loss and damage, climate security, land issues, the WASH-climate nexus, the circular economy and cooling.

Implementing these GHG reduction ambitions requires an estimated investment of USD 7 758 044 430.7, of which USD 4 956 913 742.1 for unconditional actions and USD 2 801 130 688.7 for conditional actions, i.e. 36.1% of the overall cost. To achieve this, Burkina Faso intends to mobilise resources through international mechanisms (GCF, GEF, AF, FRLD) as well as by exploiting the opportunities offered by Article 6 of the Paris Agreement and REDD+ through the carbon market.

Accountability and progress tracking will be ensured by a Transparency and Monitoring Framework (MRV) steered by the Permanent Secretariat of the National Council for Sustainable Development (SP/CNDD).

The risks to the implementation of NDC 3.0 have been assessed and are accompanied by a matrix of mitigation measures, to be regularly reassessed and updated in order to identify management actions to be integrated.

Introduction

Burkina Faso, as a country vulnerable to the adverse effects of climate change, has chosen to contribute to the global effort to stabilise atmospheric concentrations of Greenhouse Gases (GHG). As a Party to the United Nations Framework Convention on Climate Change (UNFCCC), the Kyoto Protocol and the Paris Agreement, the country has regularly committed to implementing GHG emission reduction actions at national level in order to hold the increase in global temperature well below 2 °C by 2100 relative to pre-industrial levels and to pursue efforts to limit this increase to 1.5 °C.

The country translated its commitment to reducing GHG emissions by submitting its Intended Nationally Determined Contribution (INDC) on 23 October 2015, which subsequently became its Nationally Determined Contribution (NDC) upon its ratification of the Paris Agreement. This first NDC set GHG emission reduction targets for 2030 of 21 574.63 Gg CO2eq, i.e. 18.2% relative to the reference scenario (Business As Usual) for mitigation actions, and 43 707 Gg CO2eq, i.e. 36.95%, for adaptation actions. For the period 2015-2020, Burkina Faso planned a reduction of 5133 Gg CO2eq, representing 5.58% under the unconditional scenario, and 10953 Gg CO2eq, i.e. 11.9%, under the conditional scenario for mitigation actions.

The evaluation of the first NDC in 2020 showed a reduction of 4 858.07 Gg CO2eq, i.e. 5.3% of the unconditional scenario, and 2 643.5 Gg CO2eq, i.e. 2.9% of the conditional scenario. The level of achievement of the commitment for the period 2015-2020 is 91.37% for the unconditional scenario and 24.36% for the conditional scenario. As regards adaptation actions, the country’s commitment in terms of GHG reduction, which amounted to 43 707Gg CO2eq in 2030, reached an achievement level of 89% in 2020, i.e. a reduction of 38 898 Gg CO2eq of GHG.

Burkina Faso embarked on its second NDC cycle (2021-2025). The initial GHG reduction ambitions of this NDC were 15 028.51 GgCO2eq in 2025 (16.25% relative to the reference scenario), 31 632.85 GgCO2eq in 2030 (29.42%) and 63 974.43 GgCO2eq in 2050 (34.43%). This NDC also aimed to strengthen climate resilience while integrating gender and social inclusion as cross-cutting issues. Compared with the 2016 NDC, which was 18.2%, the Burkinabe State raised its ambitions by 11.22 percentage points for the 2021 NDC. To achieve this 29.42% target, an amount of US$ 449 118 465.3 was planned under the unconditional scenario against US$ 885 670 522 expected for the conditional scenario, i.e. a total of US$ 1 334 788 987.

With a view to revising the NDC in order to strengthen its commitments and comply with the requirements of the Paris Agreement, Burkina Faso carried out a qualitative evaluation and a quantitative evaluation of its 2021 NDC.

The results of the evaluations of the 2021 NDC in terms of GHG emission reductions show low achievement rates (less than 11%) because of the lack of financing for more than 75% of the conditional projects and the late mobilisation of resources, which prevented 30% of the unconditional projects from beginning implementation. For the mitigation component, the evaluation results give reductions of 1 614.58 GgCO2eq in 2025, 2 303.37 GgCO2eq in 2030 and 5 920.65 GgCO2eq in 2050, i.e. respectively 10.9%, 7.4% and 9.4% of the reduction potential planned for these periods. The majority of conditional projects (83.33%), which have a high GHG reduction potential, were not financed, which pulled overall performance down and moved the NDC away from its targets. In addition, 30.77% of the financed projects in the unconditional component have not yet begun implementation for several reasons, including the late mobilisation of financing and cumbersome procedures.

The 2021 NDC generated significant socio-economic co-benefits alongside GHG reductions, producing an appreciable economic return of more than 26%. The 2021 NDC made significant progress in taking gender into account, but challenges remain in strengthening the gender-sensitive and transformative character of the NDC.

These two evaluations provided relevant information and data for the formulation of the country’s NDC 3.0. This revision aims not only to raise mitigation and adaptation ambitions but also to integrate in a structured way certain cross-cutting themes such as gender transformation and social inclusion, children and youth, climate security, the WASH-climate nexus, loss and damage, the circular economy and sectoral innovations.

The NDC 3.0 document is structured in nine parts: (i) the diagnostic analysis of the national context, (ii) the preparation methodology, (iii) GHG reduction ambitions, (iv) sectoral contributions to climate ambition, (v) gender transformation and social inclusion, (vi) consideration of cross-cutting themes, (vii) the transparency and progress-tracking framework, (viii) financing and implementation mechanisms, (ix) the analysis of risks and mitigation measures.

1. Diagnostic analysis of the national context

This section gives a brief overview of the sectors or areas related to climate change and greenhouse gas (GHG) emissions.

1.1 Biophysical setting

Burkina Faso is a Sahelian country in West Africa, located between latitudes 9°20’ and 15°05’ North and longitudes 2°20’ East and 5°30’ West. It covers an area of about 272 969 km² and is bordered by Mali to the north and west, Niger to the east, and Côte d’Ivoire, Ghana, Togo and Benin to the south (MATD, 2021). Burkina Faso’s climate is tropical with two distinct seasons: a dry season from November to May, dominated by the harmattan, and a rainy season from June to October, linked to the West African monsoon. The country is divided into three main climatic zones (Guinko, 1984; Barry et al., 2018): Sahelian zone (north, < 600 mm/year); Sudano-Sahelian zone (centre, 600–900 mm/year); Sudanian zone (south, 900–1200 mm/year).

Burkina Faso’s vegetation cover is strongly influenced by the rainfall gradient. According to the classification of Guinko (1984) and Fontès & Guinko (1995), the country has three major vegetation zones: Sahelian zone: shrub steppes with Acacia tortilis, Balanites aegyptiaca, Combretum glutinosum; Sudano-Sahelian zone: tree savannas dominated by Vitellaria paradoxa, Parkia biglobosa, Combretum nigricans and a

Sudanian zone: open forests and wooded savannas with Isoberlinia doka, Daniellia oliveri, Khaya senegalensis. However, land degradation and deforestation are progressing rapidly under the effect of human pressure, bush fires and climate hazards (FAO, 2020; MEADD, 2021). The deforestation rate is estimated at about 2.8% per year, i.e. more than 105 000 hectares lost each year (FAO, 2020).

1.2. Social and economic dynamics

Burkina Faso has about 22 million inhabitants in 2025, with a young population: 50% are aged 18 or under (SoAC, 2025) and more than 60% are under 25 (INSD, 2022). According to Ouédraogo (2019), demographic pressure creates strong demand on natural resources and limited infrastructure, exacerbating socio-economic vulnerability. The country has a predominantly agricultural economy, accounting for about 30% of GDP and employing more than 80% of the working population (World Bank, 2022). Agriculture is largely rain-fed, with low yields, which makes the country vulnerable to climate shocks. Transport and energy infrastructure is insufficient. Most of the rural population has no access to electricity or quality health services. According to Sawadogo (2021), these gaps limit industrialisation and the local processing of agricultural products, holding back economic diversification. The economy rests mainly on:

  • agriculture: a key sector, accounting for nearly 30% of GDP and employing more than 80% of the working population (FAO, 2022). Cash crops (cotton, shea) and food crops (millet, sorghum, maize) dominate (Sanou et al., 2020).
  • mining: gold, zinc and manganese, which contribute significantly to exports and tax revenue (Ouédraogo, 2021).
  • services and trade: growing rapidly, particularly in urban areas, with a very large informal sector (Kinda, 2019).

1.3. Climate vulnerability profile

Burkina Faso is ranked among the countries most vulnerable to climate change according to the ND-GAIN index (2023). The country is also ranked 15th out of 163 countries in the children’s climate risk analysis (UNICEF, 2023). The main ecological degradation processes include: (i) desertification (about 34% of the territory affected), (ii) water and wind erosion, (iii) biodiversity loss and (iv) increasing scarcity of water resources (SP/CNDD, 2022). These phenomena weaken productive ecosystems and threaten the food and water security of rural populations, more than 80% of whom depend directly on natural resources (MEEA, 2023). Soil degradation, desertification and climate hazards directly affect livelihoods (Ouédraogo et al., 2019).

Net national GHG emissions show a general upward trend, rising from 36122.07 Gg CO2 eq in 1990 to 72300.9 Gg CO2 eq in 2022 (MEEA, 2024). Table 1 shows the historical trend in emissions by sector. 1.5. Foundations of climate action

1.5.1. Policy framework

Burkina Faso has integrated climate action into its national development planning through several strategic documents:

  • The Action Plan for the Stabilisation of Development, whose strategic objective 4.5 aims to reverse trends in environmental degradation;
  • The National Sustainable Development Policy (PNDD), adopted in 2013, which “determines the means to achieve the sustainable development goals (legal, institutional, operational, financial)”;
  • The national climate security strategy (2025-2029) sets out appropriate responses to the adverse effects of climate change that exacerbate conflicts, risks of instability, social tensions and unwanted migration;
  • The National Climate Change Adaptation Plan (NAP) (2024-2028), whose vision is: “Burkina Faso manages its economic and social development more effectively through the implementation of planning mechanisms and measures that take resilience and adaptation to climate change into account by 2050”;
  • The Vision 2050 for low-carbon, climate-resilient development of Burkina Faso (LT-LEDS), which charts Burkina Faso’s path towards a low-carbon, climate-resilient economy generating green jobs and wealth in areas of high environmental impact by 2050;
  • The climate transparency framework, whose objective is to facilitate the tracking of progress and of the sustainable development impact of climate actions.

1.5.2. Legal framework

The legal framework presents not only the international instruments to which the country has subscribed but also national laws on climate change. These instruments include, among others:

  • International climate conventions and agreements ratified by Burkina Faso. These are mainly the United Nations Framework Convention on Climate Change, ratified on 2 September 1993, and the Paris Agreement on climate (2015), ratified on 11 November 2016. They commit the Parties to reducing GHG emissions through NDCs, mobilising resources for the resilience of communities and ecosystems and strengthening the transparency of climate action;
  • The Constitution of Burkina Faso, which accepts the principle of sustainable development and affirms the absolute need to protect the environment;
  • The Framework Law on sustainable development in Burkina Faso, whose purpose is to set the general guidelines for implementing sustainable development in Burkina Faso;
  • Law No. 024-2018/AN on the framework law on spatial planning and sustainable territorial development in Burkina Faso, which sets the fundamental principles of spatial planning and sustainable territorial development and aims to promote social progress, economic efficiency and environmental protection in keeping with Community spatial planning policies;
  • Law No. 006-2013/AN of 2 April 2013 on the Environment Code in Burkina Faso, which sets the fundamental rules governing the environment in Burkina Faso;
  • Law No. 003-2011/AN on the forestry code in Burkina Faso, which sets the fundamental principles for the sustainable management and enhancement of forest, wildlife and fishery resources;
  • Law No. 012-2014/AN on the framework law on the prevention and management of risks, humanitarian crises and disasters, whose purpose is the prevention and management of risks, humanitarian crises and disasters in Burkina Faso, whatever their nature, origin and scale.

In addition to these texts, there are sector-specific texts that govern particular areas and help organise the country’s action on climate change. These include, among others: Decree No. 2024_0305 establishing the national system for Measurement, Reporting and Verification of climate actions in Burkina Faso (MRV/BF) and Decree No. 2024/0340 on the National Committee on Climate Change (CNACC).

1.5.3. Institutional framework

For several decades Burkina Faso has had a structured institutional framework for climate governance in order to coordinate climate change mitigation and adaptation actions and to integrate the climate dimension into sectoral policies, planning and budgeting. This framework is also conducive to the mobilisation of international resources.

The institutional framework for climate governance in Burkina Faso is coordinated by the Ministry of Environment, Water and Sanitation (MEEA) through the Permanent Secretariat of the National Council for Sustainable Development (SP/CNDD), which hosts the UNFCCC focal point and coordinates and monitors national climate policies. This system relies on consultation frameworks such as the National Conference for Sustainable Development (CONADD), the National Committee on Climate Change (CNACC), the Single Consultation Framework for Environmental Conventions (CCUC), the consultation framework of national focal structures on climate finance (CCSFN-FC), the GCF structured dialogue, the National REDD+ Committee and the National Academy on the Green Economy. Sectoral ministries, local authorities, research institutions, the private sector, civil society organisations, non-governmental organisations and technical and financial partners take part in implementing and monitoring the NDC and other national climate policies. Despite this architecture, challenges remain in intersectoral coordination, financing and local capacity building.

1.5.4 Analysis of technical resources

The SP/CNDD has a Partnership and Resource Mobilisation unit responsible, among other things, for supporting national actors in mobilising financial resources to implement climate action. A consultation framework of national focal structures on climate finance has been set up. It brings together all national actors involved in mobilising climate finance. This framework works to build actors’ capacities on the mechanisms and processes of climate funds.

Synergy between actors enables the design and maturation of bankable climate projects for greater mobilisation of finance. To strengthen this synergy, a national climate finance platform is being operationalised and will serve as a framework for sharing and exchanging information on climate finance opportunities.

To encourage greater mobilisation of climate finance, capacity-building sessions are planned for the actors.

These actions can help increase climate finance for the effective implementation of NDC 3.0.

2. Methodology for preparing NDC 3.0

This section presents the main stages of the NDC 3.0 formulation process, the GHG emission reduction calculations and the project selection approach.

2.1. Participatory process and stakeholder dialogue

The process of preparing Burkina Faso’s NDC 3.0, led by the Ministry of Environment, Water and Sanitation through the SP/CNDD, was participatory and inclusive. A technical monitoring group was set up to follow and guide the team of experts. The process began with evaluations (quantitative and qualitative) of the 2021-2025 NDC and continued with a series of dialogue and data collection workshops, at both national and local levels, involving a wide range of stakeholders. The actors mobilised included State institutions, local authorities, the private sector, Technical and Financial Partners (TFPs), Non-Governmental Organisations (NGOs) and civil society actors.

The process also achieved gender-sensitive social inclusion, with the participation of women, young people and vulnerable people in the workshops for identifying and selecting projects and validating deliverables. Beyond the participation of young people and women in the preparation process, NDC 3.0 fully incorporates projects led by young people, women and local authorities.

The inclusive and iterative preparation process gives NDC 3.0 a role not only as a technical mitigation and adaptation instrument but also as a tool for community cohesion and social transformation.

2.2. Methods for calculating GHG emission reductions

Once projects are retained, activity data (AD) are sought, which makes it possible to calculate greenhouse gas (GHG) emission reductions. With reference to the theoretical frameworks and basic approaches adopted in the NDC, two tools for calculating GHG emission reductions were used: (i) the EX-ACT tool and (ii) the GACMO tool.

Version 9.4.2 of FAO’s “EX-Ante Carbon-Balance Tool (EX-ACT)” was used to calculate the level of GHG emission reductions in the AFOLU, Waste and WASH, and Water resources sectors.

The GACMO tool (Greenhouse Gas Abatement Cost Model) is a model developed by the International Energy Agency (IEA) to assess the costs and potential of greenhouse gas (GHG) emission reductions in different economic sectors. The processing and analysis of results were carried out with the GACMO software. This tool was used for the Energy, Transport, Housing and Infrastructure sectors.

GHG emissions and/or removals related to the different activities are estimated using the following formula:

E = AD × EF; where E = Emission; AD = Activity data; EF = Emission factor

For most sectors, tier 1 was used for emission factors.

The overall mitigation potential (Bnet) of the project is estimated using the following formula:

Bnet = Net emissions with project - Net emissions without project

A negative Bnet means that the project contributes to reducing GHG emissions, whereas a positive Bnet means an increase in emissions.

2.4. Main innovations of NDC 3.0

The main innovations of NDC 3.0 are as follows: (i) alignment with the long-term low-carbon strategy that embodies Burkina Faso’s vision 2050 for low-carbon, climate-resilient development, (ii) the paradigm shift in structuring NDC 3.0 in the form of “areas of action” in which actions and projects will be housed, (iii) the explicit consideration of cross-cutting themes, including gender transformation, children and youth, climate security, land issues, the

WASH-Climate Nexus, loss and damage, the circular economy and cooling. These innovations are developed in specific sections of this document.

The paradigm shift in structuring NDC 3.0 in the form of “areas of action” is justified by the observation that the national effort to reduce GHG emissions is a continuous process, through the preparation and financing of sectoral projects and programmes, which continues after the adoption of NDCs. Adopting a structure of “areas of action”, in which actions and projects will be housed, allows projects ready at the time to be included in the NDC, while opening the possibility for projects prepared and implemented after the adoption of NDC 3.0 to be counted in the “areas of action” defined in the NDC 3.0 document, which allows a fairer valuation and assessment of the national GHG emission reduction effort. For example, the qualitative evaluation of the 2021 NDC (2021-2025) showed the existence of more than one hundred (100) projects adopted after 2021 whose GHG reductions are not counted, owing to a lack of flexibility and openness in the NDC planning structure to allow their ex-post accounting. Structuring NDC 3.0 in the form of “areas of action” makes it possible to: (i) correct this under-accounting of the national GHG reduction effort, (ii) offset the non-financing of some conditional projects explicitly included in the NDC by other projects that would emerge after the adoption of the NDC and play the same role, except that the predictability of financing did not allow them to be included.

3.2.2 Contribution to resilience and livelihoods

5. Gender transformation and social inclusion

Climate change and gender inequality are inextricably linked. Disparities between men and women can amplify the vulnerability of certain groups. They can also limit their resilience to climate change and their ability to contribute to reducing GHG emissions, hence the importance of taking gender and inclusion into account in the NDC preparation process. Thus, in keeping with the spirit of the United Nations Framework Convention on Climate Change, NDC 3.0 takes into account all categories of actors, notably civil society organisations (CSOs), local authorities, women, young people, children and people with disabilities.

5.1. Challenges and priorities

In view of the strengths and weaknesses observed in the 2021 NDC and the recommendations from the quantitative and qualitative evaluations, three (03) major challenges were identified for the formulation of NDC 3.0, as follows:

  • Maintaining and strengthening the gains of the 2021 NDC in terms of gender and social inclusion;
  • Identifying gender-sensitive indicators: one of the weaknesses observed in the 2021 NDC was the lack or insufficiency of indicators to track progress on Gender and Social Inclusion (GSI). To correct this shortcoming, related indicators have been defined and attached to the actions or projects and to the sectors of the NDC.
  • Inclusion of actors. The conditions were met to ensure the formal involvement of all categories of actors at both national and local levels.

To meet these challenges, priority was given to aligning this NDC with national reference frameworks on gender and climate, such as the national gender strategy, the gender and climate change action plan and the directory of good practices in adaptation and mitigation.

5.2. Objectives and targets

The objective is to ensure that taking gender and social inclusion into account, in addition to promoting the economic empowerment of women and young people, results in an NDC that is more ambitious, more inclusive and positioned as a lever for increasing resource mobilisation, while ensuring compliance with the country’s international commitments in this area.

In terms of targets, the aim during the revision was to achieve a participation rate of at least 30% women and 15% young people, and to ensure that actions led by women, young people, people with disabilities and local authorities are taken into account in the NDC.

6. Consideration of cross-cutting themes

In addition to the gender-transformative approach, seven cross-cutting themes are integrated into NDC 3.0: (i) children and youth, (ii) climate security, with particular emphasis on conflict sensitivity, (iii) land issues, (iv) the WASH–Climate nexus, (v) loss and damage, (vi) the circular economy and (vii) cooling. These themes will be integrated through three main levers: (i) projects and actions specifically focused on these issues, (ii) the risk mitigation measures associated with them, and (iii) the disaggregation of indicators to report on inequalities, inequities and/or progress made

6.1. Children and youth

This theme covers all issues concerning children, from birth to puberty, and young people, i.e. those between puberty and adulthood. Children, especially girls and those from vulnerable communities, are highly exposed to the adverse effects of climate change. Their physiological and developmental characteristics make them particularly sensitive to impacts that are sometimes irreversible and may durably affect their growth, health and schooling. The fundamental rights to be promoted for these groups include: (i) education, (ii) health, (iii) nutrition, (iv) protection against violence, (v) access to water, sanitation and hygiene, and (vi) the meeting of humanitarian needs and emergencies.

Several national and international institutions, including UNICEF, work to defend and promote these fundamental rights in collaboration with other stakeholders, including traditional and community leaders, through projects and programmes. This work integrates climate resilience, education, health, social protection and gender equality, creating environments conducive to youth participation. Particular attention is paid to adolescent girls, who face intersecting challenges linked to gender inequality, poverty and climate vulnerability. The projects and actions of NDC 3.0 aim to take children’s and youth issues into account. In addition to specific projects addressing these themes, allocated to the sectors defined in NDC 3.0, they are taken into account in a cross-cutting way through the disaggregation of indicators to report on inequalities, inequities and/or progress made, and through the risk mitigation measures associated with these themes.

6.2. Climate security

6.2.1. National orientations on climate security

Climate security refers to the response to insecurity created or exacerbated by the effects of climate change on populations and their livelihoods. This insecurity takes the form of repeated tensions, conflicts over the use of natural resources, climate migration and risks of instability linked wholly or partly to climate change.

Burkina Faso has prepared a National Climate Security Strategy (2025-2029) and an operational action plan to respond to these challenges. Regional initiatives in the Liptako Gourma area aim to harmonise tools for managing climate-related security risks, including through local dialogues, mediation and resilience capacity building. The vision of this Climate Security Strategy is: “By 2029, the population of Burkina Faso strengthens its resilience to climate change and lives in peace and security for equitable, inclusive and sustainable growth”. This vision could be realised by achieving the overall objective, which is to “strengthen the resilience of populations to climate hazards with a view to improving human security in Burkina Faso”. The strategy is broken down into three (03) axes: (i) improving climate security governance; (ii) sustainable management of natural resources and promotion of climate justice, (iii) improving human protection against disaster risks and humanitarian crises.

6.2.2. Sensitivity to conflicts induced by climate change

Climate change increases the vulnerability of populations, particularly in the rural areas of the Sahel. Its effects give rise to conflicts, weaken social relations and undermine traditional regulatory mechanisms. Climate hazards exacerbate tensions over resources through the shortening of the rainy season and the decline in agricultural productivity, the advance of the desertification front and the silting of lowlands, pressure on water resources, the decline in pastoral resources (water points, pastures), and the increase in internal and cross-border migration linked to droughts. These factors cause increased scarcity of vital resources, which fuels rivalries between users (farmers, herders, forest operators, etc.). Integrating conflict sensitivity into NDC 3.0 profoundly transforms its scope. It makes it possible to go beyond a purely climate logic to embrace essential social and institutional dimensions:

Thus, NDC 3.0 is no longer merely a technical instrument for GHG emission reduction and/or adaptation, but becomes a tool for peace, collective resilience and societal transformation.

6.3. Land issues

Integrating land tenure into NDC 3.0 aims to ensure both sustainable management of natural resources (land, forests, etc.) and full and inclusive participation of local communities and effective protection of their rights, while promoting actions to combat climate change. Land governance is one of the foundations of local communities’ climate resilience.

Land management in Burkina Faso is governed by several laws and regulations, including: Law 015-2025/ALT of 21 October 2025 on agrarian and land reorganisation (RAF), the framework law on pastoralism, the national policy on spatial planning and sustainable development, and the national policy on rural land tenure security. The modernisation and digitalisation of land services make them more accessible and more transparent, with the launch of the Integrated Cadastre and Domain Management System platform (SYC@D).

The implementation of NDC 3.0 projects and actions and the related land issues will be handled in accordance with the legal and regulatory provisions in force. Emphasis will be placed on promoting and securing the land rights of local communities through certificates of rural land possession (APFR) on the one hand, and of commonly used natural resources through local land charters on the other. It will also involve capitalising on and sharing data on land management at local level in relation to climate change, and building stakeholders’ capacities on land management in Burkina Faso.

6.4. WASH-Climate nexus

The link between water, sanitation and hygiene (WASH) and climate change is now widely recognised as a strategic issue for the resilience of rural and urban communities in Burkina Faso (MEEA, 2024). This WASH–Climate nexus refers to the interdependence between the sustainability of WASH services and the effects of climate hazards, and to the need to integrate climate change adaptation into the planning, financing and management of these essential services (WaterAid, 2024). The WASH sector is both highly vulnerable to the effects of climate change and a vector of resilience for populations exposed to environmental shocks. Equitable, sustainable and resilient access to water, sanitation and hygiene must therefore be guaranteed in a context of increasing climate stress.

6.5. Loss and Damage

NDCs 3.0 must reflect not only mitigation and adaptation ambitions but also the consideration of loss and damage, which constitutes the third pillar of climate action.

Burkina Faso recognises that, despite its sustained mitigation and adaptation efforts, the adverse effects of climate change are already causing economic and non-economic loss and damage. The repeated droughts that occurred between 1970 and 2012 caused human and livestock losses, led to large population migrations and considerably reduced the level of watercourses. These events worsened the food crisis, affecting more than 2.6 million Burkinabe in severe food insecurity.

In addition, recurrent floods were recorded between 1988 and 2012, leaving thousands homeless, destroying vast areas of crops and decimating millions of head of livestock. Successive desert locust invasions, notably in 1908, 1948, 1961, 1995, 1996 and 2004, also led to famines (NAP 2024-2028). Aware of the irreversible nature of some of these losses, the country intends to integrate the management of loss and damage as an essential pillar of its climate response under NDC 3.0.

To this end, it commits to strengthening national systems for monitoring/evaluating and managing loss and damage.

Burkina Faso seeks the cooperation and support of the international community, notably through the Fund for responding to Loss and Damage and the Santiago Network, in order to strengthen its national capacities, improve early warning systems, support climate insurance mechanisms and support the recovery of affected communities. Through this approach, Burkina Faso reaffirms its determination to adopt an integrated and equitable approach to climate change, ensuring that the most vulnerable populations are not left behind in the transition to resilient and sustainable development.

6.6. Circular economy

The Burkinabe economy remains largely linear, characterised by unsustainable consumption and production patterns, heavy pressure on natural resources and inadequate management and recovery of the waste produced. Identifying and implementing actions or projects in the priority circular economy sectors of NDC 3.0 thus raises the country’s level of ambition and contributes to the sustainable management of natural resources, sanitation and the creation of green jobs, while supporting a just and inclusive transition. To this end, green or circular economy opportunities that can reduce GHG emissions in certain priority sectors of NDC 3.0 will be identified and seized.

The desired change is to structurally transform this linear economy into a circular model that advocates refusal, reduction at source, reuse and recycling of waste. This will be reflected in coordinated consideration of strategic frameworks, awareness-raising, communication and education actions on the circular economy, and efficient management of materials and waste.

6.7. Cooling

Burkina Faso, as a signatory to the Global Cooling Pledge, commits to turning global ambition into concrete action in the cooling sector, which includes domestic, commercial and industrial refrigeration as well as air conditioning of buildings and vehicles. The country thus aims to align its NDC 3.0 with the sustainable cooling pathway presented in the Global Cooling Watch 2025 report, which could reduce global cooling-related emissions by 64% by 2050 relative to the business-as-usual scenario.

Burkina Faso consumes about 419 954 kg of refrigerants annually, mainly HFC-134a and HCFC-22. The largest direct emissions come from commercial air conditioning (about 49%), followed by mobile air conditioning (27%) and domestic refrigeration (13%). The growth of the equipment stock and rising temperatures are expected to increase direct and indirect emissions. By 2035, the country aims to reduce by 30% the number of people exposed to a high risk of extreme heat, by expanding access to energy-efficient solutions, improving building design and integrating nature-based solutions.

Integrating cooling as an essential climate service into NDC 3.0 aims, on the one hand, to mitigate climate change by controlling the energy consumption and greenhouse gas emissions linked to air conditioning and refrigeration. This will be achieved by promoting efficiency standards, passive technologies and environmentally friendly refrigerants, in accordance with the Montreal Protocol and the Kigali Amendment. On the other hand, this approach aims to strengthen the resilience of key sectors such as health, agriculture and housing. This includes developing solar cold chains, promoting bioclimatic housing and strengthening the resilience of health infrastructure to heatwaves.

Taking cooling into account in NDC 3.0 could face several challenges, notably access to reliable energy, the high cost of equipment, the need to build capacity and promote technology transfer, and an essential need to mobilise international finance to support these initiatives.

7. Transparency and progress-tracking framework

The transparency framework is a crucial element for strengthening trust and international cooperation. It aims to ensure rigorous assessment of the implementation of NDC 3.0 through specific indicators, an institutional Measurement, Reporting and Verification system and a targeted communication strategy.

7.1. NDC progress-tracking indicators

Identifying and defining SMART indicators aligned with the SDGs and based on the actions of the various projects to be included in the NDC is necessary to ensure proper monitoring of the implementation of NDC 3.0. To this end, SMART indicators have been defined by sector. These indicators, formulated on the basis of each project and by sector, are aligned with the targets of the Sustainable Development Goals (SDGs) and will be reported with the help of the focal points and the implementation managers of the various NDC projects and programmes, in collaboration with the DGESS. The directory of these indicators is in the annex.

7.2. Institutional reporting and transparency arrangements

Monitoring and evaluation of climate change actions in general, and of the NDC specifically, is coordinated by the Ministry in charge of the environment through the SP/CNDD. The establishment of the national Measurement, Reporting and Verification system (MRV/BF), steered by the SP/CNDD, has given the country an inclusive and sustainable institutional and legal framework and an effective, functional system for collecting and capitalising on data to track progress in implementing the NDC. The MRV system is fed by the sectoral monitoring and evaluation systems of the ministries involved in the NDC.

7.3. Monitoring and evaluation arrangements

In order to ensure effective monitoring of NDC 3.0, an implementation monitoring and evaluation mechanism has been built around the MRV system. It is coordinated by the SP/CNDD in collaboration with the NDC focal points. In accordance with the MRV decree, a

Coordination unit comprising three technical bodies has been set up. These are: i) an “adaptation” technical body responsible for capitalising on adaptation-related data, ii) a “mitigation” technical body responsible for capitalising on data related to mitigation projects and iii) a support body responsible for capitalising on information related to support received. Civil society and other stakeholders will contribute to monitoring and evaluation by monitoring their projects included in the NDC and by taking part in the dedicated bodies.

Better functioning of the national MRV system necessarily requires (i) the development of a climate-finance database linked to the MRV platform for tracking NDC financing, (ii) building the capacities of actors, in particular focal points, on the use of the MRV platform, (iii) building actors’ capacities on the tools of the Enhanced Transparency Framework for tracking progress in NDC implementation and (iv) operationalising the national MRV system as the data collection system for tracking progress in NDC implementation.

7.4. Communication strategy

Communication is a strategic element in the implementation of NDC 3.0. Media and non-media communication actions will be developed to strengthen its visibility and ownership by all actors at both national and local levels. A realistic and achievable communication plan, with particular emphasis on mass communication, notably through the use of national languages with suitable Information and Communication Technologies (ICT), should be developed by the country in order to: (i) mobilise the resources needed to implement the NDC, (ii) secure the commitment of central and local, public and private actors to implementing the NDC.

In addition, initiatives such as continuing to organise round tables with TFPs and government and parliamentary seminars could increase the mobilisation of finance.

Achieving the ambitions of NDC 3.0 will require strong mobilisation of financial resources for conditional actions. To this end, a resource mobilisation strategy suited to the needs of the NDC and to the context of Burkina Faso should be put in place. This strategy aims to ensure sustainable, coordinated and equitable financing of the NDC’s priority conditional actions (mitigation and adaptation) by maximising access to national, bilateral, multilateral and private resources. It will focus on:

  • Strengthening the institutional and governance framework. This involves setting up a national climate finance coordination mechanism and the genuine involvement of all sectoral ministries, in particular the ministry in charge of finance, in mobilising climate finance.
  • Mobilising national public resources. This action will be carried out through (i) the operationalisation of climate-sensitive budgeting (BSC), (ii) advocacy for an increase in national climate allocations, and (iii) the creation of a functional climate window within the Environmental Intervention Fund.
  • Increased mobilisation of international finance (bilateral and multilateral). This will require (i) turning conditional projects into solid bankable proposals for the GCF, AF, GEF, LDCF, FRLD, etc., and (ii) strengthening advocacy with Technical and Financial Partners (World Bank, AfDB, GIZ, Enabel, UNDP, BOAD, IsDB, etc.). The country must also prepare for the mobilisation of resources from the Fund for responding to Loss and Damage.
  • Promotion of private and innovative finance; the following instruments should be used: (i) Public-Private Partnerships (PPPs) for climate projects with high return-on-investment potential, (ii) tax and customs incentives for green investments to attract investors, (iii) impact investments and green venture capital funds and (iv) green Islamic finance, (v) sovereign green bonds to finance sustainable infrastructure, (vi) climate micro-insurance for small producers and (vii) green crowdfunding and mobilisation of the diaspora.
  • Exploring the carbon market. In implementing NDC 3.0, Burkina Faso intends to: (i) use the cooperation mechanisms under Article 6 of the Paris Agreement to finance some conditional projects, (ii) use REDD+ mechanisms for forest and agricultural carbon projects, and (iii) use voluntary market mechanisms.

  • Adoption of the national climate finance mobilisation strategy and its action plan;
  • Regular sessions of the consultation framework of national focal structures on climate finance;
  • Setting up and operationalising the technical committee for reviewing projects to be submitted to climate funds;

Conclusion

This NDC reflects Burkina Faso’s commitment to complying with the provisions of the Paris Agreement. Prepared in a participatory and inclusive manner, NDC 3.0 reaffirms the country’s determination to raise its GHG emission reduction ambitions.

For the period 2026-2030, Burkina Faso commits, through its mitigation actions, to reduce its GHG emissions by 30.87% by 2030. This ambition is divided into 22.37% for unconditional actions and 8.50% for conditional actions.

The ambition of the country’s adaptation actions is characterised by a GHG emission reduction potential estimated at 41.94% relative to the reference scenario (BAU) by 2030. This reduction contribution is divided into an unconditional scenario of 39.67% and a conditional scenario of 2.27%.

The overall GHG emission reduction ambition is 55.23% in 2035 and 75.55% in 2050 for mitigation, and 70.31% in 2035 and 84.40% in 2050 for adaptation. This ambition is in line with the Action Plan for Stabilisation and Development (PA-SD), the Vision 2050 for low-carbon, climate-resilient development of Burkina Faso (LT-LEDS) and the Sustainable Development Goals (SDGs).

The mitigation and adaptation actions retained generate positive socio-economic spin-offs for health, employment, food security and the resilience of rural and urban communities…

The financing for achieving the ambitions of NDC 3.0 is estimated at USD 7 758 044 430.7, of which USD 4 956 913 742.1 for unconditional actions. Conditional financing is USD 2 801 130 688.7, i.e. 36.1% of total needs. Gender-specific actions amount to USD 146 085 885, of which USD 61 058 167 remains to be sought. Financing conditional projects remains essential to achieve the GHG emission reduction ambition and strengthen socio-economic co-benefits. The risks to the implementation of NDC 3.0 have been assessed and are accompanied by a matrix of mitigation measures, to be regularly reassessed and updated in order to identify management actions to be integrated.

NDC 3.0, beyond being a commitment document, is an essential lever for anchoring sustainable and inclusive development in Burkina Faso. Its implementation, under the coordination of the ministry in charge of the environment, requires the mobilisation of all actors.
