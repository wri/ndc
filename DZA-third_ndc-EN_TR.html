EXECUTIVE SUMMARY

Algeria, a developing country particularly vulnerable to the impacts of climate change, reaffirms its commitment to implement the Paris Agreement through a resilient, low-carbon development pathway carbon-neutral and socially inclusive. This ambition is rooted in respect for the principles of fairness and justice. climate and common but differentiated responsibilities.

As part of its Nationally Determined Contribution (NDC), Algeria has set itself the objective of reduce its greenhouse gas emissions unconditionally by 14% by 2035 compared to reference scenario. These efforts, funded by national resources, reflect a strong political will despite a challenging economic and social context. Subject to adequate international support in terms of through financing, technology transfer and capacity building, these objectives can be achieved to a 31% reduction by 2035. The financing needs for implementing this trajectory are estimated at $24 billion over the period 2026–2035. Furthermore, the development of energy renewables will significantly increase their share in the national energy mix by 2035.

The national context is marked by a strong dependence on hydrocarbons and sustained population growth. and rapid urbanization, placing increased pressure on energy demand. Faced with these challenges, Algeria leads a gradual energy transition aimed at structurally reducing emissions and strengthening the climate resilience of its population, while preserving its economic sovereignty and taking into account the imperatives of its socio-economic development. Since 2015, major progress has been made, notably with the launch of the National Climate Plan, the adoption of a renewable energy development program of 15,000 MW by 2035, the promotion of energy efficiency, the strengthening of the institutional framework and climate regulations, as well as the commitment to the process of developing the National Adaptation Plan.

The mitigation strategy is based on several key pillars, including the gradual decarbonization of the electricity mix, the introduction of electric mobility and the electrification of public transport, the reduction of fugitive methane emissions in the hydrocarbon sector, improving energy efficiency in the residential, commercial and industrial sectors, as well as the modernization of industrial processes.

Adaptation is a national priority in the face of droughts, water stress, desertification, and forest fires, health risks, and extreme weather events. The planned actions focus on sustainable water resource management, agricultural resilience and food security, protection of ecosystems, public health, and climate risk prevention and management. The needs Financial resources related to adaptation are estimated at approximately $1.1 billion per year, highlighting the need for a strengthened international support, particularly through climate finance mechanisms. Algeria also faced with losses and damages already perceptible, and calls for the rapid operationalization of the international fund dedicated.

In terms of transparency, Algeria submitted its first Biennial Transparency Report in 2024 and continues strengthening its national monitoring, reporting and verification system to ensure credibility and transparency of its climate actions. Climate governance relies on coordination an inter-institutional process led by the National Climate Committee, with the support of the relevant institutions. Finally, Algeria reaffirms its commitment to international cooperation.

1. Combating climate change: a strategic priority for Algeria

The fight against climate change represents a crucial challenge for Algeria, a country particularly vulnerable to desertification, water stress, and rising temperatures. These impacts threaten the Food security, water resources and ecosystems, while also impacting development socio-economic. Faced with these challenges, Algeria is actively engaged in the transition towards development resilient and low carbon emission.

1.1 Algeria's vision on international cooperation

Algeria considers international cooperation a fundamental pillar of the global response to to climate change. It fully supports the principles of the United Nations Framework Convention on climate change and the Paris Agreement, in particular common but differentiated responsibilities and their respective capacities, and argues for increased support to developing countries in the area of financing, technology transfer and capacity building. Algeria emphasizes that its This contribution is ambitious given its development constraints. It highlights its effort voluntary, while calling for strengthened international solidarity based on an understanding collective action and concerted action, far removed from unilateral measures that could undermine the foundations of international climatic regime.

2. Progress in Algeria's climate framework since 2015

Since 2015, Algeria has made several notable advances: adoption of its NDC in 2015, implementation of a National climate plan, launch of a National Renewable Energy Development Program, the adoption of the National Green Hydrogen Strategy, as well as active participation in negotiations climate. Algeria has also strengthened its institutional and technical frameworks for monitoring, the reporting and verification.

Algeria submitted its BTR1 in 2024, its BUR1, its third national communication, and two reports. national GHG inventory.

Since adopting its Intended Nationally Determined Contribution (INDC) in 2015, Algeria has has undertaken several reforms to prepare for the transition to a more sustainable energy system notably :

  • Rationalization of internal natural gas consumption, by improving energy efficiency and by diversifying electricity production sources;

  • Strengthening energy security while integrating environmental considerations. 2.3 Demand Management and Energy Efficiency

A National Energy Efficiency Action Plan (NEEAP) has been developed, primarily targeting:

  • The residential sector, with the promotion of LED lighting and the standardization of appliances household appliances (air conditioners, refrigerators) and the widespread use of thermal insulation;

  • Public buildings, through energy audits and pilot renovation measures;

  • The industry, through incentives for equipment modernization and heat recovery.

2.4 Transport and Sustainable Mobility

The transport sector is responsible for a significant share of emissions. Algeria has implemented several structuring actions:

  • Development of low-emission public transport including the extension of the Algiers metro and the construction of tram lines in 7 major cities and the modernization and electrification of rail transport;

  • Promotion of LPG fuel (Sirghaz) for the vehicle fleet, with significant subsidies from fuel, conversion equipment and the opening of distribution points throughout the territory ;

  • Gradual introduction of electric mobility and development of charging infrastructure.

These measures aim to reduce fossil fuel consumption in a context of high urban motorization.

2.5 Adaptation to climate change

Algeria, due to its arid climate, is particularly vulnerable to the effects of climate change, the effects of which it is already experiencing through drought and water stress and desertification.

Adaptation actions undertaken since 2015 include:

  • Combating desertification through the revival of the green dam program and operations of reforestation in the north and south;

  • Water resource management: strengthening water resource storage capacities superficial through the construction of new dams, development of the mobilization of unconventional water resources, both to secure the water supply of large cities and coastal towns with seawater desalination and to strengthen irrigation of agricultural land with the reuse of treated wastewater, preservation of water resources and promotion of water conservation and its rational use.

  • Coastal protection against erosion and sea-level rise, with pilot projects in vulnerable areas such as Algiers, Béjaïa and Skikda.

  • Support for the resilient agriculture , particularly in the High Plateaus, with drought-resistant varieties and techniques of soil conservation.

Furthermore, Algeria has launched the process for developing the National Adaptation Plan.

4. Methodology for calculating emissions and absorptions greenhouse gas emissions in Algeria

The methodology adopted by Algeria for calculating greenhouse gas emissions and absorptions (GES) complies with the requirements of the United Nations Framework Convention on Climate Change (UNFCCC). It is aligned with the methodological approaches presented in the official documents submitted to the UNFCCC, in particular the Biennial Update Report (BUR) and the First Biennial Transparency Report (BTR1).

The estimates are made using LEAP (Low Emissions Analysis Platform) software, a tool for internationally recognized modeling and recommended by the UNFCCC for scenario development of GHG emissions. LEAP allows for a detailed assessment of scenarios and their impacts on emissions of GHGs, according to the IPCC guidelines (2006 and 2019 updates), thus ensuring transparency, the consistency, and completeness of the data.

The use of LEAP and IPCC guidelines allows for the production of robust estimates and reliable, in accordance with international standards, within the framework of the enhanced transparency process established by the Paris Agreement, with the constructive involvement of national stakeholders, including the civil society, throughout the entire process.

5. Development of GHG emission scenarios and overall objectives and results of policies, measures and Algeria's mitigation actions

In the context of the prospective assessment of greenhouse gas (GHG) emissions, three scenarios are developed in accordance with the methodological guidelines of the United Nations Framework Convention on Climate Change (UNFCCC). These scenarios allow us to examine the possible evolution of emissions in depending on the country's political, technological and economic choices, and to measure the impact of contributions nationally determined (NDCs) within the framework of the Paris Agreement.

5.1 Scenario Descriptions

Three scenarios have been developed:

  1. Baseline scenario (BaU – Business as Usual): The baseline scenario describes the projected evolution greenhouse gas emissions in the absence of new policies or additional mitigation measures beyond those implemented in the reference year. It serves as a trend trajectory and basis for comparison for evaluating mitigation efforts.

  2. Unconditional scenario: This scenario incorporates all the policies, measures, and actions that the the country commits to implementing by mobilizing exclusively its national resources (financial, technological and human). It corresponds to the unconditional component of the NDC.

  3. Conditional scenario: This scenario includes additional mitigation measures that may to be implemented subject to adequate international support, particularly in the area of financing, technology transfer and capacity building. It reflects the component conditional of the CDN.

7. Adaptation

Algeria is one of the countries most exposed to the effects of climate change, due to the scarcity of its water resources, the fragility of its ecosystems, and its location in arid zones and semi-arid. The main climate risks identified concern drought, floods, the water stress, desertification, forest fires and coastal risks linked to rising sea levels the sea.

To address these challenges, Algeria adopted its National Climate Plan (NCP) in 2019, which constitutes the framework a reference point for national action on mitigation and adaptation. At the same time, it has strengthened its international transparency through the Third National Communication (2023) and the first Report Transparency Biennial (2024), which fall under the reporting obligations to the UNFCCC and provide a diagnosis of vulnerabilities, emissions and measures already undertaken through the integration of provisions relating to environmental protection, sustainable water and land management, and the prevention of major risks and protection of ecosystems in several legislative texts and regulatory framework. This legal framework constitutes an essential basis for the operationalization of adaptation and its integration into sectoral policies.

In line with national efforts and consistent with the commitments made under the Agreement of Paris, Algeria defines, through its Nationally Determined Contribution, its vision and priorities regarding adaptation by 2035. This approach aims to:

  • Ensure consistency with the National Adaptation Plan (NAP) currently being developed, which will constitute the operational instrument for implementing the defined priorities;

  • To give international visibility to the adaptation efforts already underway and to the guidelines futures;

  • Strengthening transparency and compliance with the provisions of the Paris Agreement, in particular Article 7 relating to adaptation;

  • Mobilize increased support in terms of financing, technology and capacity building for the implementation of priority actions;

  • Contributing to the achievement of the Global Adaptation Goal (GAG) and the realization of Sustainable Development Goals (SDGs).

The link with the National Action Plan (NAP), whose adoption is planned for 2026, will make it possible to translate this vision into concrete programs and projects, accompanied by monitoring mechanisms, indicators and systems Appropriate financing.

7.1 Medium-term vision for adaptation

By 2035, Algeria aspires to climate-resilient development, guaranteeing the sustainability of natural resources, protection of populations, preservation of ecosystems and the integration of adaptation measures into sectoral policies.

This vision is based on the following five pillars:

  • the systematic integration of adaptation into development strategies (water, agriculture, health, energy, infrastructure, forests and biodiversity);

  • An inclusive and equitable approach that takes into account the most vulnerable populations;

  • strengthening national and local climate risk management systems, including prevention, early warning, preparedness and disaster response;

  • the implementation of mechanisms to reduce losses and damages related to climate events extreme and slow-evolving phenomena, in order to limit the economic, social and environmental;

  • the contribution to strengthening collective resilience to climate impacts, in line with with the Global Adaptation Goal (GAG) and the Sustainable Development Goals (SDGs) of Agenda 2030.

Inappropriate agricultural practices such as plowing in the steppe and the low level of reforestation exacerbates the advance of desertification and the decline in productivity.

The risk of waterborne and vector-borne diseases that develop as a result of water stress, Climate change, pollution and heat expose rural and peri-urban populations. Inadequate sanitation, both personal and general hygiene, promotes the emergence of epidemics of waterborne diseases, malaria and leishmaniasis.

The risk of salinization and marine intrusion, linked to the overexploitation of aquifers and the rise of the The sea affects coastal areas and irrigated perimeters, which are the most exposed. This situation leads to land loss, decreased agricultural yields, and threats to health. (water and agricultural product quality).

The risk of air pollution is primarily caused by human activities, including industrial and transport-related incidents, as well as forest fires, can also contribute to the spread of the virus. These conditions contribute to the increased frequency of respiratory diseases, such as asthma and bronchopneumopathy chronic obstructive pulmonary disease (COPD).

The risk of soil erosion and landslides is linked to climatic hazards of increased the frequency of intense rainfall events. Agricultural areas and non-forested land, those located in the northern mountains are particularly exposed. Unstable soils and degradation of forest ecosystems, as well as deforestation and the lack of infrastructure protection, constitute vulnerabilities that exacerbate these hazards and lead to road closures, damage to dams and irreversible soil loss.

The risk of flooding and sea level rise resulting from heavy rainfall events affect Infrastructure such as ports (Algiers, Oran, Skikda, Annaba) and coastal areas. Vulnerabilities linked to a lack of adequate protection expose these infrastructures to deterioration, to salt intrusion and the degradation of tourist heritage.

7.3 Losses and Damages

Algeria, due to its location in arid and semi-arid zones and the fragility of its resources natural areas are particularly exposed to losses and damages caused by the impacts of climate change. These impacts manifest themselves through economic, social and growing environmental challenges that directly affect the country's sustainable development.

Over the past two decades, Algeria has experienced an intensification of events extreme weather events: recurring droughts, flash floods, heat waves and forest fires. The Algiers floods of 2001 resulted in over 700 deaths and economic losses estimated at over 2.7 billion DZD. More generally, and according to a preparatory study for the development of the PNC, carried out on behalf of the Ministry of the Environment, the costs of inaction, that is to say the the costs of environmental damage caused annually by human activities, would represent In Algeria, the equivalent of 6.9% of GDP in 2017, or USD 11.7 billion. The damage caused by the Climate change and energy sector inefficiencies reached 2.9% of GDP, or 4.9 Billions of USD per year.

The impacts on productive sectors are also significant. Agriculture is suffering a loss of Estimated yield between 20 and 30% depending on the crop due to drought and degradation land, which affects more than 12 million hectares. Coastal erosion, meanwhile, leads to direct costs (particularly in terms of loss of land, buildings or tourism activity), estimated at USD 313 million per year, or 0.2% of GDP. These losses also translate into effects social consequences: loss of human lives, displacement of rural populations, increased vulnerability of low-income households and weakening of livelihoods.

Aware of these challenges, the Algerian state has established a national framework for prevention and management major risks, reinforced by financial mechanisms such as the Natural Disaster Fund and major technological risks, as well as through risk reduction programs (dam green spaces, combating desertification, sectoral adaptation strategies). However, the scale of Losses and damages exceed national prevention and compensation capacities. In this In this context, Algeria emphasizes the importance of increased international support, in accordance with provisions of the Paris Agreement and recent COP decisions for:

  • Strengthening institutional and technical capacities for monitoring and evaluating losses and damages ;

  • Develop innovative financial mechanisms, including insurance and compensation;

  • Access to appropriate technologies and expertise for risk reduction and management climatic.

    Algeria thus reaffirms its determination to act for national resilience, while calling for the international solidarity to better cope with the losses and damages related to the changes climatic.

National policies, including the National Climate Plan, recognize adaptation as a strategic priority.

Algeria's adaptation priorities for 2035 are as follows:

  2. Strengthening agricultural resilience and ensuring food security;

  3. Protection of ecosystems and combating desertification;

  4. Resilient public health and social protection systems;

  5. Prevention and management of climate risks, including fires, floods and droughts.

There are needs in terms of strengthening technical capacities and access to data. Reliable climate solutions, appropriate technologies, and climate finance. These needs will be quantified and detailed within the framework of the PNA.

8. Towards a low-carbon and resilient trajectory

Algeria's NDC presents its vision for controlling the growth of its greenhouse gas emissions to horizons 2035, by deploying mitigation and adaptation measures in all sectors of the economy.

In terms of mitigation, Algeria is undertaking a national effort that would reduce emissions by 14% in 2035 compared to the baseline scenario (BaU), and this without any requirement for external aid. The main axes policies, measures and actions primarily concern:

  • Electricity production, with the launch of an ambitious 3.2 GW solar program by 2030, marks a major step in the diversification of the electricity mix. This program, already in its implementation phase, This work will reduce dependence on natural gas for electricity generation, and therefore limit the CO₂ emissions in the energy sector in the medium and long term.

  • Transport, with the promotion of LPG/c (Liquefied Petroleum Gas fuel) as an alternative to diesel and to gasoline. This policy and its implementation help reduce CO₂ and pollutant emissions. local resources, while developing national light hydrocarbon resources. Expanding the vehicle fleet Converting to LPG/c represents a rapid and effective lever for decarbonizing the sector. At the same time, Energy efficiency efforts also concern air, rail and maritime transport, through the partial electrification of the railway, as well as the optimization of energy consumption in navigation and civil aviation. However, the potential gains in these sub-sectors remain relatively limited in comparison with road transport, which concentrates the bulk of emissions and is therefore the priority major for mitigation.

  • The promotion of energy efficiency and clean technologies, particularly in buildings and energy-intensive industries (cement, steel, etc.).

  • Reducing fugitive emissions from the hydrocarbon sector.

Algeria is thus demonstrating its ability to reduce a significant portion of its GHG emissions compared to trend scenario, by mobilizing only its internal resources However, these efforts, while substantial, remain below the overall reduction potential. Support Increased international investment in financing, technology, and capacity building would amplify significantly the country's contribution to the fight against climate change.

In terms adaptation Algeria also places resilience at the heart of its climate strategy. Aware of its vulnerability to impacts of climate change – recurring droughts, water stress, desertification, decline in the Agricultural productivity and risks to health and infrastructure – the country is implementing programs aimed at:

  • Strengthening integrated and sustainable water resource management, particularly through the mobilization of surface water resources, seawater desalination, reuse of treated wastewater, the preservation of water resources and improvement of the rational use of water by users.

  • Protecting and restoring fragile ecosystems, particularly arid and semi-arid areas, through actions to combat desertification and land degradation using hardy species and adapted to drought.

  • Developing climate-resilient agriculture through the introduction of adapted varieties and optimization of the use of inputs and the diversification of production.

  • Strengthening the resilience of populations and infrastructure to extreme climate risks, in particular through the implementation of early warning systems and the integration of climate into planning territory.

Thus, Algeria's NDC illustrates a balanced approach combining mitigation and adaptation, reflecting a willingness to act simultaneously to reduce emissions and protect populations, ecosystems and key economic sectors are being protected from the impacts of climate change. Through this proactive contribution, Algeria affirms its commitment to low-carbon, equitable and sustainable development, while defending climate justice in line with the principle of common responsibilities but differentiated (CBDR).

The national contribution also aims to support the social development by reconciling emissions reduction with job creation, access to energy, and security water and improved living conditions.

c) The manner in which Algeria has taken into account paragraph 3 of Article 4 of the Paris Agreement.

Algeria has taken into account the paragraph 3 of Article 4 of the Paris Agreement , which recognizes that national contributions must be differentiated according to historical capacities and responsibilities Its NDC reflects this approach by combining measures unconditional financed from national resources, and measures conditionals dependent on international support. This differentiation allows Algeria to contribute to overall mitigation while taking into account its economic capacities, its sustainable development needs and its weak historical responsibility in global GHG emissions.

d) The manner in which Algeria has taken into account paragraph 4 of Article 4 of the Paris Agreement.

Algeria has taken into account the paragraph 4 of Article 4 of the Paris Agreement , which encourages the regular review of national contributions. The NDC presents a higher level of ambition compared to the NDC, by strengthening mitigation and adaptation measures, taking into account the technological, economic and climatic developments.

e) The manner in which Algeria has taken into account paragraph 6 of Article 4 of the Paris Agreement.

Algeria has taken into account the paragraph 6 of Article 4 of the Paris Agreement , which encourages the development of long-term plans to achieve carbon neutrality. Its NDC covers the period up to 2035 while preparing long-term strategies 2050 , integrating the energy transition, low-carbon economic diversification and the strengthening of the Resilience to climate impacts. This approach allows for the coherent planning of medium- and long-term mitigation and adaptation measures, while taking into account national priorities and capacities.
