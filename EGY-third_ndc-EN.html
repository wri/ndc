Egypt’s Third Nationally Determined Contribution (2030 – 2035)

1. INTRODUCTION

Egypt ratified the United Nations Framework Convention on Climate Change (UNFCCC) in 1994 to be among the first countries to respond to the threats of climate change in line with the equity principle of common but differentiated responsibilities, according to its national capabilities. Egypt submitted its Intended Nationally Determined Contribution (INDC) in 2015, which became its first NDC following the signing of the Paris Agreement on the 22nd of April 2016, and its ratification on 29th June 2017. Building on this foundation, Egypt submitted its first and second updated NDC on 8th June 2022 and 24th June 2023, respectively (NDC 2.0). The latter updates reflected a strengthened national commitment to climate action through both mitigation and adaptation, aligned with Egypt’s sustainable development goals.

This third NDC (NDC 3.0) represents Egypt’s increased ambition for the period between 2030 till 2035. It incorporates key insights from Egypt’s first Biennial Transparency Report (BTR) submitted on 30th December 2024. Furthermore, the First Global Stocktake (Decision 1/CMA.5 – UAE Consensus, COP28 Dubai) assessed collective progress under the Paris Agreement. It confirmed that global efforts remain off-track to the 1.5 °C goal, calling for accelerated mitigation and adaptation action and calling for providing the needed finance before 2030. For Egypt, the GST serves as a strategic guide to update its NDC 3.0 and align with its National Climate Change Strategy 2050 and its other national sectoral strategies (refer to Section 4 and Section 5).

Egypt faces significant challenges in accessing finance for implementing its national mitigation and adaptation plans. There is continuous need for enhanced support, including through financial resources, technology transfer and capacity-building. The financial resources required to implement Egypt’s NDC 3.0 till 2035 are estimated at minimum USD 324 billion. The mitigation interventions require USD 211 billion and the adaptation interventions require USD 113 billion (refer to Section 6.4).

2. OVERVIEW OF CHANGES FROM NDC 2.0

In assessing the achievement of measures under the NDC 2.0, Egypt’s First Biennial Transparency Report (BTR1), submitted in December 2024, illustrates that the country could remain on track to meet its targets for 2030 under NDC 2.0, conditional upon the availability of adequate financial, technical, and capacity-building support.

The following results have been achieved:

  • Remarkable success in transitioning to low carbon pathway in the electricity sector (generation, transmission, and distribution) with a substantial GHG emission reduction of 47,810 GgCO2e in 2022, with a decrease of 34.34% in emissions compared to BAU levels for that same year. Egypt’s electricity sector is advancing steadily towards the 2030 target of 37% GHG emission reduction.

  • Efforts in the oil and gas sector have resulted in a total GHG emission reduction of 1,351 Gg CO2e in 2022, with a decrease of 57% in emissions compared to BAU levels for that same year. The associated petroleum gases subsector of Egypt's Oil & Gas industry is on track to achieve the 2030 target of a 65% GHG emission reduction, with significant progress already made.

  • Egypt has achieved outstanding success in adopting more sustainable low-carbon modes of transport, with a substantial GHG Emission reduction of 14,750 GgCO2e in 2022 and a decrease of 18.84% of emissions compared to BAU levels for that same year. The transport sector is on track towards the 2030 target of 7% GHG emission reduction.

NDC 3.0 demonstrates Egypt’s progression beyond its previous NDC, in line with Article 4, paragraph 3, of the Paris Agreement. It reflects a balanced, integrated approach to mitigation and adaptation, consistent with Egypt’s national development agenda and long-term climate vision. These enhanced commitments are reflected in several areas compared with NDC 2.0: i) the adoption of more ambitious quantitative targets in three sectors, ii) broadening the scope of mitigation policies and measures to include the water resources and irrigation sector, in support to the quantitative targets, iii) an increase in the number of policies and measures from 33 to 37 in mitigation and from 26 to 29 in adaptation (refer to summary table below), and iv) a new Section 7 has also been added to highlight Egypt’s advancement toward an inclusive and equitable low- carbon, climate-resilient development pathway. Egypt has made these advances despite global geopolitical challenges, economic constraints, and limited access to climate finance.

3. NATIONAL CIRCUMSTANCES

3.1 Geography and Climate

Egypt, spanning one million square kilometers, is bordered by the Mediterranean Sea to the north, Sudan to the south, the Red Sea to the east, and Libya to the west. Egypt’s rich biodiversity encompasses deserts, wetlands, and coastal ecosystems, supporting 22 habitat groups and over 2,300 species. However, this biodiversity faces significant threats, including habitat destruction, excessive hunting, pollution, and climate change. Despite these challenges, biodiversity remains vital to Egypt’s economy, particularly through ecotourism and natural resource-dependent industries.

Egypt has a hot, arid desert climate, characterized by two main seasons: a mild winter from November to April and a hot summer from May to October. Seasonal temperatures vary with prevailing winds. Along the coast, average winter lows reach 14°C, while summer highs peak at 30°C. In the inland deserts, temperatures fluctuate between 7°C at night and 43°C during the day. Egypt is also subject to khamsin winds —hot storms that can cause temperature spikes of up to 20°C within hours. The country receives minimal annual rainfall, mostly concentrated along the Mediterranean coast. Water resources are limited due to high evaporation rates and the absence of permanent surface water.

3.2 Population and Demographics

Egypt is a lower-middle-income country with a rapidly growing population estimated at 107.9 million as of July 2025. 1 Egypt is currently hosting 925,000 refugees and asylum-seekers registered with UNHCR as of February 2025.2 The male-to-female ratio is 105.8 males for every 100 females. By 2050, Egypt is projected to be among the countries contributing most significantly to global population growth. More than 95% of the population resides along the Nile River and its delta, while the remainder area comprises of desert environments such as the Western and Eastern Deserts and the Sinai Peninsula. This rapid population growth poses challenges for food security, health, education, employment, and overall quality of life. Addressing these pressures requires adaptive policies that promote sustainable resource management and economic resilience.

3.3 Economic Profile

Since 2016, Egypt has implemented substantial macroeconomic and structural reforms, yet significant challenges persist. The COVID-19 pandemic, regional and global economic disruptions, and geopolitical tensions have hampered recent progress. Between 2015 and 2025, transformative changes—such as the Egyptian Pound's devaluation in November 2016— triggered short-term economic shocks and inflation surges. To stabilize the economy, Egypt sought additional IMF assistance, including a Rapid Financing Instrument in 2020 and a $3 billion Extended Fund Facility (EFF) loan in 2022. However, successive currency devaluations in 2022 and 2023 renewed inflationary pressures and expanded public debt. Egypt's economy is in a recovery phase, achieving a 3-year high GDP growth rate of 4.4% in FY2024/2025. The turnaround is attributed to structural reforms, a shift towards private investment, and improved foreign exchange conditions following the exchange rate unification in early 2024.

The IMF’s outlook for Egypt in 2025 is positive, supported by stronger private sector activity and improved macroeconomic stability. While challenges remain—such as managing external risks and reducing the state’s economic footprint—the IMF has commended Egypt’s reform progress and emphasized the importance of continuing structural reforms to unlock growth potential and enhance private sector participation. The Fund has also approved the Extended Fund Facility (EFF) and the Resilience and Sustainability Facility (RSF), reflecting confidence in Egypt’s reform trajectory.

As in many developing countries, there remains a need for development and climate financing to help bridge financing gaps and support fiscal sustainability.

3.4 Water Resources and Scarcity Pressures

Water scarcity poses a severe and growing challenge for Egypt, as total water demand has exceeded the Nile River’s supply since the late 1970s. Rapid population growth and economic development have exacerbated this stress, with the Nile providing 98% of Egypt’s renewable water resources. The Nile River is the main source of fresh water for Egypt supplying 55.5 billion cubic meters (BCM) per year as per the share agreed by international treaties. Additional marginal amounts are provided from deep non-renewable groundwater aquifers (2.5 BCM), limited rainfall (1.3 BCM), and desalination (0.4 BCM) to increase the total yearly available water resources to 59.25 BCM.3

Egypt ranks 10th globally in water stress4, with annual per capita renewable water supply steadily declining. Climate change is expected to intensify these challenges further. Agriculture dominates water use consuming ~76% (~62 BCM). 5 Recent global events, such as the coronavirus pandemic, have heightened food insecurity, underscoring the sector's fragility.6 Agriculture as a cornerstone of food security and the national economy is particularly vulnerable. Recent global events, such as the coronavirus pandemic, have heightened food insecurity, underscoring the sector's fragility.

3.5 Climate Vulnerability and Impacts

Egypt is among the most climate vulnerable countries, especially the Nile Delta region. Under the high-emission RCP8.5 scenario, IPCC AR5 projects a global sea-level rise of about 0.63 m by 2100 (likely range: 0.45–0.82 m). Using updated AR6 data, regional projections for the Mediterranean—including the Nile Delta—indicate sea-level rise could match or exceed 1.1 m by 2100 under SSP5-8.5 conditions. In combination with land subsidence, this could expose 1,500– 2,600 km² of Nile Delta land to flooding, threatening millions of residents (Church et al., 2013; Oppenheimer et al., 2022).7 Additional pressures from extreme weather affect coastal cities and Upper Egypt. The ND-GAIN index, Egypt ranks at 105th at the overall ranks, 100th in vulnerability and 130th in readiness, highlighting low adaptive preparedness despite significant climate exposure.8 Accessible and low-cost climate finance is essential to support measures towards climate change. Climate action and development must go hand in hand to ensure a resilient future.

3.6 Low Carbon and Resilient Development

Egypt is implementing a range of mitigation strategies to combat climate change, increase resilience, and ensure long-term sustainability. Egypt’s Vision 2030, National Climate Change Strategy 2050, and the Decent Life program prioritize investments in water efficiency, agricultural adaptation, manufacturing decarbonization, renewable energy, and urban resilience.

In FY 2024/2025, green sectors accounted for 40% of Egypt’s public investments, with a national goal of reaching 70% by 2030. This steady growth —highlighted in the Ministry of Planning’s Green Investment Plan — demonstrates Egypt’s commitment to transforming climate challenges into drivers of economic opportunities. These efforts to expand the green economy and localize climate action are further supported by Egypt's National Narrative for Economic Development, launched in September 2025.

The government is gradually phasing out subsidies and increasing electricity tariffs to promote energy efficiency and sustainability. Significant investments are being made in renewable energy projects, green hydrogen and ammonia, alongside efforts to reduce fuel consumption through energy sector reforms. Egypt is also expanding its public transportation networks and modernizing railway systems to lower emissions and improve urban mobility. Additionally, the country is prioritizing energy efficiency enhancements and transitioning towards renewable fuels to reduce greenhouse gas emissions. Industrialization and urbanization strategies emphasize sustainable development, improved green local component, public transport systems, and the establishment of smart cities. Moreover, the government has implemented a Waste Management Regulation Law to further reduce emissions and manage waste effectively.

Ongoing structural reforms, supported by development financing and expanded green investments, are increasingly being implemented to scale up climate action while maintaining fiscal stability and advancing Egypt’s broader development goals.

4. MITIGATION PRIORITIES AND ACTIONS TILL 2035

Egypt is contributing to the global efforts highlighted in the First Global Stocktake through a comprehensive set of mitigation measures, including:

  • Phasing out inefficient fossil-fuel subsidies that do not address energy poverty or support a just transition in electricity and petroleum products.

  • Tripling global renewable-energy capacity by expanding investments in solar and wind power generation.

  • Accelerating progress toward net-zero emission energy systems by increasing the use of zero- and low-carbon fuels, including nuclear energy.

  • Transitioning away from fossil fuels in a just, orderly, and equitable manner, while refraining from investments in unabated coal power.

  • Doubling the global average annual rate of energy-efficiency improvement across sectors through the promotion and implementation of energy efficiency practices and technologies.

  • Advancing abatement and removal technologies, such as carbon capture, utilization, and storage—particularly in hard-to-abate sectors like cement, steel, and fertilizers—and supporting low-carbon hydrogen production (both blue and green hydrogen).

  • Substantially reducing non-CO₂ emissions, especially methane, by recovering and utilizing associated petroleum gas generated from crude-oil fields.

  • Cutting road-transport emissions through infrastructure development, mass-transit expansion (metro, monorail, trains, buses) and shifting vehicles toward lower-carbon fuels such as natural gas and electricity.

Policies and Measures

Since road transport is by far the largest GHGs contributor in the transport sector in Egypt, it is planned to drive low carbon modal shift from private passenger and freight vehicles into mass transit and improve vehicle fuel efficiency, primarily through:

  • Continue the gradual removal of petroleum product subsidies as part of the national economic reform program launched in 2016. This policy indirectly influences consumer behavior by encouraging reduced fuel consumption, greater reliance on mass transit, and the phasing out of old, inefficient vehicles, among other fuel-saving measures.

  • Expansion of electric traction infrastructure and facilitation of modal shift from private vehicles and road freight to mass electric rail systems.

  • Implementing comprehensive fleet renewal and replacement programs for public and mass transit to gradually transition towards low-carbon technologies. Measures include expanding the adoption of low carbon fuel and introducing electric buses and vehicles.

  • Implement the National Road Project that aims to develop new roads and upgrade of the current road infrastructure, construct several bridges and tunnels, construct paved roads within the governorates, and utilize modern asphalt recycling technologies. This would improve interconnections between cities and decrease commuting time and congestion and hence fuel consumption for road vehicles.

4.4 Manufacturing Industry

Supporting policies to achieve the quantitative targets by decarbonizing the industrial sector by reducing the energy intensity, use of renewable and alternative fuels, and low carbon process improvements, primarily through:

  • Egypt’s export-oriented sectors—such as cement, fertilizers, aluminum, and iron & steel—are actively advancing decarbonization strategies. These efforts include shifting to renewable and low-carbon energy sources, enhancing energy efficiency, optimizing production processes, planting green areas, and deploying carbon capture and utilization (CCU) technologies.

  • Reduce emissions across selected sectors including manufacturing industries by replacing conventional fuels with cleaner hydrogen alternatives and renewables. The strategy also positions Egypt as a future global hub for green and blue hydrogen. The objective is to introduce hydrogen as an industrial fuel after 2030 in the local market, particularly in sectors such as fertilizers and iron and steel production. It will also serve as a cleaner substitute for natural gas in refinery operations.

  • Implement measures in the low carbon roadmap for the Egyptian cement industry including alternative fuels partial substitution up to, lowering the clinker content in cement up to 80% conditional on meeting relevant national standards, and energy efficiency improvements. Ministerial Decree 49/2021 for mandatory partial replacement of alternative fuels in cement sector was issued in March 2021 by the Ministry of Environment as supporting policy measure.

  • Enhance electrical and thermal energy efficiency and encouraging the use of renewable energy source (solar energy) in other sectors, comprising SMEs (i.e. chemicals, food & beverage, textiles, metal industries). This includes heat system optimization complemented with the installation of solar thermal technologies, in addition to other low carbon technologies and measures. Furthermore, promote local manufacturing of renewable energy technologies for industrial application in process heating.

  • A stand-alone motor system optimization programme has been initiated to replace old inefficient motors with IE3 or higher motors in industrial plants to achieve savings in electrical energy consumption. It was complemented by the issuance of Ministerial Decree #463/2020 in October 2020 by Ministry of Trade and Industry to mandate producers and importers of electric motors to comply with the Egyptian specification for minimum energy performance standard (MEPS).

  • Launch New Automotive Industrial Development Program (AIDP), to promote low carbon emissions car production, foster electric vehicles, and to incentivize large production of eco-friendly smart vehicles in Egypt.

  • Promote eco-industrial parks concept to scale up resource efficiency through intra- firm exchanges, improvement of economic, environmental, and social performances of businesses, and creation of green industries (such as recycling and renewable energy) towards an inclusive and sustainable industrial development.10

4.5 Building and Urban Cities

Supporting policies to achieve the quantitative targets by promoting sustainability in existing and new buildings and urban development's towards adopting low carbon standards and programmes, primarily through:

  • Promoting the use of renewable energy and energy efficiency in existing and new establishments and rank sustainability interventions in accordance with national priorities. This includes installation of rooftop PV panels for electricity generation, solar water heaters, and expanding the use of LED lighting in residential sector.

  • Expanding on energy efficiency labels and specifications for appliances programme, elimination of non-energy efficient equipment, and raising awareness among consumers on purchasing alternative energy efficient home appliances.

  • Promote green buildings by activating the energy efficiency codes for new buildings, adopting procedures for the renovation of existing buildings to meet energy performance standards, adopting voluntary green building rating systems and building energy performance tools (BEP tool), and rollout incentives to encourage the use of best available technologies for sustainable buildings. Energy Efficiency Building Codes for new residential buildings and for commercial and government buildings were first developed by the Housing and Building Research Center (HBRC) and then enacted by ministerial decrees issued in 2005, 2009 and 2010, and is currently being updated to facilitate application and compliance. It is planned to expand residential units according to green building standards and increase awareness and community participation on sustainable buildings.

  • Increase green spaces and sustainable parks in new cities that are irrigated with treated wastewater to act as carbon sinks to improve quality of life for citizens and reduce negative health impacts. Furthermore, increase the per capita share of public green areas in existing cities, such as Ahl Masr Walkway and Ain Hayah project.

  • The Smart Cities Strategy, launched in 2025 with support from the World Bank and Switzerland (SECO) and led by MHUUC, enhances climate resilience through sustainable, tech-driven urban planning. It promotes adaptive, inclusive, and resource-efficient cities.

  • Adopt the National Active Mobility Strategy to encourage citizens to use bicycles and walking in designated paths and shift gradually to electric vehicles using clean energy sources and the establishment of the necessary infrastructure inside cities.

  • Installing energy efficient and/or solar-operated street lighting and advertisements in internal roads and in highways between cities.

4.6 Tourism

Supporting policies to achieve the quantitative targets by striving towards low carbon touristic developments and greening of hotels and resorts, primarily through:

  • Promote the adoption of renewable energy solutions in the hotel sector, such as photovoltaic (PV) systems and solar-powered water desalination, to reduce greenhouse gas (GHG) emissions, and target the installation of Solar Water Heaters (SWHs) in eco-certified hotels to advance energy efficiency and sustainable tourism practices.

  • Implement energy efficiency improvements through LED lighting replacements in eco-certified hotels, improved building envelope, employing efficient heating, ventilation, and air conditioning (HVAC) systems, efficient water pumping, and influencing the behavior of the hotel guests towards energy efficiency practices.

  • Promote the expansion of the national eco-certification program (Green Star Hotel - GSH) by supporting hotels to adopt sustainable practices.

4.7 Waste Management

Supporting policies to achieve the quantitative targets through integrated waste management to improve the efficiency of the system across the value chain and valorization of waste through materials and energy recovery to decrease amount of waste directed to landfilling, primarily through:

  • Upgrading the solid waste management infrastructure in all governorates to improve collection efficiency and increase recycling and energy recovery rates. This entails the establishment of fixed and mobile transfer stations, rehabilitation and new construction of mechanical and biological treatment (MBT) plants to utilize at least 60% of the collected waste, and closure of uncontrolled dumpsites to be replaced with sanitary landfills, with the aim of reducing the percentage of waste directed to sanitary landfills from the total waste collected.

  • Increase waste-to-energy contribution in solid waste through utilization of waste as alternative fuel in cement sector, waste to biofuels, and generate electric power through incineration, pyrolysis, and other modern technologies.

  • Expand the coverage of municipal and industrial wastewater tertiary treatment infrastructure and rehabilitate existing facilities, utilize treated wastewater and grey water, and recover sewage sludge for recycling and energy use. At present, the 612 municipal wastewater treatment plants attend to only 70% of the country’s population, which covers 96% of urban areas and 60% of rural areas.11 Recently Bahr El Baqar, one of the largest agricultural drainage and wastewater treatment plant of its kind worldwide, was inaugurated in Port Said with production capacity of 5.6 million cubic meters per day to recycle and reuse the drainage water that flows along the Bahr Al-Baqar drain to be used in reducing the gap for agricultural irrigation. It is planned to construct 310 water treatment facilities (design capacity of 4365 thousand m3 daily) and sewage plants of total design capacity of 8.2 million m3 daily under three phases.

  • As part of efforts to expand private sector engagement in municipal service delivery, expand tendering and contracting processes with private sector companies to provide waste collection, transportation, and street cleaning services.

4.8 Water Resources and Irrigation

Supporting policies to achieve the quantitative targets by advancing comprehensive set of climate mitigation actions that involves constructing energy-efficient infrastructure to modernizing existing systems and reduce emissions from coastal protection activities, primarily through:

  • Construct 8 new energy-efficient pumping stations under the Mechanical and Electrical Department of the Ministry of Water Resources and Irrigation, integrating advanced technologies to reduce GHG emissions and improve energy efficiency in water pumping operations.

  • Rehabilitate and upgrade 34 existing pumping stations , modernize outdated equipment, improve energy efficiency, and reduce power consumption and related emissions.

5. ADAPTATION PRIORITIES AND ACTIONS TILL 2035

Egypt emphasizes the importance of the Global Goal on Adaptation (GGA) enhancing adaptive capacity, strengthening resilience, and reducing vulnerability to climate change while aligning its response with sustainable development. In this context, Egypt’s adaptation actions are directly contributing to the realization of the GGA targets by building systemic resilience across priority sectors, including:

  • Reducing climate-induced water scarcity and strengthening resilience to water- related hazards to secure a climate-resilient water supply through water reuse, rainwater harvesting and aquifer recharge, lining and rehabilitation of irrigation canals, and construction of flood protection infrastructure.

  • Coastal zones protection from sea-level rise, erosion, and extreme weather events through structural and engineering coastal protection works in Northern Delta region.

  • Achieving climate-resilient food and agricultural production and supply chains by expanding climate-smart agriculture practices, heat-tolerant and drought-resistant crop varieties, strengthening pest and disease management systems across all agroecological zones.

  • Building resilience to climate-related health impacts and promoting climate- resilient health services by upgrading health facilities with climate-resilient features, training of healthcare workers, and implementing the One Health National Plan.

  • Safeguarding ecosystems and biodiversity by accelerating ecosystem-based adaptation and nature-based solutions by expanding protected lands.

  • Increasing the resilience of infrastructure and human settlements to ensure continuous essential services nationwide through green architecture and construction based on vulnerability assessments, monitoring system for climate risks based on weather forecasting, and implementing public alert systems.

Egypt adopts a comprehensive package of adaptation measures in the water resources and irrigation sector aimed at addressing the dual threats of water scarcity and increased climate variability due to climate change. These measures include both infrastructure upgrades and strategic planning for long-term sustainability and resilience, primarily through:

  • Lining and rehabilitation of irrigation canals to improve irrigation efficiency and reduce water losses from seepage and evaporation. The lining of 7,897 km of irrigation canals has been completed, with an additional 1,692 km currently under implementation. The total planned target remains 20,000 km of canals to be lined or rehabilitated by 2035, benefiting approximately 60 million people, particularly within agricultural communities.

  • Expand the reuse of agricultural drainage and treated wastewater to increase the availability of non-conventional water recourses. Currently, 6.6 million cubic meters (MCM)/day of water are reused daily through major projects such as Bahr El-Baqar (5.6 MCM/day) and Mahsama (1 MCM/day). The target is to reach a total of 14.1 MCM/day by 2035, including the ongoing Hammam and west delta (El Dabaa) mega treatment facilities and new projects in Delta region.

  • Construct protection infrastructure to protect lives and establishments from extreme rainfall and flash floods, with a particular focus on the most vulnerable areas. A total of 413 flood protection structures has been constructed, of which 326 have been completed and 87 are currently under construction from South Cairo to High Dam. Furthermore, a total of 628 protection structures have been constructed and 47 protection structures under construction in North Sinai, South Sinai, and Red Sea. The plan aims to reach 237 protection structures from South Cairo to High Dam and 162 protection structures in North Sinai, South Sinai, and Red Sea by 2035.

  • Adopt rainwater harvesting and aquifer recharge in response to changing rainfall patterns. Egypt is enhancing its capacity for rainwater harvesting and groundwater recharge. Current systems provide 450 million cubic meters (Mm³)/year in Upper Egypt (from South Cairo to the High Dam) and 178 Mm³/year in Sinai and Red Sea regions. The 2035 target is to reach 250 Mm³/year in the south and 320 Mm³/year in the northern regions, contributing to climate-resilient water management.

  • Cooperation with Nile Basin Countries Egypt continues to promote regional water security through integrated cooperation with Nile Basin countries.

5.2 Agriculture

Egypt will implement a comprehensive package of adaptation measures in the agriculture sector aimed at enhancing resilience to climate risks such as heatwaves, wind storms, erratic rainfall, and emerging pest and disease threats, primarily through:

  • Improve climate-smart villages and demonstration farms, scaling up climate-smart agriculture practices across all agroecological zones to promote adaptive and resilient agricultural techniques. These initiatives aim to accelerate the adoption of improved crop varieties, efficient irrigation methods, and sustainable land use particularly in areas vulnerable to drought and increasing temperatures.

  • Strengthening of heat-tolerant and drought-resistant crop varieties to enhance agricultural resilience and safeguard food production. These improved varieties aim to minimize yield losses due to heat stress, pests & diseases, and water scarcity across key strategic crops.

  • Enhancement water management through expanding the deployment of efficient solar irrigation systems to address growing water stress challenges. These interventions aim to reduce reliance on fossil fuels, improve water-use efficiency, and strengthen resilience in drought-prone areas.

  • As climate change intensifies the threat of pest and disease outbreaks in Egypt, priority will be given to strengthening Integrated Pest and Disease Management (IPM) systems in alignment with IPCC climate scenarios and national agricultural strategies. Enhancing insurance schemes for climate-induced crop/livestock losses to cover climate-related losses, thereby reducing farmers’ vulnerability to climate shocks. Together, these measures aim to minimize climate-induced crop losses, decrease on chemical pesticides, and foster more sustainable and adaptive farming practices.

  • Equally important is the scaling up of early warning systems and the expansion of farmers’ access to real-time climate information through digital advisories and mobile platforms. This tool will deliver timely alerts on extreme weather events particularly relevant to Egypt’s context—such as heatwaves, prolonged droughts, unexpected floods, and sand or dust storms. Strengthening early action financing and anticipatory response mechanisms. will also be reinforced to enable farmers to prepare at the farm level, make proactive decisions, and ultimately safeguard agricultural productivity and food security.

5.3 Coastal Zones

  • Adaptation of the Northern Delta, 4 km² of land and 20 km of coastline protected (Beneficiary: 20 million people)

  • Integration of coastal protection in Mediterranean cities, full implementation across 3 cities by 2035

  • Protection of high-risk hot spots and Alexandria Coast o 40 km of coastline using nature-based solutions o 4 km along Alexandria coast east of the eastern harbor (Beneficiary: 600,000 people)

  • Structural and engineering coastal protection works o Seawalls, groins, and breakwaters o Protection coverage increasing from 8 million to 16 million people

  • Nourishment and hybrid coastal solutions: enhancing resilience along key coastal stretches (Beneficiary: 4.25 million people)

  • Nature-based land protection: expansion from 6 km to 18 km of protected land

Given the high vulnerability of Egypt’s low-lying coastal areas to sea-level rise, erosion, and extreme weather events, the adaptation policy actions in these zones are highly site-specific adopting both accommodation and protection approaches, primarily through:

  • Adaptation of the Northern Delta: A total of 69 km of coastal protection structures have been completed, with an additional 4 km planned. By 2035, these adaptation measures will safeguard 4 km² of land and 20 km of coastline, enhancing protection for approximately 20 million people living in the Delta region.

  • Integration of Coastal Protection in Mediterranean Cities: A multi-stage program is underway to embed coastal protection measures within three Mediterranean cities. The first stage (Stage 1) has been completed in one city, with full implementation across all three cities targeted by 2035.

  • Protection of Hot Spots and Alexandria Coast: Five high-risk coastal hot spots will be safeguarded using nature-based solutions spanning 40 km of coastline. Additionally, 4 km along the Alexandria coastline—east of the eastern harbor—will be fortified, collectively benefiting around 600,000 people by 2035.

  • Integrated Coastal Zone Management (ICZM) Plan: The ICZM Plan for Egypt’s North Coast has reached 70% completion. The goal is to finalize and fully implement the plan by 2035, ensuring sustainable and climate-resilient development across the coastal areas.

  • Structural and Engineering Coastal Protection Works: Hard infrastructure measures—such as seawalls, groins, and breakwaters—currently account for 40% of planned coastal protection structures, benefiting 8 million people. By 2035, the goal is to complete 100% of these works, doubling the protection coverage to 16 million people.

  • Nourishment and Hybrid Solutions: As part of an integrated coastal defense strategy, a series of nourishment and hybrid interventions are planned for completion by 2035. These measures are expected to enhance coastal resilience and benefit approximately 4.25 million people.

  • Nature-Based Land Protection: Nature-based adaptation measures such as sand dune stabilization, wooden barriers, and wild vegetation have been initiated, with plans to expand protected land areas from 6 km to 18 km by 2035.

  • Capacity Building and Early Warning Systems: To strengthens the preparedness and response to climate risks, the program includes training for 40 specialized personnel and the development and deployment of advanced early warning systems for sea-level rise and extreme weather events.

5.4 Urban Development and Tourism

The adaptation policy actions and measures in urban development and tourism are a combination of soft and hard interventions, primally through:

  • Directing city planning and architectural design towards meeting the requirements of green architecture and construction, and climate resilience including response to risks from climate change impacts (i.e. heat stress, floods), and the replacement and renovation of old houses in urban and rural areas informal housing.

  • Review of the road network to determine potential areas vulnerable to flooding, redirection of floodways away from roads, construction of obstructive dams to slow down the flow of floods, protection and diversion dams to direct floods to the main drainage basins, and construction of bridges over waterways to allow floodwater to flow unimpeded.

  • Strengthening the resilience of the national electricity grid to ensure the reliability and co ntinuity of energy supply under changing climatic conditions.

  • Conduct fragility and vulnerability assessments for Egypt’s key tourist sites, integrating Environmental Management Systems (EMS) in the hospitality sector.

  • Developing a fully operational monitoring system for the expected impacts of climate change to cover most of Egypt's key touristic areas. The system will enable real-time data collection and include the installation of early warning systems for key climate risks ensuring that most high-risk tourism sites are covered, while encouraging and supporting civil society organizations to participate in applying strategic operational policies in touristic locations.

  • Provide climate change awareness and capacity-building training for tourism operators, hotel owners, and tour guides to integrate adaptation strategies into their operations. Support tourism businesses in adopting climate-resilient and sustainable practices through MoTA’s initiatives in collaboration with development partners and stakeholders.

  • Develop and implement construction standards for eco-lodges and promote eco-tourism products in key tourist destinations.

  • Maintain and expand protected areas to cover 17% of national marine and wildlife areas, with at least 5% constituting coastal areas , while aligning ongoing efforts with updated global biodiversity frameworks.

5.5 Health Sector

  • Upgrade of 20 healthcare facilities with climate-resilient features

  • Training of 100,000 healthcare workers across all 27 governorates

  • Implementation of the One Health National Plan (4 key institutions engaged) (Beneficiary: national health system, including humans, animals, plants, and environmental health)

  • Resilience for Vulnerable Healthcare Facilities: To enhance climate resilience across Egypt’s healthcare sector, a nationwide program will be implemented to cover the entire population. The program will include comprehensive assessments of healthcare facilities to identify vulnerabilities in energy supply, water and sanitation systems, infrastructure, and the capacity of health staff. Plans are in place to upgrade 20 healthcare facilities with climate-resilient features and to train over 100,000 healthcare workers across all 27 governorates on climate-related health risks and response strategies. Furthermore, the One Health National Plan will be implemented to address the interconnections between human, animal, plant, and environmental health, with four key institutions actively engaged in its execution.

  • Promotion of Scientific Research and Demographic Studies: Three national institutions are working in close collaboration with the Ministry of Health and Population to advance scientific research and demographic studies focused on climate change impacts. The research aims to identify population groups in the most vulnerable areas to climate risks and to develop effective support mechanisms tailored to their needs. These efforts also include the assessment of climate change impacts on biodiversity within vulnerable and protected areas, ensuring an integrated approach to human and environmental health.

5.6 Other Adaptation Measures

  • Development of Weather Forecasting and Public Alert Systems: Egypt will strengthen its capacity to manage climate risks by developing advanced weather forecasting and public alert systems, alongside expanding functional early warning mechanisms. Forecasts will be disseminated through widely accessible platforms—such as television and official government websites—to ensure timely delivery of information, enabling citizens to take proactive, time-sensitive measures to reduce climate-related injury, illness, and mortality. Early warning systems will be expanded to serve a significantly larger population, with specialized components including a Nile water quality monitoring system in eight governorates, enabling rapid corrective actions in response to contamination or risk signals and safeguarding both public health and ecosystem integrity.

  • Integration of Climate Change into Education Curricula: The Ministry of Health & Population in collaboration with the Ministry of Education and the Ministry of Higher Education, climate change topics will be integrated into educational curricula at both school and university levels. This initiative will aim to raise awareness and build capacity among students from an early age, fostering a generation equipped with the knowledge and skills to address future climate challenges.

6. MEANS OF IMPLEMENTATION

6.1 Policy Mechanisms and Institutional Arrangements

The National Climate Change Council (NCCC) was founded in 2015 through the Prime Minister Decree No.1912 (later amended by the Prime Minister Decree No. 1129/2019) as the national authority in Egypt concerned with climate change. The members of its Supreme Committee are represented across the relevant line ministries headed by the Egyptian Prime Minister.

The Ministry of Environment (MoE) was established in 1997 by Presidential Decree no. 275/1997 and its policies are executed by the Egyptian Environmental Affairs Agency (EEAA). The Climate Change Central Department (CCCD) in the EEAA is the technical secretariat to the NCCC and focal point to the UNFCCC and coordinates climate efforts with relevant ministries and governmental entities. Furthermore, the planning and implementation of climate measures follows participatory approaches through collaboration among government, private sector, civil society, academia, media, international community and other concerned stakeholders.

In parallel, the United Nations Sustainable Development Cooperation Framework (UNSDCF) guides the UN development activities at the country level in support of the 2030 Agenda for Sustainable Development and key UN conventions, including the UNFCCC. Egypt signed its UNSDCF 2023–2027 in May 2023, structured around five strategic outcomes: People, Prosperity, Planet, Governance, and Women. It highlights the link between sustainable economic growth and environmental stewardship through efficient resource management and climate resilience. Transitioning to an environmentally sustainable economy creates new business opportunities and supports the implementation of the Paris Agreement, NDCs, the Convention on Biological Diversity, and other multilateral environmental agreements.

To operationalize these commitments, Egypt is advancing the Integrated National Financing Strategy (E-INFS), aligned with Egypt Vision 2030, to close the SDG financing gap. This supports coordinating investments across priority sectors—including social protection, health, education, women empowerment, climate action, and infrastructure.

Key measures focus on progressively increasing the share of green public investment, embedding environmental and climate considerations into investment planning and budgeting, and expanding the use of sustainable finance instruments. Institutional mechanisms are being strengthened to improve climate-related data, transparency, and project classification, including climate tagging and climate risk assessment within public investment systems. At the same time, policies seek

enhance market-based incentives for emissions reduction, and mainstream environmental sustainability across public policy and investment decision-making, supporting the transition toward a greener and more resilient economy.

This approach reinforces the implementation of Egypt’s NDC 3.0 and advances the broader sustainable development agenda.

6.2 International Agreements

Egypt adopts a coherent approach in addressing climate change, biodiversity loss, and land and ecosystem degradation. Egypt is a party to the three Rio Conventions (UNFCCC, CBD, and UNCCD), and recognizes the interconnectivity between them particularly with regards to adaptation and resilience, in addition to Montreal Protocol and chemicals conventions, among other multilateral international agreements.

In this context, Egypt is forging new partnerships to accelerate the implementation of its international commitments. These include initiatives on localization—such as “Strengthening Evidence-Based and Inclusive Planning, Budgeting, and Monitoring for SDG Transitions at the Local Level in Egypt”—and efforts to advance sustainable food systems through the program “Strengthening Sustainable and Resilient Food and Nutrition Systems in Egypt for SDG Acceleration.” Egypt is also deepening collaboration on emerging climate–economy mechanisms to enhance national readiness and ensure stronger alignment with sustainable development objectives.

6.3 Capacity Building and Technology Transfer

  • Building a strong foundation for the planning, implementation, and reporting of national climate actions requires adequate institutional, technical, and financial arrangements. In this context, several gaps should be addressed through capacity building and technology transfer.

  • Egypt has a strong climate institutional set-up is in place, but it is constrained by the limited resources and incomplete supporting legal architecture. The CCCD requires substantial financial and human resources to achieve its mandate, cooperate effectively with national partners, implement capacity building programs, and establish robust information systems to address the challenges of climate change.

  • The national climate reporting relies mainly on donor-funded projects and contracting consultants on an ad-hoc basis to prepare the reports required under the UNFCCC. To sustain these efforts and scale up the implementation of mitigation and adaptation measures across Egypt, the proposed MRV system should be institutionalized (refer to Section 8). The relevant ministries should establish climate change unit and/or assign employees to be responsible for climate data collection. Moreover, CAPMAS, the national statistical agency, to expand its environmental unit to include a Climate Change GHG Unit to aggregate climate data from the ministries in coordination with CCCD. A technical support working group could be formed from consultants and experts to provide support to CCCD, CAPMAS and ministerial units and design the data collection forms. For capacity building requirements, data management training (collection, analysis, archiving, and QA/QC) is required for the full-time employees within CCCD, CAPMAS, and ministerial units. The additional requirements include the legal instrument to mandate CAPMAS to report climate data to CCCD.

  • Technology, research and development, and innovation play an important role in pursuing ambitious climate actions. Egypt’s ability to implement climate action is highly constrained by the availability of appropriate technologies. Support is required to deploy climate- friendly technologies (such as energy storage), build administrative capacities, and supportive legal frameworks. Climate know-how should be freely-available as a global public good to collectively fast-track the transition towards the temperature goal of Paris Agreement. Furthermore, secure and diversify the supply of critical components and raw materials required for climate technologies (such as renewable energy), which are currently concentrated in few countries.

6.4 Financial Support

The financial resources required to implement Egypt’s NDC 3.0 from 2023 to 2035 are estimated at minimum USD 324 billion. The mitigation interventions require USD 211 billion and the adaptation interventions require USD 113 billion. The financial estimates are derived from the required upfront capital expenditures to implement mitigation and adaptation programmes, capacity building and technology transfer, and the human resources needed to implement the actions. The actual implementation of these mitigation and adaptation measures are conditional on the provision of adequate, appropriate external international finance through highly concessional finance and grants as appropriate.

Egypt is strongly committed to achieving the Sustainable Development Goals in alignment with the country's Vision 2030, and the government has already mobilized investments from domestic public and private resources. To fully realize the country’s ambitious aspirations outlined in this updated NDC, and to maximize its contribution to global greenhouse gas reduction efforts, enhanced international cooperation and support will be essential.

7. JUST AND EQUITABLE TRANSITION

Egypt’s NDC 3.0 prioritizes a just, inclusive transition that considers the socio-economic impacts of climate policies on workers, vulnerable groups, and regions heavily reliant on high-emission activities. The industrial sector, for example, recognizes both the risks and opportunities of decarbonizing areas such as cement, iron and steel, aluminum, and fertilizer production, highlighting the need for technology transfer, workforce retraining, and support mechanisms to minimize job displacement. The waste management sector is advancing reforms that emphasize job creation in underserved areas, the formalization of informal labor, and improved community- level services. Investment promotion efforts are directed toward channeling public and private capital into climate-smart sectors with strong social co-benefits, such as renewable energy and inclusive infrastructure.

Within this broader transition, climate-resilient agriculture, agrifood systems, and sustainable land use are key pillars of Egypt’s climate and development agenda, contributing to food and water security, rural livelihoods, public health, economic resilience, and social stability. National priorities include climate-smart and resource-efficient agricultural production, improved soil and ecosystem health, resilient agricultural value chains, digital agriculture, early-warning and risk- management systems, and inclusive social protection. These measures aim to protect vulnerable rural communities, strengthening the resilience of agricultural livelihoods, and ensuring that the benefits and costs of the transition are equitably distributed. Stronger cross-sector coordination across agriculture, water, environment, health, trade, investment, and local development is essential to support integrated planning and implementation at national and subnational levels. Enabling policies, strengthened institutions, measurable targets, and investable project pipelines are also required to mobilize public, private, and international finance and scale up prioritized agrifood system solutions that reduce climate risks, enhance land and water productivity, and accelerate a just transition towards resilient, low-emission agrifood systems.

While direct gender or youth targeted programs are not yet widely detailed, some sectoral strategies indicate growing alignment with national frameworks on gender equity and inclusive development. Egypt’s NDC 3.0 considers to strengthening the integration of gender equity, youth empowerment, children protection, and social safeguards across sectors with vulnerable groups, with plans to expand capacity building, stakeholder engagement, and sector-specific response measures to ensure that climate action advances social justice and leaves no one behind. Egypt’s institutional and regulatory framework, guided by Egypt’s Vision 2030 and constitutional principles, ensures that the green economy transition is fair, inclusive, and sustainable. The Government Program (2024/2025–2026/2027) prioritizes Building the Egyptian Human, while the Gender-Responsive Planning Guide integrates social dimensions into development, promoting equal opportunities across all social groups.

8. MONITORING, REPORTING, AND VERIFICATION

Egypt recognizes that a robust National Monitoring, Reporting, and Verification system is essential to ensuring the credibility and effectiveness of its NDC 3.0. Building on the framework established under NDC 2.0, Egypt will maintain the core MRV structure, with the National Council for Climate Change as the supervisory body and the Climate Change Central Department as the coordinating entity, working with all relevant sectors and agencies.

Formalized under Minister of Environment Decree No. 415/2023, the MRV system will align with UNFCCC and Paris Agreement Enhanced Transparency Framework (ETF) requirements. Its full launch remains contingent on funding, but Egypt will continue to build on existing partial MRV activities as the basis for a fully operational national system. The MRV structure will include two specialized working groups under the NCCC and CCCD, the Quality Assurance Working Group (QA-WG) and the Technical Support Working Group (TS-WG)—with CAPMAS serving as the central data coordination entity. The system will operate across four integrated tracks: 1. GHG Inventory MRV – tracking national emissions and removals. 2. Mitigation Policies and Actions MRV – assessing policy effectiveness. 3. Support Received MRV – monitoring financial, technical, and capacity-building support. 4. Adaptation Policies and Actions MRV – evaluating adaptation measures and resilience efforts.

A centralized climate database will compile and report data across all tracks, support monitoring of Internationally Transferred Mitigation Outcomes (ITMOs) under Article 6 and ensure long-term data archiving for consistency and comparability. The MRV process will embed structured stakeholder engagement, involving sectoral bodies, private sector, civil society, and academia to identify challenges, capture co-benefits, enhance accountability, and strengthen national ownership of climate action. How the Party considers that its nationally determined contribution is fair and ambitious in the light of its national circumstances:

(a)

How the Party considers that its nationally determined contribution is fair and ambitious in the light of its national circumstances;

Egypt adopts the equity principle of common but differentiated responsibilities in accordance with the respective national capabilities, in accordance with the UNFCCC and Paris Agreement. Despite that Egypt’s contribution to global emissions is marginal, but the country is highly vulnerable to climate change impacts (specifically Delta region) that threaten its path to sustainable development. Egypt has set an ambitious target to contribute to reducing GHG emissions. Egypt update of its NDC is ambitious in terms of meeting the long- term goal set by the Paris Agreement including mitigation and adaptation.

Leveraging this ambition is directly proportional to the availability of additional sustainable and predictable support to facilitate the means for implementation.
