NOTE TO READERS

Gabon's third Nationally Determined Contribution (NDC 3.0) builds on the country's climate commitments, while reflecting a strengthened ambition in terms of sustainable development, resilience and low-carbon transition.

It sets out the strategic orientations, national objectives and priority mitigation and adaptation measures that Gabon intends to implement by 2030 and beyond, in accordance with the Long-Term Development Vision and the commitments made under the Paris Agreement.

The document is structured in two main parts:

  • The first 55 pages present the NDC itself, including the context, objectives, scenarios, assumptions, quantified contributions and the implementation and monitoring mechanisms.
  • The annexes detail the planned and proposed projects for achieving the objectives by 2035. For each project, these annexes specify the description, the implementing entity, the supporting entities, the financing needs, the beneficiaries, the implementation period, as well as the actions planned between 2025 and 2035.

NDC 3.0 was developed under the coordination of the Ministry of Environment, Climate and Sustainable Development, with contributions from public institutions, the private sector, local authorities, civil society, academia and technical and financial partners.

The analyses, assumptions and projections presented are based on the best data available at the time of writing and may be updated to reflect the economic, technological and institutional developments of the country.

NDC 3.0 thus constitutes a national roadmap towards carbon neutrality, climate resilience and green and inclusive growth for Gabon.

Executive summary

Context and scope

Gabon presents its revised NDC for the period 2025-2035, aligned with the conclusions of the first Global Stocktake at COP28. As a country with high forest cover and low deforestation, Gabon retains its exceptional status as a net carbon sink, with net removals of -72,4 MtCO₂eq in 2023. This third version builds on the assessment of the previous NDC, strengthens the country's ambition to maintain its status as a net carbon sink, and makes adaptation a strategic priority in its own right.

Mitigation commitments

Gabon's national trajectory, which integrates both emissions from all sectors and removals by forests, reveals a considerably strengthened climate ambition. The country starts from a unique situation, that of a net carbon sink, and its objective is to maintain and even increase this capacity in the face of development pressures.

The reference scenario (BAU) shows a vulnerability. Without strengthened climate action, the country's net removal capacity is projected at -69,3 MtCO₂eq in 2030, then -58,2 MtCO₂eq in 2035. This gradual decline means that Gabon's carbon sink would weaken, reducing its contribution to the fight against climate change.

The implementation of existing policies (With Existing Measures scenario, WEM) already reverses this trend. Thanks to measures already under way, Gabon projects to maintain net removals of -79,4 MtCO₂eq in 2030, an improvement of 14,6% compared with the BAU scenario. This trajectory strengthens further by 2035, with removals of -92,4 MtCO₂eq, exceeding the trend scenario by 58,7%.

Maximum ambition (With Additional Measures scenario, WAM) relies on international support. With additional financing and technologies, Gabon could deploy its full potential. As early as 2030, net removals would reach -94,9 MtCO₂eq, exceeding the BAU scenario by 37%. The peak objective for 2035 is -121,5 MtCO₂eq, which represents more than double (+108,6%) the removal capacity that would be available without climate action.

Adaptation strategy

Gabon's NDC 3.0 integrates an adaptation strategy developed to address the already visible impacts of climate change. It is structured around four essential pillars aimed at protecting the country's population, economy and ecosystems.

In the face of the threat of sea-level rise, the protection of coastal areas is a national priority. The objective is to safeguard vital economic infrastructure located on the coastline, such as ports and industrial facilities, through a mix of civil engineering works and nature-based solutions such as mangrove restoration.

To ensure food security, Gabon will rely on a transformation of its agriculture. The country is promoting climate-smart practices and agroforestry to strengthen crop resilience to droughts, floods and new pests, while improving sustainable productivity.

Gabon also aims to secure the supply of drinking water and water for agricultural use. This involves protecting watersheds, better demand management and strengthening infrastructure in the face of increased risks of shortage and pollution.

Gabonese cities, often affected by floods, will need to be adapted. The strategy provides for strengthening the resilience of urban infrastructure through better stormwater management, the modernisation of sanitation networks and the development of early warning systems for disasters, even beyond urban areas.

Finally, the health system will need to adapt to respond to climate-sensitive diseases. The country will strengthen epidemiological surveillance of diseases such as malaria or dengue, whose spread is favoured by changes in temperature and precipitation, and will prepare its health facilities for these new threats.

Cross-cutting aspects and other strategic considerations

Cross-cutting aspects, notably gender and social inclusion, youth and children, just transition, loss and damage, climate-related peace and security, as well as other strategic issues, notably the blue economy and accession to the Freshwater Challenge, digital technologies, and the promotion of traditional knowledge, have been taken into account in view of their place in the socio-economic development of the country. In addition, the increased engagement of the private sector and financial institutions has been taken into account in view of their strategic role.

Financing needs and mobilisation strategy

The implementation of Gabon's NDC 3.0 requires an overall budget estimated at 3 951,314 billion FCFA, of which 83 % is for mitigation (3 278,6 billion FCFA) and 17 % for adaptation (672,7 billion FCFA). The priority sectors in terms of investment are energy (3 115 billion FCFA), forests (342 billion FCFA) and agriculture (242 billion FCFA).

Of this total amount, only 92,133 billion FCFA have already been mobilised, revealing an additional need of more than 3 850 billion FCFA. This situation highlights the need for an ambitious and coordinated strategy for mobilising climate finance.

Gabon is adopting an innovative approach to mobilise the necessary resources. The creation of the Gabonese Platform for Climate Finance (PGFC) will serve as a one-stop shop to structure projects and attract investment. The valorisation of carbon assets represents a crucial financial lever, with the use of the 187 million REDD+ credits already certified and recourse to the mechanisms of Article 6 of the Paris Agreement. The country is also positioning its strategy on international voluntary markets and is actively participating in the Africa Carbon Markets Initiative. Gabon is also counting on the mobilisation of philanthropic funds, notably resources from foundations, companies and individuals willing to support climate action in Gabon.

In addition to mitigation and adaptation measures, cross-cutting and implementation measures (institutional strengthening, MRV, capacity building, transparency, fiscal and financial tools, etc.) are necessary to ensure the effectiveness and sustainability of NDC 3.0. The estimated budget for these cross-cutting measures is 144,5 billion FCFA. When this amount is added to the initial need, the overall total cost of NDC 3.0, including sectoral measures and cross-cutting implementation measures, therefore amounts to 4 095,464 billion FCFA.

Institutional strengthening for implementation

The success of NDC 3.0 relies on a solid institutional framework. The creation of a Climate Project Development Unit (UDPC) will make it possible to professionalise the structuring of bankable projects. The strategic steering role of the National Climate Council (CNC) is strengthened, while the improved Measurement, Reporting and Verification system guarantees the transparency and accountability of climate actions and above all facilitates the assessment of the efforts and progress achieved. Collaboration with all stakeholders is also strengthened to ensure successful implementation of NDC3.0. The role of the private sector and civil society organisations (CSOs) is strengthened to support the government in its efforts to combat climate change and achieve the projected ambitions.

Outlook

NDC 3.0 consolidates Gabon's position as one of the climate leaders in Africa and in the world, demonstrating the compatibility between environmental preservation and socio-economic development. Its successful implementation will require a solid partnership between the State, the private sector, civil society and international partners, making natural capital a strategic asset for future generations.

Planning process

The planning of Gabon's NDC3 is based on a participatory, multisectoral and integrated approach, building on recent legal reforms and institutional arrangements already established.

  • 1. Political and strategic steering
    • The National Climate Council (CNC), placed under the authority of the Presidency of the Republic, retains a strategic coordination role. It ensures coherence between Gabon's international commitments (Paris Agreement, CAFI, Kigali Amendment) and national development priorities (Emerging Gabon Strategic Plan, Vision 2050).
    • The Ministry of Environment, Climate and Ecological Transition is the supervising entity for the technical implementation and oversight of NDC3
    • Gabon's Long-Term Climate Vision, which charts the national trajectory towards a low-carbon, climate-resilient and inclusive economy by 2050, constituted a strategic foundation of this NDC3.0
  • 2. Institutional and sectoral arrangements
    • Each sectoral ministry (energy, agriculture, forests, mines, transport, urban planning, health, water, waste) is responsible for translating the objectives of NDC3 into its sectoral policies and plans.
    • Technical climate units are being set up within ministries in order to integrate mitigation and adaptation objectives into sectoral strategies and annual budgets.
    • Local authorities are involved in adaptation, notably for the management of urban flooding, coastal adaptation, territorial planning and the protection of natural resources.
  • 3. Legal and regulatory framework
    • Law No. 018/2022 on climate change provides the legal foundation, making it mandatory to integrate climate considerations into public policies, budgets and private investments.
    • It also establishes economic instruments (eco-taxes, carbon fees, domestic carbon markets) and institutional instruments (National Climate Fund) to support implementation.
  • 4. Monitoring, evaluation and transparency system
    • The national MRV (Measurement, Reporting, Verification) system is strengthened to track the implementation of mitigation measures (GHG) and adaptation measures (resilience indicators).
    • The First Biennial Transparency Report (2024) serves as a basis for institutionalising the tracking of commitments.
    • Periodic reports to the CNC and to Parliament will ensure accountability.
  • 5. Stakeholder mobilisation and financing
    • The private sector is mobilised through public-private partnerships (climate PPPs), green tax incentives and access to carbon credits.
    • Civil society, NGOs and local communities participate in the co-design and monitoring of projects, notably in the forestry sector (community forests, REDD+).
    • Financial mobilisation is based on two pillars: national resources (green budgets, Climate Fund, eco-taxes) and international resources (GCF, GEF, CAFI, ACMI, green bonds).
  • 6. Operational implementation mechanism
    • NDC3 will be implemented through a portfolio of climate projects and programmes, classified as unconditional (WEM) and conditional (WAM).
    • Each project will be accompanied by a technical sheet detailing objectives, costs, monitoring indicators and sources of financing.
    • The National Adaptation Plan (PNA) and the Long-Term Climate Vision 2050 serve as coherence frameworks, ensuring that NDC3 fits within a sustainable national trajectory.

Assumptions and methodological approach

Gabon's NDC3 builds on the assumptions and methods developed in the First Biennial Transparency Report (2024). Three scenarios were developed: BAU (continuation of trends), WEM (existing measures) and WAM (conditional additional measures). The projections integrate demographic, economic and energy dynamics, as well as changes in land use and forest management. The inventories and projections are carried out in accordance with the 2006 IPCC Guidelines (with the 2019 Refinement) and the GACMO software for the cost-benefit assessment of measures. Forests and land are modelled on the basis of remote sensing data and recent deforestation trends, while the agriculture and waste sectors integrate assumptions of growth in demand and strengthening of sustainable practices. This approach ensures coherence between the commitments of NDC3.0, the Long-Term Climate Vision 2050 and the Enhanced Transparency Framework.

How the NDC is fair and ambitious

NDC3 aims to be more balanced and ambitious: it consolidates the role of net carbon sink while placing adaptation as a national priority, in order to respond to growing vulnerabilities (floods, coastal erosion, droughts, heatwaves). It justifies its ambition by the dual requirement of climate justice (Gabon emits little but is vulnerable) and African leadership (carbon neutrality + resilience).

1 Introduction

The Gabonese Republic, a signatory to the Paris Agreement since 2016, reaffirms through this Nationally Determined Contribution (NDC 3.0) its commitment to contribute to global efforts to combat climate change. In accordance with Article 4.9 of the Agreement, Gabon has revised its NDC in response to the conclusions of the first Global Stocktake (GST1) published at COP28 in 2023. This stocktake highlighted the collective inadequacy of current efforts and called for an urgent increase in ambition in terms of mitigation, adaptation and support to developing countries.

In this international context, Gabon's NDC3.0 is in line both with respect for international climate commitments and with a national will to intensify its efforts by 2035 and beyond. As a country with high forest cover and low deforestation (HFLD), Gabon remains one of the few States in the world to maintain a net negative greenhouse gas emissions balance, with net removals estimated at 72,4 MtCO₂eq in 2023 according to the work of the Biennial Transparency Report (BTR) published by the country in 2024. This singularity positions the country as a global carbon sink, playing a strategic role in stabilising the global climate.

Since NDC 2.0 submitted in 2022, significant progress has been made, notably the adoption of legislative and regulatory texts (ordinance on climate change, revision of the forestry law, etc.), the establishment of strengthened climate transparency tools, notably the preparation of the country's first BTR as well as the country's 3rd national communication on climate change, and the development of strategic frameworks such as Gabon's Long-Term Climate Vision (VLT). The assessment of NDC2.0 identified fifty-four sectoral projects covering energy, agriculture, forestry and other land use (FOLU), waste and coastal zones, contributing to the achievement of the set objectives and to an improvement of the country's net removal capacity of approximately 6% between 2021 and 2024.

However, the implementation of the NDC also revealed major challenges related, among other things, to access to climate finance, the increased integration of adaptation, the valorisation of carbon markets and the strengthening of institutional and sectoral capacities.

NDC3.0 is thus part of the operationalisation of Gabon's Long-Term Climate Vision, which charts the national trajectory towards a low-carbon, climate-resilient and inclusive economy by 2050. It aims in particular to:

  • Maintain and strengthen Gabon's carbon neutrality beyond 2050;
  • Draw on the lessons learned from previous NDCs and multi-stakeholder consultations;
  • Improve the consideration of adaptation to climate change;
  • Improve climate governance and strengthen transparency in Gabon's climate action.

This new NDC constitutes a decisive step in Gabon's climate trajectory. It results from an inclusive and multisectoral consultation process that mobilised all national stakeholders: public institutions, local authorities, the private sector, civil society, academia and technical and financial partners. Based on updated scientific data, this NDC reflects the country's will to anchor its climate choices in the best available knowledge.

It thus affirms the country's determination to combine ambition and responsibility, by setting measurable, transparent and credible objectives, and by strengthening synergies between climate policies, sustainable development, biodiversity protection and community resilience.

2 National circumstances

2.1 Ecological context

Gabon is a Central African country with an area of nearly 267 667 km². Located at the heart of the Congo Basin, the second largest tropical forest massif in the world, it benefits from a strategic geographical position on the Atlantic seaboard, with more than 800 km of coastline (VLT, 2024)2. The country is recognised as a State with high forest cover and a low deforestation rate (HFLD), with nearly 88 % of its territory covered by natural forests (VLT, 2024). This exceptional natural capital, also highlighted in Gabon's Long-Term Climate Vision, makes Gabon a major actor in carbon sequestration.

Thanks to this extensive forest cover, Gabon plays a major role in carbon sequestration. Until 2015, the country recorded net removals estimated at more than 100 MtCO₂eq per year (VLT, 2024), positioning itself as a carbon sink of global importance. However, this removal capacity has gradually declined, reaching 72,4 MtCO₂eq in 2023 (VLT, 2024). Despite this decrease, Gabon retains its status as a net carbon sink and remains an essential actor in global climate change mitigation efforts.

2.2 Socio-economic context

Gabon is classified as an upper-middle-income country, with GDP per capita fluctuating between 6 800 and 7 700 USD over the last decade, making it one of the highest in sub-Saharan Africa (VLT, 2024). However, this macroeconomic performance contrasts sharply with the social situation: poverty and inequality indicators remain close to those of the least developed countries (LDCs), underlining the persistence of an unequal distribution of wealth (BTR, 2024).

Economic growth remains fragile and dependent on natural resources. After a recovery in 2022, with growth estimated at 3 %, the country experienced a slowdown in 2023 to 2,3 %, due to the decline in timber and manganese production, as well as logistical disruptions related to the Trans-Gabon railway (BTR, 2024). Public debt also remains a factor of macroeconomic vulnerability, reaching 72 % of GDP in 2023 (BTR, 2024).

The Gabonese economic model is dominated by the exploitation of extractive resources, notably oil, mining and forests, to which are added services and a still underdeveloped agriculture.

  • Oil and hydrocarbons: The oil sector represents approximately 45 % of the country's GDP and 80 % of its export revenues. Hydrocarbons represent 50 % of Gabon's exports and 38 % of its GDP. The country faces economic challenges, notably an excessive dependence on the oil sector, which makes it vulnerable to fluctuations in world oil prices. This situation underlines the importance of diversifying the economy to mitigate the risks related to this dependence, as highlighted in the PNDT (VLT, 2024).
  • Mining: The mining sector is also important, producing manganese, iron, gold, diamonds and other minerals, whose contribution to national wealth is relatively low, as it represents between 4 % and 7 % of GDP depending on the year (VLT, 2024).
  • Forest and timber: Timber is an essential resource for the Gabonese economy. The country is the second largest timber producer in Africa. Forests cover 88 % of the national territory. The forestry sector is one of the pillars of the economy: it represents 6 % of non-hydrocarbon GDP and is the largest private-sector employer (20 000 direct and indirect jobs) (VLT, 2024).
  • Agriculture: Despite its potential, agriculture contributes only 6 % of GDP, but it employs approximately 30 % of the working population. It remains dominated by food crops (cassava, plantain) and a few cash crops (rubber, palm oil). The country is highly dependent on food imports, which grew at an average annual rate of 7 % between 2010 and 2019 (VLT, 2024).
  • Industry and services: The industrial fabric is still limited and dominated by agri-food, wood processing and refining. Special economic zones, such as the one in Nkok, nevertheless contribute to gradual industrialisation and the promotion of local processing (VLT, 2024).

Despite the national wealth, Gabon has a structurally high unemployment rate. It rose from 16,3 % in 2005 to 25,7 % in 2017, particularly affecting young people aged 15 to 29, whose unemployment rate ranges between 25 and 34 % (National SDG Report, 2019). Women are also more affected, with an unemployment rate of 32,3 % in 2017 compared with 20,5 % for men.

Monetary poverty affects approximately 33,4 % of the population. Territorial inequalities are marked: poverty is estimated at 29,4 % in urban areas compared with 59,5 % in rural areas. Extreme poverty is more concentrated in the countryside (25,4 %) than in the cities (5,6 %) (National SDG Report, 2019).

2.4 Climate vulnerability profile

Gabon's climate is equatorial, characterised by hot and humid temperatures and abundant rainfall. However, for several decades, signs of change have been observed:

  • The mean annual temperature has increased and is expected to rise by 0,9 to 2,5 °C by 2060, and by 1,3 to 4,1 °C by 2090 (VLT, 2024).
  • Precipitation is becoming more variable, with projections ranging from -22 % to +25 % by 2090, suggesting an intensification of extreme events (prolonged droughts, torrential rains).

These changes increase the country's exposure to climate hazards such as:

  • Floods: these constitute the most recurrent risk, affecting both urban areas and river floodplains. The events of 1988 and 2012 affected 10 000 and 77 000 people respectively, while the storms of 2009-2010 generated economic losses equivalent to 3,2 % of GDP (VLT, 2024).
  • Droughts: recorded in 1983, 1992, 2002, 2007 and 2015-2016, they compromise food crops and worsen food insecurity.
  • Sea level rise: it directly threatens Libreville and Port-Gentil, two vital economic centres, and weakens oil infrastructure located in the coastal zone (NDC2.0, 2022).
  • Heatwaves: historically less frequent, they are increasing in intensity and pose growing health risks, particularly in poorly ventilated urban areas (BTR, 2024).

Gabon's climate vulnerability is strongly shaped by structural factors:

  • Rapid and poorly planned urbanisation: more than 87 % of the population lives in urban areas, often in neighbourhoods exposed to flooding and landslides, with inadequate drainage systems (BTR, 2024).
  • Dependence on rain-fed agriculture: although agriculture represents only 6 % of GDP, it employs nearly 30 % of the working population and remains vulnerable to rainfall variability. This dependence reinforces food insecurity in rural areas.
  • Deficit of resilience infrastructure: waste management and wastewater treatment are still inadequate, aggravating health risks during floods (NDC2.0, 2022).

The effects of climate change manifest differently across sectors:

  • Agriculture and food security: droughts and rainfall variability reduce agricultural productivity and weaken livelihoods, in a context where the country already imports nearly 50 % of its food needs.
  • Coastal zones: erosion and marine submersion threaten coastal ecosystems, oil infrastructure, urban areas and coastal national parks.
  • Forests: although they constitute a powerful carbon sink, they are weakened by deforestation and logging, which could reduce their sequestration capacity.
  • Public health: floods promote the spread of water-borne and vector-borne diseases, while heatwaves increase cardio-respiratory risks in urban areas (BTR, 2024).
  • Energy and infrastructure: hydroelectric, mining and oil networks are exposed to floods and extreme winds, compromising the continuity of essential services.

2.5 Policy, legal and institutional framework for climate action

Over the past two decades, Gabon has established a solid legal and institutional framework aimed at structuring its climate action and translating its international commitments into national planning and governance instruments. This architecture combines strong political will at the highest level of the State, a modern legal basis, and a set of institutions dedicated to the coordination, regulation and monitoring of climate actions.

2.5.1 The policy framework

Gabon has integrated climate action into its national development planning through several strategic documents:

  • The Strategic Plan for an Emerging Gabon (PSGE), which recognises climate as a cross-cutting pillar of sustainable development.
  • The National Climate Plan (2012), the first operational framework bringing together the country's mitigation and adaptation strategies.
  • The Nationally Determined Contribution (NDC2.0, 2021), which sets an ambitious objective of maintaining carbon neutrality beyond 2050, with specific sectoral measures in energy, agriculture, forests, transport and urban planning.
  • The Long-Term Climate Vision (2024), which charts Gabon's trajectory towards a low-carbon and resilient economy by 2050.

These national policies are aligned with Gabon's international commitments, notably its active participation in the UNFCCC, the Kyoto Protocol, the Paris Agreement, and the Central African Forest Initiative (CAFI), which provides financial support for the conservation and sustainable management of Gabon's forests.

2.5.2 Legal framework

The cornerstone of national climate legislation is Law No. 018/2022 on combating climate change in Gabon. This law is a first in Central Africa and establishes:

  • the guiding principles of climate action, based on prevention, precaution, responsibility and intergenerational equity;
  • the obligation for the State to integrate climate into all sectoral policies (energy, agriculture, forestry, transport, urban planning, etc.);
  • recognition of the economic value of the ecosystem services provided by Gabon's forests, paving the way for the certification and commercialisation of sovereign carbon credits (BTR, 2024).

The law also strengthens provisions relating to transparency, accountability and the participation of local communities, in line with the Paris Agreement and UNFCCC guidelines.

2.5.3 Institutional framework

Climate governance in Gabon is based on several key bodies:

  • The National Climate Council (CNC), created in 2010, serves as a strategic steering body and advisory body to the government. It ensures intersectoral coordination and the coherence of climate policies.
  • The Ministry of Environment, Climate and Ecological Transition is the supervisory authority, responsible for implementing policies and overseeing technical instruments such as the national greenhouse gas inventory, the national MRV (Measurement, Reporting and Verification) System, and the preparation of climate communications and reports.
  • Specialised agencies such as the Gabonese Agency for Space Studies and Observations (AGEOS), the National Agency for National Parks (ANPN) and the Gabonese Agency for Green Economy Development (AGADEV) provide data, support planning and develop field projects.

At the local level, local authorities are beginning to be involved through territorial planning and community adaptation projects, in accordance with the provisions of the climate law.

3 NDC3.0 development process

The development of Gabon's third Nationally Determined Contribution (NDC3.0) followed a highly collaborative and inclusive process aligned with international best practices. Conducted under the coordination of the National Climate Council (CNC), with technical support from Climate Analytics and the NDC Partnership, this process benefited from interministerial oversight and multi-stakeholder engagement aimed at ensuring the legitimacy and national ownership of the NDC.

The process was steered by the CNC, the body mandated by the government to coordinate climate action, with the active participation of key sectoral ministries (Environment, Energy, Agriculture, Forests, Transport, Industry, Health, Planning, Finance, etc.).

Stakeholder engagement

A particular effort was made to involve a diversity of stakeholders. In total, more than 300 representatives from public institutions, the private sector, local authorities, civil society, academia, women's and youth associations, as well as technical and financial partners took part in the consultations.

The process combined several mechanisms:

  • National launch and validation workshops that brought together all stakeholders around the objectives of the NDC3.0, including technical and financial partners.
  • Targeted sectoral consultations organised in ten technical ministries, making it possible to identify mitigation and adaptation priorities, ongoing projects, and capacity-building and financing needs.
  • Bilateral interviews with strategic actors, in order to collect specific data and deepen discussions on certain sensitive sectors, such as energy, forests or coastal zones.

From NDC2.0 to NDC3.0

The NDC3.0 builds on the NDC2.0 submitted in 2022. Its development was informed by the evaluation of the NDC2.0, which made it possible to identify successes3 as well as gaps, challenges and opportunities for raising ambition. These lessons constituted an essential starting point for defining updated targets and realistic measures while proposing a strengthened implementation framework.

Alignment with national priorities

In order to ensure its successful implementation and to engage all actors, whether political, technical or community-based, alignment with national priorities was a key element of the process. This alignment was ensured with Gabon's Long-Term Climate Vision (VLT), the national communications and biennial transparency reports (BTR), as well as with the main national development plans and strategies (PNDT, PSGE, etc.) and sectoral strategies, which are detailed in the annex. This alignment aims to ensure the consistency of data and their comparability at both national and international levels.

4 Contributions to the mitigation of greenhouse gas emissions

This section presents in detail the national commitments regarding net greenhouse gas emission reductions, based on updated reference (BAU) scenarios, and setting quantified targets for 2030 and 2035.

4.1 National mitigation scenario

The BAU scenario illustrates a business-as-usual trajectory in which no additional mitigation measures are implemented. National emissions then follow the historical dynamics of growth in energy demand, urbanisation, industrialisation and logging.

Without taking into account the Forests and Other Land Use (FOLU) sector, Gabon's national emissions would follow a worrying trajectory. They would increase from 8 053,6 GgCO₂eq in 2020 to more than 11 500 GgCO₂eq in 2035, representing an increase of approximately +43 % over fifteen years.

When the FOLU sector is included, Gabon's situation is radically different and confirms its status as a net carbon sink, even in a business-as-usual (BAU) scenario. However, this absorption capacity would undergo a worrying erosion, falling from -73 294,40 GgCO₂eq in 2020 to -58 259,50 GgCO₂eq in 2035. This decline of nearly 20,5 % reveals growing pressure on forest ecosystems.

In its Long-Term Climate Vision document (VLT, 2024), Gabon has set itself the ambition:

“By 2050, Gabon aspires to be a united nation, with a competitive and diversified economy based on clean and renewable energy, low greenhouse gas emission industries, cities and commercial sectors that creates inclusive and equitable opportunities aligned with the aspirations of the entire Gabonese population, with sustainable territorial development and resilient communities while maintaining its carbon sink status, a healthy and self-reliant population, girls, men and women with a strong sense of belonging, living in a healthy and safe environment, through good governance and strong partnerships between government, business and civil society, scientific research and technological innovation.”

To realise the 2050 national vision, the country has set, within the framework of this NDC, mitigation targets for 2030 and 2035 according to the WEM and WAM scenarios.

The WEM scenario incorporates all unconditional measures already being implemented, financed by national resources and partner initiatives under way.

Excluding FOLU (table 5), the climate policy scenarios show a clear improvement. The with existing measures (WEM) scenario would reduce emissions by more than 20 % compared with BAU by 2030, and by more than 23 % by 2035. The highest ambition (WAM), supported by international cooperation, could lead to reductions of approximately 30 % in 2030 and exceed 40 % in 2035.

With FOLU (table 6), in 2035, removals would increase from -58 259,5 GgCO₂eq (BAU) to - 92 430,6 GgCO₂eq, i.e. an improvement of +58,7 % in the country's carbon sequestration capacity. This confirms that ongoing actions, such as the National Land Use Plan, timber traceability and the establishment of community forests, are consolidating the country's sink function.

The WAM scenario, for its part, combines unconditional measures (already financed) with conditional measures that require international financial and technical support.

Gabon's Long-Term Climate Vision (VLT, 2024) defines strategic pillars to guide the country's transition towards a low-carbon, resilient and inclusive economy by 2050. These pillars are broken down into priority mitigation measures by sector, consistent with the country's national and international commitments.

In the energy sector, the priority is to gradually transform the national energy mix. In the buildings sector, considered a subset of energy, the VLT emphasises the development of sustainable, low-carbon human settlements. This includes the integration of green building standards, energy efficiency in buildings and sustainable urban planning, in order to reconcile population growth with a low carbon footprint.

These measures will significantly reduce transport-related emissions while improving the quality of urban life.

In the industrial sector, also attached to energy, priority efforts focus on the energy efficiency of industrial processes and the adoption of clean technologies in production chains. In agriculture, forestry and other land use (AFOLU), the priority is to build agricultural resilience capable of supporting food security and rural development while contributing to emissions reduction. The country is focusing on developing a sustainable, technologically advanced and commercially viable agricultural sector, as well as on maintaining and restoring the forest and natural ecosystems that form the basis of its status as a high forest cover, low deforestation (HFLD) country.

Finally, in the waste sector, the VLT promotes integrated and circular waste management, in which solid waste is transformed into a resource. This involves putting in place modern treatment infrastructure, energy and material recovery from waste, and the promotion of a circular economy.

These sectoral priorities show that Gabon is committed to a trajectory of structural transformation, in which economic growth and social development are accompanied by a significant reduction in emissions and better management of natural resources. 4.2.3 Contribution of the waste sector

The waste sector, although representing a relatively small share of national emissions, plays an important role in Gabon's transition towards a circular economy and carbon neutrality.

In the BAU (Business as Usual) scenario, waste-related emissions show a steady increase. They rise from 2,91 GgCO₂eq in 2025 to 3,23 GgCO₂eq in 2030, reaching 3,58 GgCO₂eq in 2035, i.e. an increase of nearly 23 % between 2025 and 2035. This trajectory reflects population growth, accelerated urbanisation and the inadequacy of modern waste management infrastructure.

Gabon's Long-Term Climate Vision to 2050 sets out a clear vision for the waste sector:

“By 2050, Gabon aspires to implement integrated waste management strategies with a priority on the reduction, reuse and recycling of waste, integrating the development of waste infrastructure and eco-citizen responsibility, minimising the impact of waste disposal on the environment and health, and fostering the transition to a circular economy within a strengthened regulatory and normative framework that is inclusive, participatory and collaborative, with the increased involvement of private actors and civil society.”

For the waste sector, the mitigation actions planned by Gabon focus on improving waste collection and treatment, reducing methane emissions through the recovery of organic waste, and developing recycling. Details are available in the annex.

The WEM (With Existing Measures) scenario (Figure 6), which incorporates actions already undertaken such as improved collection, the promotion of selective sorting and initial efforts to recover organic waste, makes it possible to slightly contain this increase. Emissions remain virtually stable at 2,91 GgCO₂eq in 2025, then reach 3,22 GgCO₂eq in 2030 and 3,56 GgCO₂eq in 2035. Compared with BAU, the gain remains marginal (≈ -0,55 % in 2035), which shows that current measures, although useful, are not sufficient to significantly alter the trend.

In the WAM (With Additional Measures) scenario, which assumes international support to deploy modern treatment, energy recovery and recycling infrastructure on a large scale, Gabon could achieve the ambitious “zero waste” objective. This scenario is based on a structural transformation: generalisation of source separation, development of composting and biogas units, industrial recycling, and the progressive prohibition of landfilling untreated waste. 5 Contribution to climate change adaptation

Unlike the NDC2.0, which emphasised Gabon's role as a net carbon sink and a major mitigation actor thanks to its forests, the NDC3.0 now gives priority to adaptation. This strategic choice reflects at once:

  • recognition of the country's high vulnerability to climate hazards (floods, droughts, sea-level rise, heatwaves) (NDC2.0, 2022),
  • the need to protect populations, infrastructure and ecosystems against impacts already observed,
  • and the determination to establish resilience as a central pillar of sustainable national development.

Thus, while maintaining its commitment to carbon neutrality beyond 2050, Gabon affirms that adaptation is the indispensable condition for guaranteeing human security, economic prosperity and the preservation of its natural capital (VLT, 2024).

6 Cross-cutting aspects and other strategic issues

Gabon's NDC 3.0 is not limited to sectoral actions: it also integrates cross-cutting dimensions that are essential to guarantee an inclusive, sustainable approach aligned with national priorities. These aspects help strengthen the relevance of climate commitments, foster their ownership by all stakeholders and maximise development co-benefits. They must be systematically taken into account from the design of projects in order to ensure their coherence and effectiveness. Depending on the scale of the needs identified and the evolution of the country's socio-economic context, these cross-cutting dimensions may also be the subject of specific projects, complementing and strengthening sectoral actions.

Private sector and economic engagement

In Gabon, the private sector is an indispensable actor for a successful climate transition. The State alone cannot mobilise all the necessary financing nor drive all the innovations required to reduce emissions and strengthen resilience. Local and international companies have a capacity for investment, research and technology dissemination that it is essential to mobilise. The development of public-private partnerships can thus accelerate the establishment of low-carbon energy infrastructure, innovative industrial solutions and sustainable agricultural value chains. The establishment of guarantee and risk-sharing mechanisms for private investment in adaptation projects will help stimulate private investment.

Furthermore, the involvement of small and medium-sized enterprises is crucial to anchoring the transition at local level and stimulating green employment. Gabon must therefore strengthen its incentive framework through fiscal, regulatory and financial measures that make low-carbon investment attractive. By placing the private sector at the heart of its climate action, the country opens the way to a structural transformation of its economy, oriented towards innovation and sustainability. A structured and permanent dialogue between public and private actors in order to explore and seize financing opportunities would be an asset. This permanent dialogue will also make it possible to capture and account for all the efforts deployed by the private sector within the country's overall effort to implement the NDCs.

Gender, social inclusion, youth, children and local communities

By 2050, Gabon aspires to equity and equality between the sexes as well as the training and empowerment of young people and women for a just and equitable transformation that fosters harmonious development and strengthens human capital.”

The effects of climate change in Gabon are not distributed equally: local populations, women, young people and children often suffer the most severe impacts. Local peoples, whose ways of life are closely linked to forest ecosystems, see their livelihoods, traditions and food security threatened by deforestation, land degradation and climate disruption. In rural areas, women play a key role in agriculture and natural resource management, but they still have limited access to finance, technology and training. Young people, who represent a growing share of the population, are both vulnerable to unemployment and bearers of considerable innovation potential.

Integrating gender equality, social inclusion and recognition of the rights and traditional knowledge of local peoples into climate policies is therefore not only a requirement of equity, but also constitutes a lever for economic and social transformation. By investing in education, vocational training, green entrepreneurship and the active participation of these groups in decision-making bodies, Gabon can strengthen the resilience of its communities and create the conditions for more inclusive and equitable development.

Just transition

The integration of the principle of climate justice into Gabon's public policies aims to prevent the worsening of social and territorial inequalities, while guaranteeing the active and inclusive participation of all stakeholders in strategic decisions related to the transition towards low-carbon development.

This transition, which is indispensable to meet climate imperatives, must be just, equitable and inclusive. It requires anticipation of socio-economic impacts, in particular for workers and communities whose livelihoods depend on carbon-intensive sectors. The aim is thus to reconcile the fight against climate change with national priorities in terms of poverty reduction, economic diversification and the promotion of decent and sustainable jobs.

By placing vulnerable populations, notably young people, women, rural and forest communities including indigenous peoples, at the heart of concerns, the just transition will help strengthen the country's collective resilience, consolidate economic and social stability, and align Gabon's development trajectory with the major regional and international frameworks such as the African Union's Agenda 2063, the United Nations 2030 Agenda for Sustainable Development, and the Paris Agreement on climate.

With this in mind, Gabon will put in place targeted support mechanisms, notably in terms of professional retraining, creation of green jobs and social protection for the populations most exposed to the economic and environmental transformations induced by the transition.

Loss and damage

As a coastal and forested country, Gabon is exposed to growing risks linked to climate change: sea-level rise, erosion of coastal areas, changes in rainfall patterns and intensification of extreme climate events. These phenomena result in real loss and damage, affecting both infrastructure and the livelihoods of local communities.

Gabon now integrates this dimension into its climate response, drawing on the international mechanisms established under the UNFCCC while exploring the establishment of a national fund to support affected communities. Such an approach aims not only to strengthen national solidarity in the face of disasters, but also to anticipate the irreversible impacts of climate on ecosystems and populations. This recognition of loss and damage as a strategic issue helps provide a fairer and more comprehensive response to climate challenges.

Peace and security

Climate change is not limited to environmental impacts: it also acts as a multiplier of social and security risks. In Gabon, growing pressure on natural resources - agricultural land, forests, fishing areas - can heighten tensions between communities, and even weaken social cohesion. Displacement linked to climate hazards could also increase the vulnerability of certain areas.

Climate action must therefore be conceived as an instrument of peace and stability. By promoting equitable and sustainable management of natural resources, strengthening community dialogue mechanisms and integrating conflict prevention into adaptation strategies, Gabon can reduce the risks of social tensions. Linking climate and security thus constitutes an essential axis for preserving national and regional stability, while fostering collective resilience.

Transboundary initiatives

The effects of climate change transcend borders and call for strengthened regional cooperation. Located at the heart of the Congo Basin, Gabon shares with its neighbours vital ecosystems whose preservation requires concerted and sustainable management.

The strengthening of transboundary initiatives aims to harmonise policies on forest management, biodiversity protection and energy infrastructure development. In this regard, the Integrated Development Programme for the Three-Border Zone (PDIZTF) – Gabon, Cameroon, Equatorial Guinea – supported by the Economic and Monetary Community of Central Africa (CEMAC) and other partners, constitutes an essential strategic framework for identifying and implementing priority projects in this shared area.

Other strategic issues

Finally, Gabon's climate trajectory cannot ignore the emerging issues that will take on growing importance in the coming decades.

The blue economy represents major potential, given the richness of the coastline and marine resources, and constitutes a lever for diversifying the national economy while generating sustainable jobs. Its development must nevertheless take place responsibly, preserving marine and coastal ecosystems, and integrating approaches to sustainable fisheries management, sustainable aquaculture and protection of marine biodiversity. In addition, Gabon's accession to the Freshwater Challenge reinforces its commitment to the restoration, protection and sustainable management of freshwater ecosystems, which are essential for water security, biodiversity and livelihoods. The integration of this commitment into future climate policies will make it possible to better link the objectives of ecological resilience, food security and sustainable development.

Digitalisation and digital technologies also offer unprecedented opportunities to optimise energy management, improve carbon traceability and strengthen the effectiveness of public policies.

Moreover, the valorisation of traditional knowledge and local practices constitutes an asset for community resilience, particularly in rural and forest areas for local communities. By integrating these emerging dimensions, Gabon demonstrates its capacity to anticipate global developments and to embed its climate action in a dynamic of innovation and continuous adaptation.

7.2 Financing mobilisation mechanisms

The implementation of the NDC3.0 requires the coordinated mobilisation of public and private, domestic and international financial resources. Gabon must not only strengthen its capacities to capture these resources, but also improve its attractiveness and competitiveness for climate investments. Financing sources can be structured at two levels: national and international.

7.2.1 National sources of financing

Domestic resources constitute an essential basis for initiating and sustaining climate actions. They can come from three main channels:

  • National and sectoral budget: progressive integration of climate into budget planning through green budgets and dedicated lines in the finance law. Particular attention could be given to emerging sectors such as the blue economy and integrated freshwater resources management, in order to anticipate their growing role in the green transition.
  • Local budgets: local authorities can play an increasing role through decentralised financing and resilient local investments (green urban infrastructure, waste management, coastal adaptation). Strengthening their financial and technical capacities will be essential to capture so-called « decentralised » financing.
  • National private finance: national banks and financial institutions, insurance companies, microfinance companies, and extractive, agro-industrial and forestry companies are key partners for filling public financing gaps. The development of incentive mechanisms (eco-taxes, carbon taxation, public guarantees, climate PPPs) will encourage their engagement

7.2.2 International sources of financing

Gabon, as a forest country and a major contributor to global carbon sequestration, has strong potential for access to international climate finance. Several channels are to be considered:

  • UNFCCC financial mechanisms: Global Environment Facility (GEF), Green Climate Fund (GCF), Adaptation Fund, Fund for responding to Loss and Damage. These mechanisms, notably the GCF, can also support the implementation of initiatives such as the Freshwater Challenge (Défi de l’eau douce), with which Gabon is already associated, in order to strengthen the restoration, protection and sustainable management of freshwater ecosystems and wetlands.
  • Multilateral financial institutions: World Bank, International Monetary Fund (IMF), African Development Bank (AfDB), Development Bank of Central African States (BDEAC), as well as regional mechanisms such as the African Climate Change Fund and the Sustainable Energy Fund for Africa (SEFA). These actors are increasingly financing initiatives related to the sustainable blue economy and hydrological resilience.
  • Bilateral cooperation: partnerships with the French Development Agency (AFD), German Cooperation (GIZ, KfW), JICA (Japanese cooperation), the European Union, as well as other bilateral donors.
  • Implementing partners: United Nations Development Programme (UNDP), FAO, etc.
  • International financial markets: access to sovereign green bonds, sustainable investment funds and international public-private partnerships for renewable energy, sustainable mobility or coastal protection projects.
  • Voluntary and innovative mechanisms: certification and sale of forest carbon credits (REDD+), financing by international foundations (Rockefeller, Bloomberg Philanthropies, Green Climate Fund for Cities), and development of South-South initiatives via the Congo Basin Climate Commission and the Africa Carbon Markets Initiative;
  • Philanthropic funds 4: financial resources from organisations, foundations, companies or individuals who support climate, environmental or scientific causes through donations, grants or partnerships. These funds support initiatives related to environmental protection, the reduction of greenhouse gas emissions, the preservation of biodiversity, or climate justice.

7.2.3 Prospects for the development of carbon markets in Gabon

Gabon has major opportunities to mobilise resources via carbon markets and innovative financing instruments. The UNFCCC certification, in 2022, of 187 million REDD+ credits (2010–2018) positions the country as a leader in the valorisation of forest ecosystem services (UNFCCC, 2022)5. These credits constitute a strategic reserve that can be mobilised on voluntary markets and under Article 6 of the Paris Agreement.

In 2021, Gabon became the first African country to receive a REDD+ results-based payment (17 million USD from CAFI, under an overall agreement of 150 million USD over 10 years with Norway). However, these resources remain modest in relation to the economic potential of Gabon's natural capital, estimated by the World Bank 6 at more than 75 billion USD over the last two decades.

Beyond CAFI, the country will be able to explore a diversified portfolio of carbon mechanisms:

  • Certification and sale of forest carbon credits (REDD+): these credits can be sold directly to international companies as part of their « net zero » commitments. Bringing them to market nevertheless requires a prudent strategy, in order to maximise value and guarantee an equitable redistribution of benefits to local communities and to sustainable forest management.
  • Domestic carbon markets: the development of a national market would make it possible to set an internal carbon price and to involve the energy, mining and industrial sectors in a reduction and offsetting approach. This system would help strengthen Gabon's sovereignty over its carbon assets and create a sustainable instrument for domestic climate finance.
  • Sub-regional carbon markets: building on the Congo Basin Climate Commission, Gabon could participate in a harmonised regional market, offering better visibility to investors and the possibility of collectively negotiating more favourable prices.
  • Africa Carbon Markets Initiative (ACMI)7: launched at COP27, it aims to mobilise 6 billion USD by 2030 and more than 120 billion USD by 2050. Gabon, as an African pioneer in carbon certification and a major forest country, is well positioned to capture a significant share of this financing, while benefiting from a platform for connecting with investors, foundations and multilateral institutions.
  • International voluntary markets: Gabon can also valorise its credits on global marketplaces (Verra, Gold Standard, etc.), where corporate demand to offset their irreducible emissions continues to grow. Prices, which vary according to credit quality, can reach 30–35 USD/tCO₂. A progressive and selective sales strategy would make it possible to generate potential revenues while avoiding market saturation.

Furthermore, Article 6 of the Paris Agreement governs the establishment of mechanisms such as

  • Bilateral cooperation (Article 6.2 of the Paris Agreement): Gabon can conclude agreements with other Parties to transfer mitigation outcomes in the form of Internationally Transferred Mitigation Outcomes (ITMOs). Such partnerships, already implemented between Switzerland and countries such as Peru, Thailand or Ghana, would pave the way for financing low-carbon projects in energy, forestry, transport or agriculture, while guaranteeing environmental integrity and avoiding double counting.
  • Multilateral market mechanism (Article 6.4 of the Paris Agreement): Gabon could develop projects certified under this mechanism supervised by the UNFCCC, notably in sustainable forest management, agroforestry, waste-to-energy and renewable energy. The carbon credits generated would be marketable on international markets, subject to the establishment of a Designated National Authority (DNA) responsible for approving projects and liaising with the UN system.
  • Non-market approaches (Article 6.8 of the Paris Agreement): the country can also take advantage of the opportunities offered by non-market cooperation: capacity building, technology transfer, South-South cooperation and technical support, which usefully complement the financial flows from carbon markets.

By capitalising on the experience acquired and consolidating its institutions, Gabon can transform its natural assets into sustainable financial levers, contributing at once to the achievement of its NDC 3.0, to the financing of the low-carbon transition, and to the socio-economic development of the country.

7.3 Increasing attractiveness and access to financial resources

Gabon currently has a System for Monitoring Climate Finance, other climate support and the SDGs impacted (SSFCODD). Presented in the BTR, it consists of:

  • National coordinator: oversees the entire system;
  • Two key officers: one responsible for monitoring financial flows, capacity building and technology transfer, and one responsible for monitoring the impacts of climate actions on the SDGs;
  • Interactions with operational actors: The national coordinator and the two key officers collaborate directly with the NDC mitigation and adaptation action monitoring system; sectoral administrations and local authorities, as well as technical and financial partners.

This framework ensures the collection, monitoring and communication of information on climate finance, while assessing its contribution to the sustainable development goals.

To strengthen this existing system, transform its climate ambitions into investments and increase its attractiveness to donors and investors, Gabon will undertake the implementation of a set of structural and operational reforms. The effective mobilisation of the financing required to achieve its NDC 3.0 depends on the creation of an enabling, transparent and incentivising ecosystem. This initiative aims to bridge the gap between strategic planning and access to capital by moving from theory to action through renewed governance and innovative financial instruments.

One of the flagship measures is the creation of the Gabon Climate Finance Platform (PGFC)8. Jointly steered by the CNC, the Ministry in charge of climate and the Ministry of Finance, this platform will serve as a one-stop shop to accelerate and simplify resource mobilisation. Such a country platform is essential to overcome the challenges of fragmented initiatives and weak coordination of interventions, while strengthening national ownership and the capacity to mobilise financing at scale. The PGFC will be regarded as sending a positive signal to the market by reducing risks for investors, increasing their confidence and encouraging long-term investment in the climate transition. This mechanism would make it possible to channel resources more effectively towards the priorities defined in the Nationally Determined Contributions (NDCs).

The PGCF should include certain important elements, notably: (i) the centralisation and coordination of resource mobilisation, (ii) securing investments through the strengthening of the legal and institutional framework, (iii) the development and structuring of bankable projects, (iv) steering the national climate finance strategy, (v) ensuring transparency and monitoring of financial flows, (vi) strengthening the capacities of administrations, local authorities and private actors, and (vii) the effective inclusion of local communities in the design and implementation of climate projects.

7.4 Technology transfer

The transfer and adaptation of low-carbon and resilient technologies constitute a major pillar of the implementation of the NDC3.0. For Gabon, this process is not limited to importing foreign innovations: it also involves ownership, adaptation to local realities and the progressive development of endogenous capacities in technological innovation.

Gabon conducted the technology needs assessment process (TNA, 2022) with the support of the CTCN, UNEP and UNIDO. This process made it possible to identify and prioritise needs in the Energy, Agriculture, Coastal (coastal zone) and waste sectors.

In the energy sector, the deployment of solar power plants, decentralised mini-grids and energy storage solutions is a priority to reduce dependence on fossil fuels and accelerate rural electrification. These technologies must be accompanied by maintenance arrangements and local training to ensure their sustainability.

In the agricultural field, the dissemination of agroecological techniques, water-efficient irrigation systems and improved climate-resilient varieties will help strengthen food security while reducing the carbon footprint of production. The integration of digital solutions (remote sensing, mobile decision-support applications) will also facilitate the sustainable management of agricultural resources.

In the waste sector, the introduction of energy recovery technologies, industrial composting and smart sorting systems will promote the reduction of methane emissions and contribute to the circular economy.

In the Coastal sector, the following were identified: beach nourishment, artificial dunes and dune rehabilitation, wetland restoration, longitudinal coastal defence structures, sea dikes, and the coastal setback margin.

In addition to these 4 priority sectors defined under the TNA, other sectors are at stake under the NDC, notably: (i) the transport sector, where the progressive introduction of electric and hybrid vehicles, the installation of charging stations and the adoption of intelligent traffic management systems will make it possible to reduce emissions related to urban mobility with solutions adapted to the Gabonese context; (ii) the forest sector and environmental monitoring, where strengthening existing systems by providing capacities and tools in remote sensing, digital mapping and environmental information systems will enable Gabon to better monitor GHG emissions and removals, improve forest governance and valorise its carbon credit potential.

The success of technology transfer depends on several levers: (i) international cooperation, notably through the technology transfer mechanisms of the UNFCCC, the Global Environment Facility and the Green Climate Fund; (ii) South-South and regional partnerships, capitalising on the experience of Congo Basin countries and other emerging countries in developing low-carbon solutions; (iii) scientific and academic cooperation, which will foster the development of local research and innovation centres capable of adapting technologies to Gabon's ecological and socio-economic conditions; (iv) the involvement of the private sector, in particular through public-private partnerships and support for green entrepreneurship.

By integrating technology transfer into a coherent national strategy, Gabon will be able not only to accelerate the implementation of its NDC3.0, but also to create economic opportunities and strengthen its technological autonomy in the long term.

7.6 MRV system and governance framework

7.6.1 Strengthened MRV framework

a) Existing MRV system in Gabon

Gabon has structured a national MRV system to meet the transparency requirements of the Paris Agreement and monitor its climate commitments. This system aims to monitor greenhouse gas emissions, assess the effectiveness of climate policies and facilitate access to international financing. Steered by the National Climate Council (CNC), it relies on institutional collaboration involving several key actors such as the Gabonese Agency for Space Studies and Observations (AGEOS) for satellite data, the National Agency of National Parks (ANPN) for biodiversity, and the Gabonese Agency for Green Economy Management (AGADEV) for the valorisation of carbon credits.

The Gabonese MRV system rests on three complementary pillars. The National GHG Inventory System (SNIGES) produces an annual inventory covering the forest, agriculture, energy and waste sectors. The NDC Mitigation and Adaptation Actions Monitoring System (SSAAA-CDN) ensures the monitoring of climate policies and measures. Finally, the Climate Finance Monitoring System (SSFCODD) tracks financial flows and carbon credits.

7.6.2 Governance framework

Gabon has a hierarchical and multisectoral climate governance framework (Figure 9). Placed under the highest authority of the State, it is designed to:

  • Decide strategically (CNC under presidential authority).
  • Coordinate at the highest governmental level (Prime Minister).
  • Implement in a cross-cutting manner across all relevant ministries and technical agencies.
  • Consult and involve local actors and civil society.

This model aims to integrate climate imperatives into all public policies and to honour the country's international commitments in a coherent manner. It is structured as follows:

a) High-Level Strategic and Policy Pillar

At the top is the National Climate Change Council (CNC), created in 2010 and placed directly under the authority of the President of the Republic. This council is the central body responsible for developing and guiding the national climate strategy.

Its main missions are:

  • Define the national policy to combat climate change.
  • Anticipate the vulnerabilities of the country and its populations.
  • Examine and evaluate climate-related projects.
  • Ensure compliance with Gabon's international commitments (e.g., UNFCCC, Paris Agreement).
  • Appoint negotiators for international climate conferences.
b) Operational and Coordination Structure

The diagram in Figure 9, taken from Gabon's Long-Term Climate Vision, illustrates that the CNC relies on an extensive network of actors to implement its policy:

  • A Management Committee, a Secretariat and a Sectoral Technical Committee for expert advice.

  • The Technical Ministries: Thirteen key ministries (Environment, Economy, Forests, Transport, etc.) are involved, reflecting a cross-cutting and sectoral approach in which each ministry integrates climate issues into its area of competence.
  • Youth Actors and Specialised Agencies ("Youth" List): Numerous directorates-general and agencies (Environment, Statistics, Planning, etc.) provide technical expertise and data and implement projects on the ground.

9 Conclusion

Gabon's third Nationally Determined Contribution (NDC 3.0) constitutes a strategic and operational milestone in the country's climate trajectory. More than a simple update, it embodies a raised ambition and an integrated vision, aligned with the Paris Agreement, the Long-Term Climate Vision 2050 and national development objectives. Building on the lessons learned from NDC 2.0 and the first Global Stocktake (GST), Gabon presents a robust, costed and transparent roadmap for the decade 2025-2035.

NDC 3.0 stands out for the scale and diversity of its project portfolio. With 35 sectoral mitigation measures (Energy, Agriculture, FOLU, Waste) and 60 adaptation measures covering key sectors including Water Resources, Health, the Coastal Zone, Biodiversity and Urban Development, i.e. 95 measures in total, NDC3.0 demonstrates a holistic approach to combating climate change. This ambition is reinforced by the systematic integration of fundamental cross-cutting aspects: gender and social inclusion, mobilisation and engagement of the private sector, just transition, loss and damage issues, and peace and security. This approach ensures that climate action is a lever for social cohesion and equitable sustainable development.

The implementation of this ambition represents an estimated sectoral financial need of 3 951,314 billion FCFA, of which 83% is for mitigation and 17% for adaptation. Faced with the gap between the financing already mobilised (92,133 billion FCFA) and the needs, Gabon proposes a bold and structuring response: the creation of the Gabon Climate Finance Platform (PGFC). Designed as a one-stop shop, this platform will be mandated to centralise resource mobilisation, structure bankable projects, coordinate the interventions of technical and financial partners and steer a national blended climate finance strategy, built around domestic resources, international financing and, above all, the strategic valorisation of carbon markets. It is jointly steered by the structures responsible for climate and finance. In support of this architecture, several cross-cutting and institutional measures are planned to ensure the success of NDC 3.0. All of these measures represent an estimated additional cost of 144,5 billion FCFA, bringing the overall total budget of NDC 3.0 to 4 095,464 billion FCFA over a period of 10 years.

Gabon, building on its historic certification of 187 million REDD+ credits and its status as a net sink country, places carbon markets at the heart of its financial strategy. NDC 3.0 plans to use the mechanisms of Article 6 of the Paris Agreement (bilateral and multilateral), voluntary markets, and the Africa Carbon Markets Initiative (ACMI) to generate sustainable revenues. This approach is not limited to the sale of credits; it includes the development of a domestic carbon market and participation in an integrated regional market, making Gabon's natural capital a strategic economic asset serving its transition.

The success of NDC 3.0 relies on a renewed governance framework and an effective Measurement, Reporting and Verification (MRV) system. Strengthening the capacities of the National Climate Council (CNC), creating a Climate Project Development Unit (UDPC) and professionalising technical teams are sine qua non conditions for transforming commitments into concrete actions. The MRV system, designed as a steering tool and no longer merely a reporting tool, will make it possible to track precisely the progress of projects, the effectiveness of financing and the contribution to the Sustainable Development Goals (SDGs), thereby ensuring full transparency and accountability.

The realisation of projects, particularly in renewable energy, climate-smart agriculture, resilient waste management and sustainable mobility, depends on the transfer of appropriate technologies and the strengthening of technical and institutional capacities. NDC 3.0 plans to draw on UNFCCC mechanisms, South-South partnerships and cooperation with the private sector to close this technology gap and foster the emergence of an innovative and competitive green economy.

Thus, Gabon's NDC 3.0 is much more than an international commitment document; it is a social and economic contract for a sober and resilient future for the country. It charts the path towards a structural transformation to a resilient, inclusive and low-carbon economy that preserves its exceptional natural capital while creating prosperity for all. Its successful implementation will require an unprecedented mobilisation of the State, the private sector, civil society and international partners. It will make Gabon a model of climate leadership in Africa and in the world, demonstrating that it is possible to reconcile environmental ambition and economic development for the benefit of present and future generations.
