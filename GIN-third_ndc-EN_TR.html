Summary

1. General Context

The Republic of Guinea, a signatory to the United Nations Framework Convention on Climate Change (UNFCCC) and the Paris Agreement, has been committed for several years to strengthening its actions in support of the climate. This NDC 3.0 (2026–2035) builds on the efforts undertaken in NDC 2.0 of 2021 and aims to raise the level of national ambition in mitigation and adaptation, in accordance with to the Sustainable Development Goals (SDGs), the Simandou 2040 Programme, and the National Strategy on the Climate Change (CCNS) and the Environmental Code which governs national policies environmental planning and governance.

The NDC 3.0 was revised ahead of the 30th Conference of the Parties (COP-30) in Belém, Brazil. results from a participatory and inclusive process coordinated by the Ministry of the Environment and the Sustainable Development (MEDD), with the support of the United Nations System, the World Bank, and the NDC Partnership and from all sectoral ministries, local authorities, civil society organizations, researchers, media and private sector.

2. Development and Validation Process

The process of revising and developing the NDC 3.0 took place over the period 2024–2025, according to a a multi-sectoral and inter-institutional approach. It was based on:

  • a technical evaluation of the implementation of the NDC 2.0, to be completed by the end of 2024,

  • a updating emissions inventories with the first Updated Biennial Report (BUR-1) of 2024 and the fourth National Inventory Report (NIR-4),

  • The launch of the NDC 3.0 review process during the first half of 2025,

  • a analysis of climate risks and vulnerabilities by sector,

  • of the national and regional consultations in the eight administrative regions of Guinea,

  • A national validation workshop , held in Conakry on October 16, 2025, chaired by Ms. Djami Diallo, Minister of the Environment and of Sustainable Development, and bringing together all public, private and community. The workshop approved the final version of the NDC 3.0 (subject to integrating the recommendations relating to sectoral prioritization, budgeting, youth, gender and the public communication in national languages).

In conclusion, compared to the previous NDC 2.0 dated 2021, the NDC 3.0 raises its ambition by both mitigation and adaptation: it expands the sectors covered, strengthens the instruments of funding and monitoring, and aligns with new national dynamics, including the Programme Simandou 2040. Regarding the mitigation component, the target excluding AFAT increases from 17% (CDN 2.0) to 20%. in a conditional scenario, thanks to updated inventories and improved data quality. The seven mitigation commitments are maintained, and even strengthened. Regarding adaptation, the three commitments of 2021 (water/ecosystems; coastal zones; agricultural-pastoral-fisheries systems) are enhanced and complemented by four new commitments in the areas of public health and water, sanitation and hygiene services, education and the promotion of families, children and youth with an intergenerational dimension, infrastructure and land-use planning, and climate-related mobilities, supported by an MRV adaptation and consolidated governance.

3. Economic and climatic context

Guinea possesses exceptional natural capital: 6.1 million hectares of forests, more than 350 km of coastline, considerable water reserves and significant energy potential (hydroelectricity, solar, biomass). Thus, Guinea's Gross Domestic Product (GDP) growth reached 5.7% in 2024, driven by both the mining and non-mining sectors, and could reach a level close to two medium-term figures, due to the start of iron production at Simandou. The expected growth of Iron ore exports by 2026 should accelerate GDP growth, but they also risk to accentuate the existing challenges in terms of economic inclusion.

The country also remains vulnerable to climate variability, localized droughts, recurring floods, land degradation, and coastal erosion.

The impacts of climate change are already affecting agricultural and fisheries production, public health, infrastructure, as well as the livelihoods of rural and coastal populations, sometimes causing internal migration movements.

5. Adaptation Objectives

The commitments made in the 2021 NDC 2.0 are reinforced and new commitments are also made. The three existing commitments whose ambitions have been increased are:

  1. protection and Sustainable management of water resources and natural ecosystems ,

  2. the protection, conservation and management of ecosystems , the revitalization of economic activities and the strengthening the resilience of the populations in its area coastal ,

  3. the strengthening of the resilience of agricultural, pastoral and fisheries systems and supporting the adaptation efforts of rural communities.

    The CDN 3.0 therefore retains and strengthens these three commitments, while adding four new ones commitments, in response to the rise in social and infrastructural vulnerabilities:

  4. Towards resilient and accessible health for the well-being of populations: strengthening systems health, the integration of climate-related epidemiological surveillance, and improved coverage sanitation, and better access to water and sanitation (WASH).

  5. Education for all, equality for all, resilience for every family: the integration of education climate change in school curricula, the construction of resilient schools, the promotion of participation of girls and women, and strengthening social safety nets for households vulnerable.

  6. Rethinking the territory, reinventing infrastructure, re-enchanting housing: the design resilient infrastructure, the integration of climate considerations into construction standards and in urban planning, the promotion of green buildings and solar microgrids.

  7. Climate migration: planning, protecting, welcoming: recognizing the link between climate change climate change, population displacement and human security; the inclusion of preventive measures and support for communities of origin and host communities.

These seven commitments now form the strategic framework for national adaptation, aligned with the National Adaptation Programme (NAP) and Global Adaptation Goals.

6. Gender and social inclusion

The NDC 3.0 of the Republic of Guinea reinforces the inclusive and participatory approach initiated in NDC 2.0, by placing gender equality, youth, people with disabilities and vulnerable populations at the heart of the climate transition. She acknowledges that women, young people and children suffer in a way disproportionate impacts of climate change, but also play a key role in implementation sustainable local solutions. The development process therefore incorporated vulnerability analysis. differentiated according to sex, age and socio-economic status, in order to design measures equitable and gender-sensitive adaptation and mitigation.

On this basis, Guinea commits to operationalizing a National Gender and Climate Strategy, structured around strengthening the capacities of rural women, promoting women's green entrepreneurship and the systematic integration of gender into sectoral policies (agriculture, health, education, energy, waste management and coastal areas). Training programs and access to financing inclusive climate initiatives will be developed for young people and women, while campaigns for Raising awareness in national languages will help promote the active participation of communities, child protection and social cohesion. This approach reflects Guinea's commitment to making the climate justice and social inclusion a cross-cutting pillar of the implementation of its NDC 3.0.

7. Means of implementation

The success of the CDN 3.0 rests on four essential levers:

Governance and institutional coordination: An Interministerial Climate Committee will be consolidated under the The Ministry of Environment and Sustainable Development (MEDD) oversees this area, with sectoral focal points for mitigation, adaptation, and gender. Local coordination will be ensured through the Regional Environmental Inspectorates and the Committees of local development.

Climate finance and climate-sensitive budgeting: Guinea plans to implement a [system/program] by 2026 Investment and operationalization plan for its CDN 3.0, integrated into the Annual Operational Plans (AOPs) and the state budgetary framework, in order to monitor climate spending, distinguishing between objectives unconditional objectives (funded nationally) versus conditional objectives (funded internationally). This In doing so, the country hopes to mobilize innovative resources.

MRV System and Transparency An integrated Measurement, Reporting and Verification (MRV) system, common to the mitigation sectors and adaptation will be operationalized with a dedicated unit for the CDN within the National Directorate of Pollution, Nuisances and Climate Change (DNPNCC) of the MEDD. It will be based on a dashboard nationally, harmonized indicators and annual monitoring reports open to the public. In terms of In coordination, Guinea has already launched the project entitled " Partnership Action Fund » (PAF) dealing with the establishment of an institutional, legal, and regulatory framework plus strengthening of capacities for the implementation of the NDC.

Participation, inclusion and communication : The implementation of the CDN 3.0 will be accompanied by a communication strategy with dissemination in multiple languages national channels via community radio, public media and diplomatic relays, in order to ensure the transparency, citizen ownership and international mobilization.

8. Support Needs and Partnerships

The overall cost of implementing NDC 3.0 is estimated at approximately USD 17.7 billion over the period 2026–2035, including USD 9.3 billion for mitigation and USD 8.4 billion for adaptation. These figures must be refined during the preparation of the CDN 3.0 Investment and Operationalization Plan in 2026. The Government of Guinea will mobilize national resources to achieve its objectives unconditional, and seeks the support of bilateral and multilateral partners, the Green Climate Fund for the Climate (GF), the Global Environment Facility (GEF) and international cooperation mechanisms at title of Article 6 of the Paris Agreement, to achieve the conditional objectives.

9. Conclusion

The NDC 3.0 of the Republic of Guinea reflects a renewed political will and a vision of green growth. inclusive and resilient. It marks a substantial leap in ambition compared to the previous National Development Contract, in expanding the sectors covered, deepening adaptation commitments and strengthening the financing and monitoring instruments.

Driven by national leadership, community mobilization and the support of its technical partners and In this way, Guinea affirms its determination to contribute to the global effort to limit global warming. planetary warming of 1.5°C or even 2°C, while guaranteeing sustainable development and the prosperity of its population.

a. National circumstances (geography, climate, economy, sustainable development, eradication of poverty).;

The Republic of Guinea is located in West Africa, covering an area of 245,857 km², with a The country has a 350 km coastline on the Atlantic Ocean. It is characterized by a great diversity geographically structured around four distinct natural regions: Maritime Guinea (Lower Guinea), coastal and coastal plains zone; Middle Guinea (Fouta Djallon Massif); the Upper Guinea, an area of savannas and plains; and Forest Guinea, a region of dense forests and high plateaus in the southeast. Thus, the Fouta Djallon massif, notably known as the water tower of West Africa, is currently the subject of a nomination process for inscription on the World Heritage List UNESCO, recognition of its exceptional hydrological and ecological importance for the entire region.

This geographical diversity is reflected in significant variations in altitude (sea level) up to 1,752 m at Mount Nimba), of relief, vegetation cover and hydrological systems. Guinea It is home to the sources of several major West African rivers, including the Niger, the Senegal, the Gambia and numerous coastal basins (Konkouré, Koliba-Corubal, Mano), giving the country a role strategic regional approach to water resources.

The Guinean coastline includes critical coastal ecosystems, including approximately 270,000 hectares of mangroves, erosion control barriers, estuaries, and areas wetlands of international importance. These ecosystems provide essential services (coastal protection, fish nurseries, carbon sequestration) but are subject to anthropogenic pressures and increasing climates.

Guinea has a tropical climate with two main seasons: a rainy season (April-October) and a dry season (November-March), with significant rainfall gradients regional. Maritime Guinea records average annual rainfall of 2,958 mm (Conakry), while Upper Guinea receives approximately 1,472 mm/year (Kankan). This spatial variability influences directly impact agricultural systems, water resources, and population distribution.

Recent climate observations and future projections (IPCC CMIP6/AR6) confirm a trend towards intensification and increased frequency of climatic hazards: rise in average temperatures, increased variability in rainfall with episodes of prolonged drought and more intense extreme rainfall, delays and early cessation of rainy seasons, waves of increasingly frequent heat waves, and a rise in sea level estimated at +0.3 to +0.5 m by 2050.

In 2024, floods affected 132,496 people across the country, causing considerable damage. to infrastructure, housing, crops, and essential services. New episodes major events that occurred in the summer of 2025, particularly around Conakry, confirmed the amplification of the The country's structural vulnerability to climate extremes. Coastal areas are experiencing erosion. accelerated erosion of coastal barriers, marine submersion, and saltwater intrusion threaten coastal areas agricultural areas, port infrastructure and densely populated coastal neighborhoods.

Recurring droughts in Upper Guinea and parts of Middle Guinea are disrupting the Agricultural calendars reduce yields and exacerbate conflicts over resource use water stress during the dry season is described as "high" by sector stakeholders, with direct implications for hydroelectric production (76% of the national energy mix), agriculture irrigated, drinking water supply and public health.

With an estimated population of approximately 14 million inhabitants (2024-2025 projection) and a rate of With a population growth rate of approximately 2.8% per year, Guinea is experiencing sustained demographic growth. which amplifies the pressure on natural resources and basic services. The population is predominantly young (approximately 42% are under 15 years old), with rapid, concentrated urbanization mainly in Conakry, which is home to nearly 2 million inhabitants and continues to attract flows internal migration linked to climate shocks and the search for economic opportunities.

Approximately 52% of the working population depends on agriculture, livestock farming and fishing, sectors highly vulnerable to climate variability. Irrigation remains limited (approximately 32,000 ha developed) (on a potential of 360,000 ha), exposing the majority of agricultural production to rainfall hazards. Multidimensional poverty affects a significant proportion of the population, particularly in rural areas where access to basic services (drinking water, sanitation, electricity, health, education) remains insufficient.

Gender inequalities persist, with women bearing a disproportionate burden of tasks domestic tasks (fetching water, collecting firewood), childcare and elderly care, as well as that there is increased vulnerability to gender-based violence in crisis situations. Women represent a significant proportion of the agricultural workforce but have limited access to the land, credit, inputs, and improved technologies. Children, particularly girls, experience recurring school disruptions during climate crises (floods, displacements of populations), with risks of permanent school dropout and early marriages.

The Guinean economy rests on three main pillars: the mining sector (bauxite, gold, iron), Agriculture and services. The extractive sector, particularly bauxite production, including Guinea holds the world's largest reserves (approximately 7.4 billion tonnes), generates a significant share export revenues and budgetary income. The development of the Simandou (iron) project and The expansion of gold mining constitutes a major axis of economic transformation, with significant investments in infrastructure (rail, ports, energy).

Thus, Guinea's Gross Domestic Product (GDP) growth reached 5.7% in 2024, bringing it to times by the mining and non-mining sectors, and could reach a level close to double digits at medium term, due to the entry into production of the Simandou iron ore.

However, this economy remains vulnerable to external shocks (volatility of commodity prices). first) and internal factors (climatic hazards, energy instability). The energy system, dominated by Hydropower (Kaléta, Souapiti, and soon Amaria dams, etc.) is particularly sensitive to rainfall variability, siltation of reservoirs and severe low water levels, with cascading effects on economic activity, social services and industrial development.

Agriculture, although contributing less to GDP in monetary terms, employs the majority of the population and ensures food security. Priority sectors include rice (staple food) (of which Guinea aims for self-sufficiency), maize, cassava, tubers, cash crops (coffee, cocoa, oil palm, cashew), fruits (mango, pineapple, citrus fruits) and vegetable crops. Livestock farming, practiced in extensive and transhumant systems, also plays an economic and cultural role important, but faces increasing challenges related to the scarcity of pastures and conflicts agro-pastoral problems exacerbated by the climate.

Guinea has committed to sustainable development through several strategic frameworks national. The Interim Reference Programme (IRP 2022-2025) is the main instrument of economic and social planning for the transition, with priorities including stabilization macroeconomics, governance, basic infrastructure and essential social services.

The National Climate Change Strategy (NCCS 2019-2030) sets out the vision and strategic priorities and the costed action plan to integrate adaptation and mitigation into policies development. The Blue Economy Development Strategy in Guinea aims to sustainably enhance Guinea's maritime and coastal resources for the country's economic and social development. The National Strategy for Disaster Risk Reduction (SNRRC 2024-2030) defines the architecture institutional and operational for the management of climate and non-climate risks.

The National Gender Policy III (2025-2029) mandates the cross-cutting integration of gender equality. in all policies and projects, with a particular focus on climate adaptation.

Eradicating poverty is a cross-cutting priority for Guinea, consistent with Sustainable Development Goal 1 (SDG 1). Efforts focus on improving access to basic services (water, sanitation, electricity, health, education), social protection (transfers) monetary aid, school canteens, health insurance), the creation of decent jobs and economic inclusion vulnerable groups, particularly through the National Agency for Economic and Social Inclusion (ANIES) and the Unified Social Register.

However, recurring climate shocks (floods, droughts, coastal erosion) compromise the gains from development destroy the assets of poor households, disrupt livelihoods and increase structural vulnerability. Economic losses linked to climate disasters represent a growing tax burden for the state and divert resources from investments productive and social.

The Republic of Guinea faces a complex development challenge: accelerating transformation economic development, improving living conditions for the population, reducing poverty and inequality, all by building sustainable resilience to climate change. This equation requires integrating systematically integrate climate considerations into all sectoral policies and invest massively in adaptation to protect vulnerable populations, secure infrastructure critical issues, preserving ecosystems and ensuring the sustainability of development trajectories.

Guinea's NDC 3.0 reflects this dual ambition: to contribute to global mitigation efforts in accordance with common but differentiated responsibilities and respective capabilities, while prioritizing adaptation as an imperative for survival, human security, and sustainable development. The commitments made are rooted in national realities and aim to reconcile the climate emergency, Social justice, gender equity, and inclusive economic transformation.

      Coastal, maritime & port areas sector

      The Guinean coastal fringe (Conakry, Forécariah, Boffa, Kamsar) is experiencing a rise in sea level marine erosion, coastal erosion and saltwater intrusion, while waterproofing Urban development and inadequate drainage amplify stormwater flooding. The risks weigh heavily on the ports/industries, dense coastal districts and "agro-mangrovian" perimeters, with health, economic, and social impacts. The priorities combine solutions based on the nature (mangroves/dunes/buffer zones), targeted grey protections for critical assets, early warning of flooding and emergency preparedness, as well as spatial planning legally binding (2030/2050 maps, zoning of unbuildable/setback areas) supported by detailed data (altimetry/bathymetry, tide gauges, piezometric-salinity monitoring).

(f) Adaptation actions and/or economic diversification plans generating co-benefits mitigation.

Integrated adaptation-mitigation approach Guinea plans to systematically integrate the search for mitigation co-benefits: all Priority adaptation measures of the NDC generate emission reductions and a strengthening of the carbon sequestration, with an estimated cumulative effect of between 1.5 and 2.5 MtCO₂e over the period 2026-2035, while diversifying the economy, creating green jobs and strengthening the resilience of vulnerable communities. These actions combine nature-based solutions and transformation of low-carbon production systems and infrastructure, thereby maximizing synergies between adaptation and mitigation.

Ecosystem restoration and forest carbon. The program to restore 25,000 ha of forests and watersheds (Fouta Djallon / Guinean ridge, etc.) and 5,000 ha of coastal mangroves constitute significant mitigation co-benefits. These restored ecosystems simultaneously regulate flows They protect against erosion and coastal flooding, and support artisanal fisheries. and generate revenue through the sale of non-timber forest products and carbon credits Blue. Agroforestry and climate-smart agriculture. Integrating trees into systems of agriculture (10,000 farms covering approximately 20,000 ha by 2030, 25,000 farms and more than 50 000 ha by 2035) will improve soil fertility, diversify incomes and reduce pressure on natural forests. The dissemination of climate-smart agricultural practices (crop rotation, composting, mulching, controlled deficit irrigation) among 100,000 producers (2030) then 200,000 (2035), coupled with the adoption of tolerant varieties, will reduce deforestation through agricultural clearing and improves yields by 15-30%. Improved fire management will reduce emissions by decreasing areas burned by late-season fires. These measures will create rural jobs and improve safety eating.

Energy diversification and low-carbon infrastructure. The deployment of solar microgrids with storage in remote locations and for critical infrastructure (health, education, water), coupled with the gradual introduction of the first electric vehicles in Conakry (2030), will reduce dependence on fossil fuels. Securing hydroelectric production (dredging) of 5-10 Mm³ of sediments, reforestation of upstream catchments) will maintain an availability of approximately 95% of this renewable source represents 76% of the national mix, avoiding increased recourse to Thermal power plants. The construction of schools and public buildings according to ventilation standards Passive and energy-efficient solutions will reduce the need for mechanical cooling. These investments will generate jobs in renewable energy, sustainable construction and clean transport.

Economic diversification and sustainable agribusiness. The development of four integrated agropoles (Upper Guinea, Forest Guinea, Middle Guinea, Maritime Guinea) structured around cold chains Solar power, transformation units and climate-resilient storage infrastructure will transform the agricultural sectors while reducing post-harvest losses from 30% to 10-15%. This reduction is equivalent to avoid production waste and associated emissions (production, transport, decomposition). The integration of mining and agriculture (rehabilitation of mining sites, use of treated water, infrastructure) shared) and the development of 200-500 agri-industrial SMEs trained in resilient management will create between 5,000 and 15,000 green jobs (2030-2035) in low-carbon sectors. This will diversify the economy beyond mining and reduce the country's structural vulnerability.

Climate governance and innovative financing. Guinea will mobilize carbon financing mechanisms (voluntary market and Article 6 of the Paris Agreement) to generate additional revenue from the Restoration of mangroves (blue carbon), reforestation and agroforestry, with certification according to international standards.
