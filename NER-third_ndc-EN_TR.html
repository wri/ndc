PREFACE

The Republic of Niger, under the impetus of its highest authorities, reaffirms its determination to promote economic and social development founded on national sovereignty, the security of the population and the sustainable development of its natural resources. In a context marked by persistent security, climate and socio-economic challenges, the country positions climate action as a strategic lever serving national resilience and the well-being of citizens.

Highly exposed to the adverse effects of climate change, which affect key sectors such as agriculture, livestock, water resources and health, Niger intends to raise its climate ambition by strengthening the resilience of production systems and communities and by reducing the drivers of fragility.

Through this third generation of its Nationally Determined Contribution (CDN 3.0), Niger reaffirms its commitment to contribute to the global effort to combat climate change as defined in the Paris Agreement on Climate. This commitment is aligned with the national priorities set out in the Programme for the Refoundation of the Republic, while respecting the principle of common but differentiated responsibilities in accordance with the founding principles of the United Nations Framework Convention on Climate Change (CCNUCC).

Under its CDN 3.0 (2025-2035), Niger plans to implement ambitious adaptation and mitigation actions aligned with its sectoral policies, development strategies and the international commitments it has entered into. This implementation is based on an integrated, inclusive and progressive approach, mobilising all national stakeholders as well as the support of the international community through climate finance, technology transfer and capacity-building. It constitutes a strategic framework aimed at guiding investments, tracking progress and strengthening transparency, consistent with the requirements of the enhanced transparency framework of the Paris Agreement on Climate.

This new CDN is thus intended to be a unifying framework for climate action in Niger, a tool to support decision-making and the mobilisation of adequate resources. That is why I urge all stakeholders to take ownership of it and to commit fully to its effective and efficient implementation for the benefit of our communities and ecosystems.

Colonel MAIZAMA ABDOULAYE

Minister of Environment, Water and Sanitation

EXECUTIVE SUMMARY

The Republic of Niger submits its third-generation Nationally Determined Contribution (CDN 3.0) for the period 2025-2035, in accordance with the Paris Agreement (AP) on Climate, reaffirming its commitment to contribute to the global effort to combat climate change while pursuing its objectives of sustainable development and poverty reduction.

As a Sahelian country with low Greenhouse Gas emissions but high vulnerability to the impacts of climate change, Niger faces major challenges affecting its key sectors such as agriculture, livestock, forestry, water resources, health and infrastructure/housing. In this context, the CDN 3.0 adopts an integrated approach combining adaptation, mitigation, loss and damage, and the strengthening of means of implementation.

Niger’s vision for 2035 is to build a resilient, inclusive and prosperous society, able to turn climate challenges into opportunities for sustainable development, along a low-carbon development pathway.

The CDN 3.0 is based on an inclusive and participatory approach and, as such, constitutes a strategic framework for guiding public and private investment, strengthening transparency and mobilising the resources needed for a transition to a resilient, low-carbon economy.

The formulation of the CDN 3.0 took place in two (2) phases: preparation for the revision of the CDN 2.0 and the raising of ambition. The preparatory phase built on the achievements of the implementation of the CDN 2.0 and established the revision timetable on the basis of a situational analysis and a roadmap. Ambition was raised through thematic studies, the integration of cross-cutting dimensions such as gender, youth and children, migration, peace and security, green jobs and just transition, as well as the territorialisation of climate actions, modelling in the CDN sectors and stakeholder consultations. On this basis, Niger’s new ambitions are as follows:

  • In terms of mitigation, Niger intends to reduce its greenhouse gas emissions by 39,7 % (6 805,38 ktCO2e) by 2030 and 51,2 % (11 414,03 ktCO2e) by 2035 relative to the baseline scenario, with an unconditional component of 13 %, which marks significant progress compared with the CDN 2.0 (target of 35 % by 2030). This target will be achieved through the implementation of Policies, Actions and Measures in the energy, agriculture, livestock, forestry and waste sectors;
  • With regard to adaptation, which is a national priority, the CDN 3.0 provides for a set of sectoral measures aimed at strengthening the resilience of populations and ecosystems, particularly in the sectors of agriculture, livestock, forestry, wetlands, water resources/EHA, infrastructure/housing/transport, waste, health, education and the cross-cutting sector. These measures are based essentially on a coherent strategic framework structured around the National Adaptation Plan (PNA) and the National Strategy for Adaptation to Climate Change in the Agricultural Sector (SPN2A).

Furthermore, the main levers of action of the CDN 3.0 are: strengthening public policies and the regulatory framework, developing and modernising infrastructure, mobilising climate finance and investment, technology transfer and innovation, strengthening institutional and technical capacities, promoting sustainable practices and strengthening community resilience, sustainable management of natural resources and ecosystems, and finally creating green jobs and sustainable economic development.

In addition, the CDN 3.0 innovates by linking climate commitments with public finances through Climate-Sensitive Budget Tagging (MBSC) and territorialisation, and by mobilising the carbon market mechanisms of Article 6 of the Paris Agreement and the fund for responding to loss and damage as additional financing levers. Implementation of the CDN 3.0 requires total financing estimated at USD 16,94 billion, of which approximately 55,61 % is dedicated to adaptation and 44,39 % to mitigation.

INTRODUCTION

The Government of Niger presents its Nationally Determined Contribution (CDN) for the period 2025-2035, in accordance with Articles 4.2 and 4.11 of the Paris Agreement, paragraph 24 of decision 1/CP.21 and other relevant provisions of the Agreement.

In the context of implementing the United Nations Framework Convention on Climate Change (CCNUCC) and the Paris Agreement on climate, Niger has set itself the objective of contributing to the reduction of global greenhouse gas emissions while pursuing low-carbon socio-economic development that is resilient to the adverse effects of climate change. This approach is fully in line with the 2030 Agenda for Sustainable Development, in particular Sustainable Development Goal No. 13 on combating climate change and its impacts. Accordingly, the country has submitted four National Communications (CNI-2000, SCN-2009, TCN-2016, QCN-2024), a Biennial Update Report (RBA-2022), a Biennial Transparency Report (RBT-2024), an adaptation communication (AdCom-2025), an Intended Nationally Determined Contribution serving as its first CDN (2015) and a revised CDN (2021). From one contribution to the next, the national mitigation ambition has been progressively strengthened, rising from 29,8 % (first CDN) to 35 % (CDN 2.0, 2021), to reach 51,2 % by 2035 in the present CDN 3.0.

The review of the implementation of the CDN 2.0 showed a reduction of (-) 37 778,65 ktCO2e in the AFAT sector over the period 2021-2025 against a target of (-) 204 198,08 ktCO2e by 2030, i.e. 18,50 %. Reductions in the Energy sector amounted to around 2 999,53 ktCO2e over the same period against a target of 5 324,32 ktCO2e, i.e. 56,34 %. Notable progress has been recorded in financial mobilisation and international cooperation. However, this review highlights the need to strengthen national ownership of the CDN at all levels and the participation of the private and financial sector and of research and training institutions. It also underlines the need to consolidate institutional capacities and to better integrate cross-cutting dimensions, notably gender, children and youth, social inclusion, just transition and cross-sectoral nexuses.

Also, over the period 2021-2025, 69 climate projects and programmes were implemented, representing USD 5,442 billion against a total estimated need of USD 9,908 billion for the period 2021- 2030, i.e. 54,92% (MEH/A, 2025a).

Niger has committed to progressively strengthening its climate ambition through its CDNs. It is in this perspective that the country is developing its third CDN, taking into account the conclusions of the first Global Stocktake adopted at COP 28 in 2023. Indeed, the 1st global stocktake calls on Parties to accelerate the global energy transition, notably by contributing to the collective goal of tripling global renewable energy capacity and doubling the global average annual rate of energy efficiency improvements by 2030. It advocates the rational use of fossil fuels, in order to keep within reach the goal of limiting global warming to 1,5 °C.

The CDN 3.0 covers the AFAT, Energy and Waste sectors and the following greenhouse gases: CO2, CH4, N2O. It follows a pathway compatible with the 1,5 °C goal, consistent with the National Low-Carbon Strategy (SNBC-2050).

The process of developing this CDN 3.0 aims to consolidate an enhanced, integrated ambition aligned with national sustainable development priorities. The approach adopted incorporates a cross-cutting dimension based on four nexuses, selected because of their direct relevance to the national context and circumstances of Niger, which is particularly vulnerable to the effects of climate change and faces interlinked challenges of security, population mobility, demographic pressure and access to basic services. These four nexuses are: i) Climate & Peace & Security; ii) Migration & Environment & Climate Change; iii) Gender & Health & Climate Change; and iv) Waste & Green Jobs & Just Transition. It also takes into account the Water, Hygiene and Sanitation (EHA) sector, the involvement of children and youth and of the private sector, and the Carbon Market based on Niger’s commitments relating to Article 6 of the AP. This approach aims to ensure greater cross-sectoral coherence, maximise co-benefits and place climate action within an integrated perspective of sustainable development and stability.

The new ambitions of the CDN 3.0 arising from this iterative, participatory and inclusive process concern the areas of adaptation and mitigation as well as the means of implementation. Beyond mitigation, this CDN 3.0 places adaptation at the heart of Niger’s climate response, through a sectoral portfolio covering Agriculture, Livestock, Forestry, Wetlands, Water Resources, Fisheries Resources, Road Infrastructure and Buildings, Health, Education, Energy and Waste. Adaptation thus constitutes an essential lever for protecting development gains and strengthening the resilience of socio-economic and ecological systems.

The CDN 3.0 is aligned with the Programme for the Refoundation of the Republic (PRR) and is consistent with the National Low-Carbon Strategy (SNBC, 2050), the National Adaptation Plan (PNA, 2035), the Strategic Framework for Sustainable Land Management (CS-GDT, 2014-2029), the National Energy Compact (2030), the National Electricity Access Strategy (SNAE, 2035), the National Transport Strategy (SNT, 2025) and the National Strategy for Adaptation to Climate Change in the Agricultural Sector (SPN2A, 2035), with a view to initiating a transition to a low-carbon economy that is more resilient to the adverse impacts of climate change.

Implementation of the CDN 3.0 requires the mobilisation of national mechanisms and the financial contribution of Technical and Financial Partners (PTF). Its monitoring is strengthened through the enhanced transparency framework, and also through Climate-Sensitive Budget Tagging (MBSC) in the following sectors: Energy, Agriculture, Water, Livestock, Environment, Transport, Urban Planning, Population.

Since 2019, like other countries that are Parties to the CCNUCC, Niger has been revising and strengthening its CDN. This revision is conducted under the leadership of the Ministry in charge of the Environment in partnership with the entities working in the priority sectors of the CDN. These are the sectoral ministries, research and training institutions and Civil Society Organisations brought together within the National Technical Committee and the Policy Steering Committee for the implementation of the CDN.

5. Assumptions and methodological approaches, including those for estimating and accounting for anthropogenic greenhouse gas emissions and, as appropriate, removals

a. Assumptions and methodological approaches used for accounting for anthropogenic greenhouse gas emissions and removals corresponding to the Party's nationally determined contribution, consistent with paragraph 31 of decision 1/CP.21 and accounting guidance adopted by the CMA

Accounting for anthropogenic GES emissions and removals is based on the use of the 2006 IPCC Guidelines and the 2019 Refinement.

The methodological approaches applied include the carbon stock change and gain-loss methods for the land sector, as well as the use of IPCC default emission factors supplemented, where available, by country-specific data.

The assumptions adopted are based on national activity data, credible international databases such as those of FAO, as well as extrapolation and interpolation methods to fill data gaps.

b. Assumptions and methodological approaches used for accounting for the implementation of policies and measures or strategies in the nationally determined contribution

To account for the implementation of the policies, actions and measures of CDN2.0, Niger adopted a sectoral assessment approach based on the same tools as those used to define the CDN targets, in order to avoid the biases and uncertainties associated with a methodological change. Thus, in the AFAT sector, the impacts of the PAMs were estimated using the EX- ACT tool, while in the Energy sector the assessment was carried out with the GACMO tool. The analysis is based on the principles of transparency, completeness, accuracy and comparability, and relies on: the description of the sector, the definition of the 2021-2024 assessment time frame corresponding to the first five-year phase of CDN implementation, the identification of the data-holding bodies, the collection of information on the basis of Excel spreadsheets focused on the physical achievements of the measures, as well as QA/QC procedures. For the AFAT sector, the assessment also includes calibration of the data according to the PRG used by the tool and analysis of the carbon balance associated with the observed achievements. The assumptions adopted include that the PAMs cover the entire national territory with impacts varying by zone, that only the physical achievements observed over 2021-2024 are taken into account, and that the effective implementation of the PAMs contributes to meeting the CDN's reduction and/or removal commitments. Finally, to avoid double counting, each measure was linked to specific sectoral targets and indicators, then assessed separately before aggregation at the level of the sector concerned.

c. If applicable, information on how the Party will take into account existing methods and guidance under the Convention to account for anthropogenic emissions and removals, in accordance with Article 4, paragraph 14, of the Paris Agreement, as appropriate

Niger takes into account existing methods and guidance under the Convention for accounting for anthropogenic GES emissions and removals by relying on the 2006 IPCC Guidelines, supplemented by the 2019 Refinement, used in the preparation of its national GES inventory. In addition, the principles of transparency, consistency, comparability and completeness are applied in the production of data and the tracking of progress.

d. IPCC methodologies and metrics used for estimating anthropogenic greenhouse gas emissions and removals

The emission calculations are based on the use of the GIEC methodological equations applicable to the different sectors. The metrics, in particular the Global Warming Potentials (PRG) used, come from the GIEC Fifth Assessment Report, and the emission factors used are also mainly GIEC defaults, owing to the limited availability of country-specific data, and are applied consistently across the entire time series. Activity data come from national sources supplemented, where appropriate, by international databases, and are integrated into a quality assurance and quality control framework ensuring the transparency, consistency, comparability and completeness of the estimates.

e. Sector-, category- or activity-specific assumptions, methodologies and approaches consistent with IPCC guidance, as appropriate, including, as applicable:

i. Approach to addressing emissions and subsequent removals from natural disturbances on managed lands

Niger does not implement a specific approach to isolate emissions and subsequent removals due to natural disturbances on managed lands. These disturbances, mainly bush fires and overgrazing, are integrated into the overall estimates for the UTCATF sector, in accordance with the IPCC Guidelines. The immediate and subsequent effects are thus reflected in the carbon stock changes and the emission/removal fluxes estimated over the time series.

ii. Approach used to account for emissions and removals from harvested wood products

In Niger, harvested wood products (PLR) are estimated from the data available in the FAO database, extrapolated over the entire time series. As the country does not export PLR, the associated carbon fluxes come mainly from imported wood and the management of agricultural residues. Owing to the limited availability of national data, a simplified approach consistent with the IPCC Guidelines (Tier 1) was applied.

iii. Approach used to address the effects of age-class structure in forests

In the national GES inventory, the effects of the age-class structure of forests are not explicitly estimated. II. NATIONAL PRIORITIES

Niger's vision on climate change:

“By 2050, Niger aims to build a climate-resilient, inclusive and prosperous society, capable of transforming climate challenges into sustainable development opportunities through a low-carbon development pathway”.

Niger's national development priorities, as defined in the Programme for the Refoundation of the Republic (PRR, 2025-2029), place particular emphasis on the development of production bases in connection with climate resilience and economic sovereignty. This strategic orientation, which concerns the majority of public investment, aims to strengthen key sectors, in particular agriculture, livestock and infrastructure, which are highly exposed to the effects of climate change. In this framework, actions focus on promoting resilient agriculture, sustainable management of natural resources, the development of renewable energy and the improvement of productive infrastructure. These priorities are also major areas of climate finance needs in order to support adaptation and reduce the vulnerability of production systems.

Furthermore, Niger attaches particular importance to strengthening human capital, governance and social cohesion, three conditions essential for the effective implementation of development policies and strategies in general, and of climate policies in particular. Priorities include improving access to basic social services (education, health, water, hygiene and sanitation), strengthening institutional capacities, and promoting youth employment and green entrepreneurship.

2.1. Finance and Investment Mobilisation Strategies

The mobilisation of financial resources for the effective implementation of CDN 3.0 is based on an integrated approach combining public, international and private sources of finance.

At national level, the State budget is gradually shifting towards low-carbon and resilient investments. The country will also strengthen its capacity to access international climate finance mechanisms, in particular the Green Climate Fund, the Global Environment Facility, the Adaptation Fund, the Least Developed Countries Fund and the Fund for responding to Loss and Damage, among others.

Niger intends to step up the mobilisation of domestic and international private finance to support the implementation of its CDN 3.0 through a portfolio of bankable climate projects in priority sectors and sub-sectors, in particular renewable energy, energy efficiency, resilient agriculture, sustainable land management, water resources and waste management. The country also intends to take advantage of the opportunities offered by Carbon Market mechanisms, in particular under Article 6 of the Paris Agreement (Articles 6.2 and 6.4), through the mobilisation of internationally transferred mitigation outcomes (ITMO) and the development of projects generating certified carbon credits.

In addition, the country intends to strengthen environmental diplomacy for advocacy and the mobilisation of financial resources at international level, with the support of a strategic development partner.

Finally, Niger plans to set up a climate window dedicated to local climate finance in order to facilitate access by local authorities to green finance and to international climate-related mechanisms. As an extension of this approach, and in line with the international momentum for the establishment and operationalisation of country platforms launched at COP 30 with the support of the Green Climate Fund Readiness Programme, Niger intends to set up its own country platform for climate and nature finance. Designed as a one-stop shop, this platform will aim to translate the priorities of CDN 3.0 into portfolios of bankable projects, coordinate technical and financial partners, both public and private, and reduce the fragmentation of climate finance support.

2.2. CDN-National Budget Alignment

Alignment between the CDN 3.0 and the State budget is an essential lever for ensuring the effective implementation of Niger’s unconditional climate commitments. It aims to guarantee that the defined priorities are translated into concrete, coherent and traceable budget allocations within public finances. This budgetary convergence ensures multi-year coherence between the objectives of the CDN 3.0 and public expenditure, thereby improving the readability and monitoring of climate finance flows. To this end, Niger is strengthening the integration of climate objectives into national planning and budgeting processes, notably through Climate-Sensitive Budget Tagging (MBSC) in the following sectors: Energy, Agriculture, Water Resources, Livestock, Environment, Transport, Urban Planning, Population.

In this regard, strengthening the institutional and technical capacities of the actors involved in planning, budgeting and monitoring public expenditure is essential to ensure effective and lasting alignment between the CDN and the State budget. This budgetary alignment of the CDN will be strengthened at the territorial level through the progressive integration of climate expenditure into the financing and programming mechanism of the ANFICT for the benefit of local authorities.

2.3. Consideration of the social dimension

Niger’s CDN 3.0, in addition to environmental protection actions, integrates an essential social dimension that emphasises the well-being of populations, the reduction of inequalities and inequity, and the protection of vulnerable groups. This dimension is based on guiding principles of equity and social inclusion: gender equality, the participation of young people and children, territorial justice, the creation of decent green jobs, and the protection of the health and well-being of populations.

This integration of the social dimension was achieved through four complementary levers:

  • the crucial nexuses: waste, green jobs and just transition; climate, peace and security; migration, environment and climate change; health, gender and climate change;
  • the territorial approach of the CDN;
  • the integration of young people and children; and.
  • the consideration of the Water, Sanitation and Hygiene component (EHA/WASH).

In terms of implementation, the social dimension of the CDN 3.0 will be based on inclusive consultations with all stakeholders, on disaggregated data (notably by sex and age) and on dedicated monitoring indicators, making it possible to measure the social reach of the CDN’s projects and measures. This approach aims to transform sectoral climate commitments into integrated development strategies, fostering a just transition.

2.3.1. Waste, green jobs and just transition

In Niger, climate change threatens major socio-economic sectors, often with job losses and worsening inequalities that require inclusive and integrated actions to reverse the trend. The waste sector holds a special place in this dynamic: its recovery, collection, sorting, recycling, composting and energy recovery combine the reduction of methane emissions, the improvement of the urban living environment and the creation of green jobs, particularly for women and young people who are widely present in informal recovery activities. The CDN 3.0 intends to structure and professionalise these value chains in order to transform these precarious jobs into decent jobs. The country plans to support informal sector workers within the framework of a just transition that respects ILO principles. Thus, six just transition areas have been identified for the implementation of the CDN 3.0. These are: (i) strengthening governance and policy coherence, (ii) developing social dialogue, (iii) improving working conditions, (iv) strengthening skills, (v) extending social protection and (vi) promoting gender equity.

2.3.2. Climate, peace and security

In response to security and climate challenges, the climate–peace–security Nexus is emerging as a lever to address the weakening of the social fabric in Niger. This nexus reveals that the real analytical crux is not only the simultaneous existence of climate shocks and security threats, but above all the weakness of their integrated governance. The CDN 3.0 integrates this dimension through three strategic axes:

  • Improving natural resource governance;
  • Improving crisis and disaster management; and
  • Integrating climate, peace and security linkages into conflict prevention strategies and development projects.

2.3.3. Migration, Environment and Climate Change

Climate change has growing impacts on migration dynamics, notably due to land degradation, desertification and the scarcity of water resources. These phenomena accentuate the vulnerability of rural populations in Niger and can lead to internal or cross-border displacement. Added to this is additional pressure on host areas, sometimes leading to further environmental degradation or pressure on available resources.

In this context, Niger commits to integrating the migration dimension during the implementation phase of its CDN in order to manage displacement linked to climate disasters (floods, droughts). The key elements adopted include the recognition of climate migrants as vulnerable, the integration of mobility into adaptation strategies, and the development and implementation of resilience projects.

2.3.4. Health, Gender and Climate Change

Cross-analysis of climate and health data shows that variations in temperature, humidity and precipitation strongly influence the seasonal distribution of climate-sensitive diseases in Niger. The increase in diseases linked to extreme climate events has a differentiated impact on populations, in particular women, children and vulnerable groups.

The integration of the health-gender-climate nexus into this CDN requires aligning health, nutrition and gender equality policies with climate policies, based on disaggregated data, on the effective participation of women and young people in decision-making bodies and on targeted financing. The key elements include multisectoral planning, monitoring of health-gender impacts and food resilience.

2.4. Territorialisation of the CDN

The review of the implementation of the CDN 2.0 showed that the involvement of decentralised authorities in implementation had been limited to actions to disseminate the document in the Regional capitals and certain communes.

The territorialisation of the CDN 3.0 will be a central pillar in its implementation. It is based on breaking down national objectives at the level of regions and communes, taking into account local specificities. Communal climate plans will be developed, supported by a decentralised financing mechanism and a territorialised monitoring and evaluation system. This approach will strengthen the effectiveness, local ownership and impact of climate actions.

2.5. Integration of children and young people

Niger has one of the youngest populations in the world, with around 55,3 % of the population under 18 years of age, making it a country with a very high demographic dependency, which represents a major challenge for education, health and employment. The vulnerability of this segment of the population to the effects of climate change limits their potential in terms of participation, innovation and entrepreneurship.

To address the challenges of youth participation in the CDN formulation processes, emphasis was placed on their involvement in identifying climate change adaptation and mitigation measures. The integration of environmental education at all school levels, the institutionalisation of youth participation in policy-making, the establishment of climate-resilient school infrastructure and health systems, and the development of youth-led adaptation projects aim to protect children and young people, guarantee an inclusive approach and improve climate governance.

Taking children and young people into account in the formulation of the CDN 3.0 has strengthened climate ambition, intergenerational equity, and the sustainability of actions. As key actors in the country’s future development, children and young people are a major lever for innovation, the adoption of sustainable production and consumption patterns, and the dissemination of knowledge and good practices within communities.

2.6. Consideration of the Water, Sanitation and Hygiene component

Niger already has two strategic documents (PANGIRE and PROSEHA) in the field of water, hygiene and sanitation that take the climate dimension into account. Indeed, the PANGIRE is a planning tool for the sector’s priority actions, the implementation of which is indispensable for sustainable development and the coordinated management of water in order to combat poverty, preserve the environment, improve the resilience of populations and ecosystems, promote socio-economic development and conserve the resource for future generations. It is also a response to the recommendations of the various international conferences to which Niger has adhered. Its implementation is based on a series of forty (40) actions structured into four (4) components, including a component dedicated to preserving the environment and strengthening climate resilience. In parallel, the Sectoral Water, Hygiene and Sanitation Programme (PROSEHA), aligned with SDG 6, organises sectoral action around five sub-programmes, including a Sanitation sub-programme supported by operational instruments such as the National Hygiene and Sanitation Policy (PHNA) and the Roadmap for an ODF Niger (FDAL, End of Open Defecation) by 2030.

These two frameworks constitute real normative and operational achievements: they establish sectoral governance that is already in place (steering arrangements, monitoring indicators, financing mechanisms), quantified targets for access to drinking water and sanitation, and an integration of the climate dimension into the water sub-sector. Thus, with regard to the EHA/WASH component, the CDN 3.0 is a continuation of these commitments, with the specific added value of aligning these sectoral policies more closely with the national climate strategy, strengthening the resilience of water and sanitation infrastructure to the impacts of climate change (water stress, floods, deterioration of water quality), and securing complementary climate finance for the sustainability of these investments.

2.7. Consideration of climate Disaster Risk Reduction (RRC) in adaptation projects/programmes

The adaptation projects/programmes identified under the CDN 3.0 and Disaster Risk Reduction (RRC) interventions will be monitored in a complementary manner in order to respond to the different levels of climate risk. Adaptation actions will primarily target the reduction of structural vulnerabilities in the medium and long term, notably through the sustainable management of natural resources and the strengthening of the resilience of production systems, communities and ecosystems. RRC interventions will also address the management of immediate risks linked to extreme climate events, through prevention, preparedness and response measures set out in contingency and multi-hazard response plans.

2.8. CDN 3.0 development process

2.8.1. Guiding principles of the process

The process of developing Niger’s CDN 3.0 is conducted according to the following guiding principles:

  • Strategic alignment: coherence with national policies and strategies (PRR, SNBC, PNA, etc) and with the Sustainable Development Goals (ODD);
  • National ownership: a process driven by national institutions, under the leadership of the Ministry of Environment, Water Resources and Sanitation (MEH/A) and the coordination of the National Technical Committee;

2.8.2. Description of the process

The CDN 3.0 development process took place in two (2) complementary phases: preparation of the revision and enhancement of ambition .

The preparatory phase built on the achievements of the implementation of the CDN 2.0 to guide the revision with the involvement of the stakeholders who are members of the National Technical Committee. It comprises: i) the country request, ii) the official launch of the process, iii) the review of the implementation of the CDN 2.0, iv) the development of the roadmap for the formulation of the CDN 3.0, and finally v) the stakeholder consultation workshops.

Ambition was enhanced through broadening the scope of the target by integrating the Waste sector, raising the targets in all CDN sectors, strengthening adaptation measures with mitigation co-benefits, and defining a progressive multi-year quantified target of a 39,7% reduction in 2030 and 51,2 % by 2035.

III. MITIGATION OF GES EMISSIONS

The mitigation objectives of the CDN 3.0 are defined relative to a baseline scenario corresponding to the projected evolution of emissions under “Business as Usual” (CNA) up to 2035, taking 2014 as the reference year.

Niger integrates the mitigation of GES emissions as a lever for transition towards a low-carbon economy, bringing co-benefits in terms of access to energy, sustainable land management and job creation, in line with its development priorities, despite its high vulnerability to climate change. The approach adopted is based on a prospective analysis of emissions using a baseline scenario, making it possible to assess sectoral dynamics3 and to identify reduction options suited to national realities. Mitigation efforts are thus structured around actions achievable with national resources and others dependent on international support, with the aim of reconciling emission reductions with other sustainable environmental, social and economic co-benefits.

3.1. Scenarios

Under the CDN 3.0, the BAU and mitigation scenarios were defined using the GACMO4 tool on the basis of observed historical trends in GES emissions over the period 1990-2022, macroeconomic and demographic projections, existing sectoral policies and practices, as well as the Policies, Actions and Measures (PAMs) for mitigating GES emissions defined in the sectors of the CDN 3.0. They are also based on the expected dynamics of economic growth, urbanisation, energy demand and changes in land use up to 2035, in order to best reflect the country’s actual development conditions.

V. LOSS AND DAMAGE RELATED TO CLIMATE CHANGE

5.1. Findings and analysis

Loss and damage (loss and damage) refers to the adverse impacts of climate change that have not been avoided through mitigation and adaptation, including those associated with extreme events such as floods, droughts, violent winds, bushfires and pest invasions, and slow-onset events, notably desertification, land degradation, soil erosion, increasing scarcity of water resources, rising temperatures and changes in rainfall patterns (GIEC 2022). In accordance with the guidance of the Intergovernmental Panel on Climate Change (GIEC) and the Warsaw International Mechanism for Loss and Damage, this notion encompasses both economic and non-economic losses. Economic losses include, in particular, damage to infrastructure, housing, public facilities, crops, livestock and other productive assets. Non-economic losses include loss of human life, harm to health and well-being, population displacement, loss of traditional knowledge, and harm to cultural heritage, social cohesion, biodiversity and ecosystem services.

In Niger, loss and damage related to climate change is manifesting increasingly under the combined effect of extreme climate hazards and environmental degradation processes. Floods and droughts are among the main climate risks facing the country. Recurrent droughts directly affect agricultural and pastoral production, while floods cause significant damage to housing, infrastructure, agricultural land and essential services.

These phenomena, which have become more frequent and more intense, cause multidimensional impacts affecting populations and their livelihoods, with loss of human life and loss and damage to infrastructure and equipment, crops, livestock, etc.

The analysis of Figure 3 shows a high variability of loss and damage in the agricultural sector between 2000 and 2024, with a particularly marked worsening in recent years. The areas of crops affected reached 164 031 ha in 2022, then a record level of 361 135 ha in 2023, before decreasing to 58 769 ha in 2024, reflecting the high exposure of agriculture to climate hazards, particularly floods. This damage can have significant repercussions on production, the incomes of rural households, food security and livelihoods. The figure also highlights economic losses, including 59 000 USD in 2008 and a significant peak in 2015. However, the absence or low level of monetary values reported for several years, despite the size of the areas affected, suggests an under-documentation of actual economic losses. These findings underline, within the framework of the CDN, the need to strengthen systems for the assessment and monetisation of loss and damage, as well as mechanisms for financing recovery and the protection of livelihoods.

This figure highlights a worsening of loss and damage associated with floods, with high inter-annual variability but a particularly marked intensification in recent years. The impacts simultaneously concern human losses, housing and social infrastructure, which reflects the multidimensional nature of climate risks.

The trend in flood impacts over the period 2000-2024 shows a high inter-annual variability of loss and damage, with a particularly significant worsening in 2024. That year recorded 86 deaths, 179 183 houses damaged, and 189 centres and 116 hospitals affected. These impacts illustrate both significant material damage and non-economic losses, particularly in human lives, as well as potential disruptions to essential social services. Their scale underlines the persistence of a residual climate risk despite adaptation efforts and justifies strengthening, in the CDN, the systems for prevention, early warning, response, recovery and financing of loss and damage.

These trends show that floods are no longer merely an environmental disaster but a genuine factor of economic and social fragility.

In addition, according to the Early Warning System (SAP), over the period 1998-2025, floods caused considerable loss and damage in Niger, with a total of 13 922 recorded events resulting in 1 367 deaths and affecting 5 027 344 people. The associated economic losses are estimated at around 510,9 billion FCFA, or nearly 851,5 million USD, in addition to which there are significant non-economic losses (human lives, population displacement, degradation of livelihoods and cultural heritage, psychological trauma). These data illustrate the scale of the loss and damage already suffered by the country as a result of climate hazards, beyond what adaptation measures can avoid, and underline the need to mobilise dedicated mechanisms for responding to loss and damage, including international finance, notably through the Fund for responding to Loss and Damage (FRLD).

According to the Disaster Risk Profile of Niger (UNDRR, 2024), climate change is expected to significantly increase the exposure of populations to floods, with up to one million people potentially affected each year under the most unfavourable climate scenarios (Figure 5). Annual economic losses related to floods in the built sector, estimated at nearly 90 million USD under current conditions, could reach 200 million under projected climate conditions (scenario SSP1-RCP2.6) and 500 million USD under projected climate conditions according to scenario SSP5-RCP8.5.

Figure 5: Population affected under current and projected climate conditions (source: UNDRR, 2024).

Furthermore, droughts already affect around 2,69 million hectares of agricultural land each year, with major impacts on food security, livelihoods and the resilience of rural populations. This figure is expected to more than double in the coming years: 6,94 million ha will be affected each year under projected climate conditions according to scenario SSP1-RCP2.6, and 5,91 million ha according to scenario SSP5-RCP8.5 (UNDRR, 2024). This order, in which the lower-emission scenario shows a larger affected area, reflects the uncertainty inherent in precipitation projections for Niger, which are less directly correlated with emission intensity than temperature is, as also highlighted by the country's Climate Risk Profile.

Beyond economic losses, climate change also generates significant non-economic losses. The progressive degradation of natural resources, increasing water scarcity, recurrent food insecurity, population displacement and the weakening of traditional solidarity mechanisms contribute to increasing social vulnerabilities and weakening community cohesion. These impacts particularly affect women, young people and children, herders, farmers and other most vulnerable groups.

This situation underlines the need for Niger to adopt a structured approach to responding to loss and damage, going beyond adaptation measures alone. Within the framework of the CDN 3.0, Niger will progressively strengthen its national approach aimed at averting, minimising and addressing loss and damage associated with the adverse effects of climate change, in connection with floods, droughts, water stress, desertification and other climate risks affecting vulnerable populations and their livelihoods. This approach will be based in particular on the strengthening of early warning systems, the development of at-risk areas and the resilience of infrastructure as levers for minimising losses, as well as on dedicated mechanisms for addressing residual economic and non-economic losses, including through access to international finance dedicated to loss and damage.

5.2. Priority areas for action

While recognising the importance of the Warsaw International Mechanism for Loss and Damage (WIM), the Santiago Network for Loss and Damage and the Fund for responding to Loss and Damage adopted under the CCNUCC, Niger intends to take advantage of the opportunities offered by these mechanisms in order to strengthen knowledge and national institutional and technical capacities, and improve information and monitoring systems for loss and damage through the following interventions:

  • the development of national systems for the collection, management and sharing of data on loss and damage;
  • the improvement of methodologies for assessing damage and economic and non-economic losses;
  • the strengthening of multi-hazard early warning systems and climate information services;
  • the strengthening of anticipatory action, preparedness and contingency planning mechanisms;
  • the promotion of resilient post-disaster recovery and reconstruction;
  • the protection of the livelihoods of vulnerable populations, particularly in the agriculture, livestock and natural resources sectors;
  • the strengthening of adaptive social protection mechanisms in the face of climate shocks;
  • the consideration of climate change-related displacement and non-economic losses in national policies, programmes and strategies;
  • the strengthening of institutional, technical and financial capacities for the monitoring, assessment and management of loss and damage.

5.3. Institutional arrangements and governance of loss and damage

Niger has a well-developed institutional framework for risk and disaster prevention and management, which constitutes a solid foundation on which to build national governance of loss and damage. The National Mechanism for the Prevention and Management of Food Crises (DNPGCA), placed under the authority of the Office of the Prime Minister, is one of the main national mechanisms for crisis prevention, monitoring and response. It relies on a network of decentralised structures at regional and departmental levels as well as on the Early Warning System (SAP), which ensures continuous monitoring of vulnerability and risks affecting populations.

At community level, the Community Early Warning and Emergency Response Systems (SCAP/RU) and the Vulnerability Monitoring Observatories (OSV) contribute to the collection and analysis of local data, the dissemination of information, the early identification of risks and the guidance of response mechanisms. These systems promote the active participation of communities in the prevention and management of climate crises and disasters, a local and inclusive dimension that is particularly relevant in view of the intervention modalities of international mechanisms dedicated to loss and damage.

The institutional arrangement is also strengthened by the existence of the Ministry of Population, Social Action and National Solidarity, the Directorate General of Civil Protection, the National Locust Control Centre, the Directorate of Epidemic Surveillance and Response, and the National Platform for Disaster Risk Prevention and Reduction created in 2024. These institutions work in a complementary manner in the areas of prevention, preparedness, emergency response, recovery and climate disaster risk reduction, as well as social inclusion.

In the specific area of climate change, the Executive Secretariat of the CNEDD, in its capacity as National Designated Authority (AND) of the Fund for responding to Loss and Damage (FRLD), ensures national coordination of the implementation of Niger's climate policies and international commitments, in partnership with the Ministry of Environment, Water and Sanitation, with regard to strategic guidance, monitoring and evaluation, and capacity building of stakeholders.

However, these systems were designed mainly from a disaster prevention and management perspective, and their articulation around an integrated approach to loss and damage has yet to be built. The main challenges concern the establishment of a national coordination mechanism dedicated to loss and damage, the development of a systematic mechanism for collecting and recording economic and non-economic losses based on data from the SAP and community systems, and the strengthening of institutional capacities for the formulation of programmes eligible for international finance, in particular that of the FRLD. In this respect, the CDN 3.0 provides a guiding framework for progressively structuring this governance, building on existing institutional and community achievements.

VII. MEASUREMENT FRAMEWORK FOR THE POLICIES, ACTIONS AND MEASURES (PAMs) OF THE CDN 3.0

The measurement framework for the Policies, Actions and Measures (PAMs) of the CDN 3.0 constitutes an integrated system for monitoring, evaluation, learning and transparency ensuring the effective steering of the implementation of said CDN. It aims to measure the progress made in the priority sectors through monitoring of physical and financial achievements, results obtained and impacts in terms of reducing greenhouse gas (GES) emissions, carbon removal, adaptation and climate resilience.

The measurement framework covers all the priority sectors of the CDN 3.0, notably energy, agriculture, livestock, forestry, wetlands, water resources and sanitation, infrastructure and transport, waste, health, education as well as cross-cutting actions related to climate governance, children and youth, gender, environmental migration and territorial development. It will make it possible to track the climate investments that will be mobilised for adaptation actions, as well as those related to mitigation measures.

The framework is based on a results-based approach (Results-Based Approach), structured around three complementary levels of indicators:

  • output indicators, which measure the infrastructure, equipment, technologies and areas implemented;
  • outcome indicators, which make it possible to assess the changes brought about by the interventions;
  • impact indicators, which measure GES emission reductions, carbon removals, improvement in climate resilience as well as socio-economic and environmental co-benefits.

In the energy sector, the measurement indicators will cover in particular:

  • installed renewable energy capacity;
  • energy savings achieved;
  • the reduction of technical losses in the electricity grid;
  • volumes of flared gas avoided;
  • quantities of fossil fuels substituted as well as emission reductions associated with efficient equipment, solar systems and improved urban transport.

In the Agriculture, Forestry and Other Land Use (AFAT) sector, the framework will make it possible to track:

  • areas restored, reforested or placed under climate-smart practices;
  • quantities of compost produced;
  • areas under SRI and PPU;
  • areas under Farmer-Managed Natural Regeneration (RNA);
  • areas of pastoral restoration as well as emission reductions and carbon removals associated with sustainable agricultural, pastoral and forestry practices.

For the waste sector, the indicators will make it possible in particular to:

  • measure the quantities of waste recovered, recycled or treated;
  • the volumes of biogas produced;
  • installed composting capacity;
  • the quantities of plastic waste recycled as well as the reductions in methane emissions from organic waste.

In the adaptation component, notably water resources, infrastructure, health, education and wetlands, the measurement framework will also make it possible to track:

  • progress made in terms of climate resilience;
  • access to essential services;
  • reduction of vulnerabilities;
  • protection of infrastructure and strengthening of early warning systems;
  • Specific indicators will be developed to track the effects of the actions on vulnerable populations, children and youth, women and local authorities.

The measurement framework will also rely on primary data collected from sectoral ministries, technical institutions, local authorities, the private sector, projects and programmes, community organisations as well as technical and financial partners. The data collected will include in particular areas developed or restored, installed energy capacity, production volumes, energy consumption, livestock numbers, quantities of waste treated, forestry data, hydrometeorological data as well as geospatial information derived from remote sensing and geographic information systems.

Estimates of emission reductions and removals will be made in accordance with the 2006 IPCC Guidelines and their 2019 Refinement as well as the GACMO tool used for the scenario modelling of the CDN.

The system will be supported by the MRV-CDN digital platform enabling the centralisation, processing, analysis, storage and reporting of climate data relating to the implementation of the CDN 3.0. It will integrate specific monitoring and impact indicators for each of the PAMs in order to ensure regular monitoring of progress towards the national objectives of resilient and low-carbon development.

Coordination of the measurement framework will be ensured by the Ministry of Environment, Water and Sanitation through the CDN coordination unit, in collaboration with the sectoral structures involved. Particular attention will be paid to strengthening the technical, institutional and operational capacities of the actors involved in the collection, management, processing and reporting of climate data in order to ensure the sustainability, reliability, transparency and continuous improvement of the national CDN monitoring system.

CONCLUSION

The submission of the CDN 3.0 constitutes a decisive step in strengthening Niger's climate ambition and in consolidating a resilient, inclusive and sustainable development model. It reflects the country's determination to place climate action at the heart of its national priorities, in line with the Programme for the Refoundation of the Republic and its international commitments.

The new ambitions focused on broadening the scope of the target through the inclusion of the Waste sector and crucial nexuses, the increased involvement of children and youth and civil society, the consideration of certain emerging themes, notably loss and damage, carbon credits, and alignment with national planning instruments (PRR, SNBC, PNA) give this CDN reinforced institutional legitimacy and strategic scope. These ambitions are also reinforced by the territorialisation of the CDN through communal development plans, representing a structuring advance for local ownership of climate issues and the effectiveness of public investment.

However, achieving the set targets remains heavily dependent on the mobilisation of adequate financial resources, technology transfer and capacity building, particularly within the framework of international cooperation. To this end, Niger calls for support from the international community in accordance with the principles of equity and common but differentiated responsibilities. The financing needs for implementation over the 2025-2035 period are estimated at USD 16 937,94 million (16,94 billion), of which USD 3 452,84 million (Unconditional scenario), i.e. 20,4%, supported by the State, and USD 13 485,11 million (Conditional scenario), i.e. 79,6 %, to be mobilised from the PTF and International Climate Finance. This breakdown by conditionality constitutes a complementary reading, from a financing perspective, of the same overall amount of USD 16 937,94 million already presented by component (55,61 % for Adaptation and 44,39 % for Mitigation).

Furthermore, Niger reaffirms its commitment to strengthen, in an integrated manner, its actions to manage loss and damage, while mobilising the means of implementation necessary to protect populations, livelihoods, ecosystems and development gains against the growing effects of climate change.

Moreover, the commitment of all national actors, notably public institutions, local authorities, the private sector, civil society organisations, children and youth and technical and financial partners, is essential to support these efforts.

Also, strengthening governance, monitoring-evaluation and transparency arrangements will be essential to guarantee effective and measurable implementation of the actions undertaken.

Ultimately, the CDN 3.0 is intended as a unifying and operational framework for transforming climate challenges into development opportunities, for the benefit of populations and future generations, while contributing significantly to the global effort to combat climate change.
