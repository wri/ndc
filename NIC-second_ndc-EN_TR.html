I.

Introduction

Nicaragua, as a Member State of the United Nations Framework Convention on Climate Change (UNFCCC) and the Paris Agreement, reaffirms its commitment to advancing towards a sustainable, resilient, and low-carbon development model, in strict adherence to the Political Constitution of the Republic and the principles of climate justice. The Nationally Determined Contributions (NDCs) constitute the sovereign expression of this commitment and represent a fundamental pillar of the national policy for climate change mitigation and adaptation.

In this context, the Government of Reconciliation and National Unity (GRUN) has strengthened the institutional, regulatory, and programmatic framework that guides climate action in Nicaragua. The National Climate Change Policy, approved in 2022, and the National Plan for the Fight Against Poverty and for Human Development 2022-2026 explicitly integrate climate management into their strategic pillars, coordinating inter-institutional efforts to reduce poverty, guarantee food security, and protect natural resources.

This document outlines the main progress made by the country in mitigation and adaptation, the characterization of its greenhouse gas emissions, the principles underpinning national climate action, and the projections for the implementation of the NDCs. It also describes the communication strategy adopted, which promotes the active and leading participation of families, communities, indigenous peoples, Afro-descendants, women, youth, the private sector, universities, and regional and local governments, consistent with the model of dialogue, consensus, and partnerships that characterizes national public management.

Nicaragua has made progress in key sectors such as energy, land use and land-use change, forestry, and industry, through diversification of its energy matrix, forest restoration, sustainable ecosystem management, and the progressive reduction of ozone-depleting substances. These actions not only contribute to fulfilling international commitments but also strengthen the country's economic and social resilience.

Achieving Nicaragua's NDC goals and ambitions depends on effective support under the Convention, particularly in climate finance, technology transfer, and capacity building. The commitment of developed countries to fulfill these obligations is essential for Nicaragua to implement its climate plans and move towards sustainable development that guarantees the well-being of families and reduces poverty.

Under these principles, Nicaragua reaffirms that compliance with its NDCs represents an opportunity to consolidate inclusive and equitable development that prioritizes life, the well-being of families, and the protection of Mother Earth.

III. Main National Advances in Climate Change

Nicaragua has made significant national progress through the establishment of a strategic political and regulatory framework, comprised of instruments that provide the necessary conditions to coordinate efforts among different sectors and levels of government, ensuring a comprehensive approach to climate action.

3.1 Climate Change Mitigation in Nicaragua

National framework for climate change management

In 2010, Nicaragua was the first country to sign the universal declaration of the Common Good of Mother Earth and Humanity through the 'Charter of the Rights of Mother Earth,' and in 2014, it was incorporated into the Political Constitution of Nicaragua.1 This is one of the many examples of the country's commitment and efforts to contribute, according to its respective capacities and under the principle of Common and Differentiated Responsibilities, to global efforts to address climate change.

To further strengthen the country's strategic and political framework related to climate change, in 2022, Nicaragua approved and published the National Climate Change Policy. The policy's objective is to contribute to the fight against poverty and to Nicaragua's sustainable human development by preparing a society with a greater capacity to respond to the impacts of climate change, and one that is more aware and responsible in the face of this challenge; one that promotes a low-carbon economy according to capacity, based on environmentally, socially, and economically sustainable production processes and services, incorporating knowledge and innovation.

The implementation of the Policy, its plans, programs, and projects will depend on the extent to which developed countries assume their historical responsibilities for causing global warming and fulfill their commitments to provide new, predictable, and additional financial resources, as well as technology transfer. Nicaragua's climate change management model integrates all sectors that drive the country's economy with a low-carbon approach and with the participation of public and private actors at the national and international levels. To strengthen this model, the Policy plays a key role in climate action, coordinating with the public policy for combating poverty and aligning it with the National Policy for Climate Change and Human Development (PNLCP-DH) 2022-2026, which includes among its lines of action "Measures to address the impacts of climate variability and climate change" (Figure 5).

3.1.1. Advances in the Energy Sector

The transition to an energy matrix less dependent on fossil fuels is a fundamental priority of the Government of Reconciliation and National Unity (GRUN). This commitment is reflected in the design, enactment, and implementation of policy instruments aimed at the sustainable development of the energy sector.

The following are the main policy instruments in force in the country that contribute to strengthening the energy sector and achieving climate action goals, as well as the renewable energy projects that are part of the national energy system and are currently operational:

Law for the Promotion of Electricity Generation with Renewable Sources

The objective of Law No. 532, the Law for the Promotion of Electricity Generation from Renewable Sources, is to promote the development of new electricity generation projects using renewable sources, as well as the expansion of the installed capacity of existing projects that utilize these sources. Furthermore, it incentivizes electricity generation using sustainably produced biomass and/or biogas. The law establishes fiscal, economic, and financial incentives to facilitate this development, framing all actions within the sustainable use of renewable energy resources.

This law defines renewable energy sources as those natural resources that can be extracted sustainably to generate electricity. These sources include hydropower, wind, solar, geothermal, biomass, and others. Specifically regarding biomass, all locally and sustainably produced organic resources that can be used for energy generation are considered, such as crop residues (sugarcane, peanut hulls, coffee grounds, etc.).

The law also stipulates that new renewable energy generation projects must align with the following criteria:

  • Comply with the National Energy Policy approved by the Presidency of the Republic (presented in the following section)
  • Follow the guidelines of the current Indicative Expansion Plan.
  • To contribute to diversifying the national energy matrix through the use of renewable resources authorized by this law.
  • To support the supply of the country's growing energy demand through sustainable projects, implemented in timeframes consistent with the increase in national demand, or intended to supply both the Central American and national electricity markets.
  • Facilitate the increase of electricity coverage in the country.
  • Comply with current environmental regulations.

National Energy Policy

Nicaragua's National Energy Policy was established in 2004 through Executive Decree No. 13-2004. The policy's strategic objective is to ensure a sustainable, accessible, and efficient energy supply, promoting the diversification of the energy mix with renewable sources, increasing electricity coverage, and fostering efficiency and environmental sustainability in the sector. Furthermore, it establishes a regulatory framework that favors investment in and development of energy infrastructure, strengthening the country's energy resilience and autonomy.

The energy sector is a key component in the country's sustainable development strategies; therefore, the National Energy Policy considers the following principles crucial for the management of the sector:

Policy Leadership: The State Administration: Compliance with the commitments of public and private actors in the sector. Recognition of the State's role in the management of natural and energy resources.

Legal Compliance: The Financial Sustainability policy: Sector Efficiency: must align with the Constitution, national laws, international agreements, and State policies. Ensure the economic viability of energy projects. Promote efficiency in the performance of all actors in the energy sector.

State Participation: Ensuring Environmental Sustainability: Comprehensive Consideration: State intervention in the use of fossil fuels. Evaluate the current situation, challenges, investments, and needs in the energy sector with respect for the environment.

Indicative Plan for Generation Expansion 2021-2035

In line with Law 532 and the National Energy Policy, Nicaragua has the Indicative Plan for Generation Expansion 2021-2035, linked to the conditional goals of the NDC in the energy sector, which prioritize the transition to renewable sources and the progressive reduction of emissions from electricity generation.

The implementation of the Plan aims to increase installed clean energy capacity, reduce dependence on fossil fuels, and improve the resilience of the electricity system. At a strategic level, the Plan is a fundamental instrument for attracting national and international investment and financial support for renewable energy projects, aligned with the M1 conditionality of the NDC.

The Plan establishes ambitious projections to increase the country's installed renewable energy capacity in the coming years. According to forecasts, by 2028, 58% of installed capacity is expected to come from renewable sources. In the longer term, the plan projects that by 2035 renewable energy will represent 69% of installed capacity, consolidating Nicaragua's position as a country committed to the transition to a cleaner and more resilient energy mix.

To achieve the projected goals in terms of installed renewable energy capacity, the Plan includes the implementation of several strategic projects across different renewable technologies. This will not only contribute to increasing clean energy generation but will also strengthen the country's energy security. 3.1.2 Progress in the Land Use, Land Use Change and Forestry sector

Starting in 2007, a sustainable development strategy was implemented that has had a positive impact on the conservation and sustainable management of forests. This strategy is carried out through national policies and programs that guide public and private investment, which have shifted the country's natural resource exploitation patterns toward a model of rational and responsible use.

Nicaragua has made significant efforts towards strengthening institutional capacity and defining public policies and management instruments that contribute to mitigating climate change through sustainable forest management:

  • General Law of the Environment and Natural Resources: Contains the general rules to regulate the conservation, protection, improvement, and restoration of the environment and natural resources and to ensure their rational and sustainable use. Its provisions are of public order; that is, mandatory compliance with environmental management regulations. Among the objectives of this law are the prevention, regulation, and control of any of the causes or activities that lead to environmental degradation and pollution of ecosystems.

  • National Policy for Sustainable Development of the Forestry Sector4: Establishes that Nicaraguan families can improve their quality of life in an associative and gradual manner, under a model of sustainable forest, agroforestry and agro-industrial use and management, articulated with other actors in rural and non-rural, national and international value chains, based on environmental conservation and sustainable national production for food security, under a territorial planning approach.

National Strategy for Reducing Emissions from Deforestation and Forest Degradation (ENDE-REDD+) (2018-2040)

Since 2008, Nicaragua has participated in the international REDD+ mechanism, which was designed by the UNFCCC with the objective of reducing CO2 emissions from deforestation and forest degradation.

As an effective response to these commitments, Nicaragua has the “National Strategy for Reducing Emissions from Deforestation and Forest Degradation (ENDE-REDD+)4”, promoted by the GRUN, through MARENA. The ENDE-REDD+ is conceived as an instrument for implementing the strategic and programmatic framework for climate change mitigation and adaptation. Its objective is to contribute to reducing emissions from deforestation and forest degradation, as well as preventing and reducing the negative impacts of climate change, by increasing the resilience and adaptive capacity of forest and agricultural ecosystems and the populations dependent on them. This aims to reduce social, ecological, and economic vulnerability, building capacity to contribute to GHG mitigation (mainly CO2), sustainable forest management, biodiversity conservation, increased carbon stocks, the generation of conservation co-benefits, and an alternative source of supplementary income for families involved in protecting Nicaragua's natural forest heritage.

Its goal is to reduce the deforestation rate by at least 50% by 2040, which will be achieved if programs and projects with a focus on sustainable forest management and the recovery and increase of forest cover are guaranteed. The National Strategy for Renewable Energy and Sustainable Development (ENDEREDD+) represents an opportunity to strengthen the implementation of the environmental policy framework, consolidate domestic investments and attract international investment, and will also contribute to reducing risks from natural disasters, protecting and/or restoring water resources and degraded ecosystems, restoring the landscape, and protecting biodiversity. Development Strategy for the Caribbean Coast, Alto Wangki and Bocay (2019 –2029)

The Nicaraguan Caribbean and Upper Wangki Bocay region comprises two Autonomous Regions and one Special Regime Zone, constituting 46% of the national territory. It includes 20 municipalities and 23 territories of indigenous and Afro-descendant communities. It contains 89% of the country's forests, over 50% of its cattle herds, and 23% of its total agricultural area, as well as 70% of its fish production and 60% of its mineral resources. It also boasts over 400 km of coastline and 100,000 km² of marine platform in the Caribbean Sea.

Due to the importance of the Caribbean Coast, a review and analysis process was carried out to update the Development Strategy for the Caribbean Coast and the Upper Wangki and Bocay (2019-2029)5, which is an instrument designed to mainstream the programs and projects designed to combat the main causes of deforestation and forest degradation. This strategy projects the implementation of a more intensive, equitable, and environmentally sustainable production-protection model. The strategy contains four pillars: 1) Sociocultural Development; 2) Territorial Economic Development with a focus on climate change; 3) Productive and Economic Transformation; and 4) Strengthening of autonomous institutions, structured into 28 programs that in turn contain results/guidelines and indicators, each of which is structured with programs.

Axis 2: Territorial Economic Development with a Climate Change Focus, proposes the implementation of Program 1, “Environmental Management, Risk Management, and Climate Change,” which is directly linked to the objective of the Caribbean Coast Emissions Reduction Program, whose overall goal is to reduce 14.13 MtCO2eq over 5 years. It is expected to improve intensive production systems, employment, and biodiversity conservation.

One of the most relevant aspects of this axis is its proposal to implement Program 1, “Environmental Management, Risk Management, and Climate Change,” which is directly linked to the objective of the Caribbean Coast Emissions Reduction Program, whose overall goal is to reduce emissions by 14.13 MtCO2eq7 over 5 years. It is expected to improve intensive production systems, employment, and biodiversity conservation (Table 20).

In 2017, Nicaragua, as a party to the Convention to Combat Desertification, through its Land Degradation Neutrality (LDN) Program, presented its National Strategy for Land Degradation Neutrality, which includes goals and measures, as well as a baseline identifying critical areas resulting from loss of land cover, low productivity, and reduced soil carbon.

These actions planned in the strategy will contribute to reducing pressure on the forest and directly impact the reduction of the deforestation rate.

National Forestry Program (2020 – 2030)

The National Forestry Program (NFP)8 is the main planning and management instrument for the Nicaraguan forestry sector and operationalizes the National Sustainable Development Policy. It is linked to the main environmental management instruments at the regional and national levels, as well as to the main public policies promoted by the Government of Nicaragua. It allows for the promotion of actions for climate change mitigation and adaptation, reduces deforestation and forest degradation, contributes to carbon sequestration estimates, and is key in identifying actions to promote the development of the value chain for timber and other forest products.

Its objective is to provide guidelines that contribute to promoting the conservation and restoration of Nicaragua's forest landscape (forest ecosystems) through sustainable forest management and planning; ensuring the contribution to climate change mitigation and adaptation and the protection of Mother Earth, with the active participation of the productive sectors, indigenous and Afro-descendant peoples, families and the community, prioritizing youth and women, with access to incentives consistent with the country's Forestry Development Policy.

It is divided into eight subprograms that dynamically address the sector and its guidelines will contribute significantly to achieving the NDC's LULUCF target:

  • Subprogram 1: Forest governance and institutions.
  • Subprogram 2: Forest and agroforestry plantations
  • Subprogram 3: Forest Management and Conservation
  • Subprogram 4: Management and generation of forest knowledge (research and technology)
  • Subprogram 5: Development of the forestry industry, value chain, trade and investment
  • Subprogram 6: Dendroenergy (firewood and charcoal)
  • Subprogramme 7: Climate Change and Forest Resilience
  • Subprogram 8: Generation and analysis of information for reporting and decision-making

National inter-institutional plans for the prevention and control of forest, agricultural and brush fires

The Government of Nicaragua implements annual plans for the prevention and control of forest, agricultural, and brush fires. These plans coordinate strategic inter-institutional efforts through a commission that includes entities such as SINAPRED, MARENA, Civil Defense, MINED, the Fire Department, the Attorney General's Office, the National Police, producers, and community members.

Compared to 2024, the number of fires decreased by 91, resulting in a reduction of 8,651.29 hectares affected. Forest fires saw a reduction of 34 fires, with a decrease of 5,845.44 hectares. Agricultural fires decreased by 57 fires, with a reduction of 2,806.30 hectares.

National Strategy for Firewood and Charcoal (2011 – 2021)

The strategy establishes key guidelines for all productive and social sectors of the population involved in the firewood and charcoal value chain. It orients the forestry sector's vision towards the sustainable production and optimal marketing of forest energy products and their efficient transformation into heat energy.

It will contribute to reducing deforestation by lowering the rate of forest degradation through incentives for the promotion of energy forest plantations, agroforestry systems, and silvopastoral systems.

National Reforestation Campaign

This is part of the initiatives implemented by the Government of Nicaragua since 2007. The campaign's objective is to raise public awareness about the importance of reversing deforestation, increasing forest cover, and maintaining/improving the production of environmental services provided by forests, including carbon storage. The reforestation campaigns carried out under this plan, known as reforestation crusades, are implemented by state institutions at different levels: central and territorial, with the support of the population.

Between 2007 and 2023, 19,700 nurseries were established, producing 221 million plants, which enabled the restoration of 321,000 hectares nationwide. Strategically, the Government of National Unity (GRUN) has strengthened this initiative by integrating youth, communities, and producers through the new Reforestation and Restoration Campaign, “Green, I Love You Green.” For the period 2024–2029, the campaign projects the production and establishment of 99.3 million plants. To date, progress toward this goal has been made with 16.8 million plants produced, 4,509 nurseries established, and 16,842 hectares restored throughout the country.

3.1.3 Advances in the Industrial Processes Sector and Product Use

Regulation for the Control of Substances that Deplete the Ozone Layer

Since 2022, Nicaragua has had a Regulation for the Control of Ozone-Depleting Substances (Decree 09-2022)10 which establishes guidelines for the regulation, import, export, and use of ozone-depleting substances and HFCs, complying with the country's international commitments, such as the Montreal Protocol and the Kigali Amendment. This regulation includes specific provisions to prevent the illicit trade of these substances and promote sustainable practices in key sectors such as refrigeration, hospitality, food, and transportation. Among the highlights are:

  • Registration and licensing: Requires importers and exporters of regulated substances to register and obtain specific licenses to operate.
  • Customs control: Mechanisms at customs are being strengthened to guarantee compliance with regulations, preventing the illegal entry of these substances.
  • Tariff code update: The codes are adjusted to facilitate the identification and monitoring of controlled substances.
  • Technical training: This includes training personnel in the public and private sectors on best practices in handling these substances.

The strategic objective of the regulation is to ensure the preservation of the ozone layer and mitigate climate change by limiting the use of substances with a high global warming potential, such as HFCs. Furthermore, effective implementation allows access to international resources for projects related to environmentally and climate-friendly technologies.

Roadmap for the implementation of the Kigali Amendment

The Kigali Amendment to the Montreal Protocol, adopted in 2016 and in force since 2019, establishes a global framework for the gradual reduction of HFCs, compounds widely used in refrigeration and air conditioning systems that have a high global warming potential. For Nicaragua, as a party to the Montreal Protocol, the implementation of this amendment represents an opportunity to strengthen climate mitigation actions aligned with its Nationally Determined Contributions (NDCs).

In this context, in 2023 the country drew up a National Roadmap to achieve the commitments under the Amendment and simultaneously generate additional co-benefits that contribute to the fight against climate change, poverty and the care of natural resources11.

3.2 Adaptation to Climate Change in Nicaragua

National circumstances, institutional arrangements and legal framework

National circumstances

Nicaragua is located in the center of the Central American isthmus, joining the two large continental landmasses of North and South America. The national territory covers an area of 130,327.9 km², bordering the Republic of Honduras to the north, the Pacific Ocean to the west, the Republic of Costa Rica to the south, and the Caribbean Sea to the east. Due to its geographical position, it is a country highly vulnerable to extreme and slow-developing hydrometeorological phenomena such as hurricanes and droughts. The intensity of both phenomena is determined by the behavior and anomalies of the El Niño-Southern Oscillation (ENSO), a large-scale climatic phenomenon that causes fluctuations in ocean temperature in the central and eastern regions of the equatorial Pacific Ocean.

During the period from 2000 to 2019, the Global Climate Risk Index (Germanwatch, 2021) ranked Nicaragua 49th among the countries most affected by extreme hydrometeorological events globally and first in the Central American region. In 2020, Nicaragua suffered the passage of two hurricanes of categories 4 and 5, affecting the entire country: Hurricane Eta, a category 4 hurricane, which impacted the North Caribbean Coast Autonomous Region (RACCN) on November 3, 2020, and ten days later, Hurricane Iota, a category 5 hurricane, which struck the same region, causing enormous destruction and the loss of 21 human lives, affecting more than 3 million people throughout the country. They caused losses and damages estimated at US$990.1 million, equivalent to 7.84% of the nominal GDP of 2020, affecting, among other assets: 45,523 homes, 261 schools, 95 health facilities, 201 bridges, 1,975 km of macadam roads, 4,889 km of rural roads and 1,750 km of highways, 36 public buildings, and 2 docks; not including damages to production and the environment (PNLCP, 2022-2026).

The country has been severely affected by tropical cyclones with direct consequences throughout its history. According to the First Biennial Update Report of Nicaragua (Government of Nicaragua, 2023), between 1980 and 2022, 29 tropical cyclones, 20 floods, and 12 drought events were recorded. These events vary in the magnitude of their socioeconomic and environmental effects. As of 2022, losses and damages caused by hydrometeorological phenomena totaled USD 8,354.5 million, distributed as follows: USD 8,318.3 million from tropical cyclones; USD 1.0 million from floods; and USD 35.2 million from droughts. According to the data compiled in the First Biennial Update Report, the greatest losses and damages have been caused by tropical cyclones, due to their higher frequency and their direct impacts on infrastructure. Information is available on 15 cyclones, totaling USD 8,318.3 million. 85% of this amount is concentrated in five hurricanes: Joan, Mitch, Eta, Iota, Alleta, and Felix.

Adaptation priorities and barriers

Given Nicaragua's high vulnerability to climate change, the need to address critical challenges requiring strategic efforts in various areas is evident. These challenges highlight the importance of strengthening the country's institutional, technical, and social capacity to effectively manage climate impacts. The main aspects and key actions are detailed below:

1. Ongoing fight against poverty and reduction of territorial vulnerabilities

Poverty is a factor that exacerbates climate vulnerability, restricting communities' capacity to cope with, respond to, and recover from climate impacts. Therefore, it is crucial to implement sustainable development programs that generate local economic opportunities, reduce insecurity, and strengthen territorial resilience.

2. Reduction of sociocultural, economic, political, historical, and gender gaps. Structural inequalities amplify risks for the most vulnerable groups, including women, indigenous peoples, and marginalized communities. Addressing these gaps requires integrating gender equity approaches and respect for cultural diversity into public policies and development projects.

3. Access to and Mobilization of International Financial Resources Mobilizing external financing is essential to mitigate the losses and damages resulting from climate change. Nicaragua needs to strengthen its capacity to access international funds, as well as ensure the implementation of adaptation strategies that protect both ecosystems and communities.

4. Mainstreaming climate change adaptation across all sectors of society. Integrating adaptation into public policies, development plans, and economic activities is essential. This approach must involve local governments, the private sector, academia, and civil society, promoting climate action as a cross-cutting theme in all sectors.

5. Implementation of a sustainable and resilient development model. Development based on sustainability requires prioritizing the proper management of natural resources, promoting sustainable agricultural and economic practices, and building infrastructure adapted to climate risks. This model must be aligned with international commitments and geared towards a balance between human needs and environmental protection.

Additionally, strengthening climate awareness and education at all levels of society is fundamental to equipping communities with the tools they need to adapt to an uncertain future. Promoting research and access to innovative technologies also play a key role in improving national capacities to address this global challenge.

Progress in the implementation of the adaptation

In 2020, Nicaragua submitted an updated version of its Nationally Determined Contribution (NDC) to the UNFCCC, increasing its ambition by incorporating priority climate change adaptation measures. These actions, however, are contingent upon receiving international financial support for their implementation, underscoring the need for global cooperation to address climate challenges.

In 2023, the study entitled "Systematization and Evaluation of Climate Change Adaptation Measures Implemented Through Projects Executed in Nicaragua" was carried out as part of the efforts to monitor and evaluate adaptation actions implemented in the country. This analysis, based on the evaluation methodologies promoted by the UNFCCC, focused on projects financed by international organizations between 2010 and 2020, providing a comprehensive assessment of their scope and results. Among the results, climate change adaptation measures were classified and categorized by sector of intervention. This contributed to identifying knowledge, technological, and support gaps in order to define a monitoring and evaluation system for adaptation in Nicaragua.

IV. Current status of the NDCs in Nicaragua

Nicaragua's Nationally Determined Contribution (NDC) was submitted to the UNFCCC in 2018 and updated in 2020, qualitatively highlighting the efforts made to contribute to climate change mitigation and adaptation, in compliance with the Paris Agreement.

Regarding mitigation, the NDC focuses on the energy and forest management sectors, land use, and land-use change. It proposes measures to expand renewable energy generation, as well as actions for forest conservation and restoration.

In accordance with the right to sustainable development of the Nicaraguan people, the NDC contemplates the implementation of national production strategies aligned with the country's regulatory and policy frameworks. These actions seek to promote foreign investment and international cooperation, contributing to the achievement of the established goals in the priority sectors.

4.1 Mitigation Actions proposed in the NDC

Energy Sector

The growth of electricity generation through renewable energy sources has been limited by the high price of clean energy generation technologies, forcing Nicaragua to establish a balance between energy production from diverse sources.

Due to the above, the NDC proposes increasing the percentage of electricity generation through other renewable energy sources such as solar, wind, and biomass to 65% by 2030, which represents a 35% increase in the share of renewable energy in the national electricity mix compared to 2007, considering the increased coverage.

The goal is aligned with the country's strategic priorities and takes into account the main policy instruments for the development of the energy sector, particularly the Electricity Generation Expansion Plan (2019-2033).

By 2023, the nominal installed capacity from renewable sources was reported to be 45.75%, 6.77% higher than the NDC baseline capacity (installed capacity in 2007 was 38.98%). The greatest advance in Nicaragua's energy sector has been the diversification of its installed capacity in terms of renewable energy: hydroelectric, geothermal, biomass, wind, and solar.

According to Nicaragua's National Public Investment System (SNIP), during the period 2007–2022, the country has used national treasury funds and international cooperation (donations and loans) to achieve the transition to renewable energy. Over the last 15 years, a total of USD 855.67 million has been invested in the energy sector, with the largest proportion (74.55%, or USD 637.88 million) coming from loans, 17.54% from national treasury funds (USD 150.05 million), and 7.92% (USD 67.74 million) from donations.

Monitoring and evaluation of adaptation actions and their progress

Within the framework of the commitments assumed by the GRUN before the UNFCCC to report progress on adaptation, as well as to monitor national public policies, this section presents the general guidelines and criteria that guide the proposed development of a national system for monitoring and tracking adaptation measures to climate change.

This system is conceived as an inter-institutional tool designed to standardize the processes related to the collection, recording, storage, analysis, and presentation of information, thus facilitating the monitoring of progress toward achieving established goals. The system's proposal is approached from two complementary perspectives, which seek to guarantee a comprehensive and efficient vision in its implementation.

  • Monitoring and Tracking (MT), conceptualized as a systematic, standardized, and continuous process for collecting, recording, storing, and using data to monitor progress in achieving the goals and expected results with the implementation of change adaptation measures.
  • Evaluation (E), conceptualized as the process of reviewing, examining, analyzing, and determining how the implementation of climate change adaptation measures meets the achieved results and compares with the planned results.

Integrating these two perspectives (SM&E) allows us to establish the conceptual basis of the system for identifying the objective of climate change adaptation; defining the parameters for measuring success in implementing adaptation measures; developing appropriate indicators for monitoring; identifying methods or mechanisms for collecting appropriate data; and providing greater clarity regarding how implementing adaptation measures is expected to create changes in the stakeholders and their main livelihoods.

The purpose of the proposed SM&E system is to provide a framework for the processes required to collect, record, store, review, examine, analyze, and determine progress in fulfilling the GRUN's commitments regarding climate change adaptation. The scope of this system is defined by the following criteria:

Institutionally:

  • The system is applicable nationwide.
  • The Ministry of Environment and Natural Resources (MARENA) will be the responsible and coordinating body for the management and administration of the system.
  • Public institutions that implement development or related projects will assist in implementing the system.
  • MARENA will be responsible for coordinating with other relevant institutional bodies or platforms to implement the system, specifically with the National System for Disaster Prevention, Mitigation and Response and the Production, Consumption and Trade System.
  • The proposed evaluation and monitoring system must be aligned with the System of

Information from the Government of Reconciliation and National Unity (SIGRUN) to provide information on the effects achieved in the public policy dimension of adaptation.

In operational terms:

  • Applies to the climate change adaptation measures defined in the
  • When climate change adaptation measures are implemented, the degree of compliance will be measured and evaluated at three levels: (1) activity; (2) effects (or results); and (3) objectives of the sectors prioritized for adaptation.
  • The proposed indicators at the level of: (1) activity, are to evaluate the achievement of the actions implemented in the adaptation measures; (2) effect, to evaluate the effectiveness of the measures to achieve adaptation to climate change in the various; and (3) objective, are strategic indicators to measure the country's contribution to international commitments based on the strategic sectors prioritized by the GRUN.

To begin the system design process, it is essential that MARENA establish and maintain a permanent and up-to-date registry of projects or programs financed by multilateral organizations. To achieve this, communication and coordination must be fostered with the Ministry of Finance and Public Credit (MHCP), which acts as the GRUN's counterpart in financing agreements, and with the ministries responsible for project implementation. These institutions must provide the necessary information and documentation to guarantee a complete and reliable registry.

In compliance with CMA 5 requirements regarding transparency and support for information sources, MARENA shall use existing institutional mechanisms to access, safeguard, and protect the confidentiality of the information provided. This includes the proper handling of digital documents related to the design, implementation, and completion of projects, ensuring their exclusive use for official purposes.

Considering resource limitations, it is recommended to begin registering existing projects or programs by systematizing the information in manageable databases, such as Excel spreadsheets. This initial approach will allow for building a solid foundation for information management, while working on progressive improvements toward a more robust and specialized system.

V. Fundamental Principles of the NDC Goals

Nicaragua

Implementing the NDC requires recognizing that climate change is a cross-cutting challenge that affects public and private decisions, with direct impacts on the country's economic and social growth. Its integration into national development demands the adoption of the following general principles:

1. Focus on sustainable development

Climate change must influence development decisions and actions, prioritizing measures that combine mitigation and adaptation, ensuring sustainable economic growth in the face of its impacts, contributing to the reduction of greenhouse gas (GHG) emissions and to the achievement of the Sustainable Development Goals (SDGs).

2. Complementarity with disaster risk management

Climate action is complemented by risk management, understanding that reducing current exposure and vulnerability also strengthens future adaptive capacity. Economic options that are fragile in the face of current climate variability will not adapt to future climate change; therefore, measures must address current and future risks simultaneously.

3. Innovation and efficient use of resources

The response to climate change must incorporate innovation in technological development, production processes, business strategies, and public investment. This will allow for more secure economic development, with less exposure to climate risks and greater capacity for GHG mitigation.

4. Ecosystem approach

Adaptation must recognize that ecosystem degradation increases vulnerability and reduces mitigation capacity. Measures will be implemented within a dynamic framework that prioritizes the conservation and restoration of the country's ecosystems.

5. Gender equality

Considering that women are key players in all economic sectors of the country, including the production and marketing of agricultural and fishery products—activities that are directly impacted by climate change and climate variability—adaptation and mitigation measures to climate change will incorporate all the rights enshrined in the various national legal instruments on gender equality. Nicaragua has a Law on Equal Rights and Opportunities (Law No. 648, approved on February 14, 2008)16, which establishes six guidelines on environmental matters, directing the incorporation of a gender perspective as a cross-cutting theme in the country's environmental policies.

6. Citizen participation

Climate change adaptation and mitigation measures will incorporate the participation of the general population, especially the most vulnerable, under the family and community model promoted by the country.

7. Feasibility of the measures

The measures must be carefully evaluated to determine their economic, social, environmental, and climate viability, so that the goals proposed in the policy can be achieved at the lowest possible cost and generate the greatest possible benefits.

8. Climate Finance

The public and private sectors will work together to mobilize financial resources to support climate change mitigation and adaptation.

9. Recognition of native peoples and indigenous communities

Climate change particularly affects the habits, customs, and traditions of Indigenous and Afro-descendant peoples. Within this framework, their full participation and respect for their communal property rights, as established in Law No. 445, will be guaranteed. Likewise, national strategies, such as Reducing Emissions from Deforestation and Forest Degradation (REDD+), will comply with environmental and social safeguards to ensure the inclusion of these peoples in the sustainable management of natural resources.

VII. Nicaragua's commitments to improving the metrics of its NDCs

Climate change is one of the greatest challenges of our time, with effects on a global scale. Faced with alarming climate scenarios, in 2015, through a legally binding international agreement, the parties to the UNFCCC adopted the Country Agreement17. The Agreement entered into force in 2016 and its objective is to “hold the increase in the global average temperature to well below 2°C above pre-industrial levels and pursue efforts to limit the temperature increase to 1.5°C above pre-industrial levels, recognizing that this would substantially reduce the risks and impacts of climate change.”

To achieve this goal, countries proposed reaching peak GHG emissions as soon as possible so that the planet will have a climate-neutral climate by mid-century. This commitment is set out in Article 4, which also encourages each Party to prepare, communicate, and maintain its planned successive nationally determined contributions. Parties will endeavor to adopt domestic mitigation measures to achieve the objectives of these contributions.

In this regard, Nicaragua joined the Paris Agreement in 2017, reinforcing its commitment to the fight against climate change and its effects, considering that the Agreement is a global instrument that will allow for unity of intentions and efforts to address climate change and disasters.

7.2 Land Use, Land Use Change and Forestry Sector

The study on the Causes of Deforestation (MARENA, 2019) indicates that during 1983–2000, Nicaragua's forest cover decreased by 2.8 million hectares, an average of 170,000 hectares per year. The expansion of agricultural land advanced in the mountain ranges of the northeastern central region and in the Caribbean lowlands. Annual deforestation continued until 2005; however, the period of highest net deforestation was likely the 1990s. The study reflects that, according to official maps of the country, gross deforestation in the period 2005–2015 was 147,000 hectares, showing a slight decrease compared to the period 1983–2000. Deforestation and forest degradation in Nicaragua are caused by various direct and indirect agents, including demographic factors (population growth, migration) and biophysical factors (climate, soils, deposits, water supply, presence of fine woods and accessibility) (Figure 15).

The estimated baseline for assessing progress in meeting the NDC is the Forest Emission Reference Levels for the period 2005–2015, which constitute a comparative basis for monitoring the performance of activities carried out in the country to reduce emissions caused by gross deforestation. The following assumptions were considered to establish the baseline:

  • Only two carbon reservoirs were considered: aboveground and belowground biomass.
  • The country's definition of forest for NREF-N and the REDD+ mechanism is: A continuous area, equal to or greater than 1 hectare, with a tree cover equal to or greater than 30%, and an average tree height greater than 4 meters. This definition includes bamboo ecosystems, mangroves, natural palms, dry forests, riparian vegetation, and permanent crops with shade trees.
  • According to this definition, areas covered with natural secondary vegetation and/or isolated trees, whether or not combined with agricultural areas, that do not reach the minimum of 30% canopy cover, such as: scrublands, wooded pastures, natural savannas, wetlands, or open woodlands, are not considered forests.
  • Gross deforestation: any set of changes from one type of vegetation on forest land to another type of land use and vegetation on non-forest land (pastureland, cropland, wetlands, settlements, or other land). Natural or induced forest gain or restoration is not considered.
  • Forest degradation: the decrease in biomass due to a loss of canopy cover between 30% and 69%, which is associated with anthropogenic disturbances. Forest degradation involves the partial loss of woody vegetation cover resulting from a disturbance that does not imply a permanent change in land use from forest to non-forest. Emissions due to forest degradation are estimated in forest lands that remain as such.

7.3 Industrial Processes and Product Use Sector

Nicaraguan industry has undergone several stages of development over the decades. In the 1950s, the transformation of traditional products began, while in the 1960s industries oriented towards the Central American Common Market were established, and in the 1970s, industry experienced significant growth, positioning itself as the third largest in Central America. However, in the 1980s, it suffered a setback due to the imposed trade embargo and the closure of export industries, which presented an opportunity to strengthen small industries focused on the domestic market. In the 1990s, market liberalization and tariff reductions boosted exports, but also increased imports, impacting local industries, which became less competitive against foreign technological goods (MIFIC, 2010).

The industrial sector in Nicaragua represents 14.04% of the Gross Domestic Product (GDP), according to data from the Central Bank of Nicaragua (BCN, 2022). However, its profile does not include advanced transformation activities using chemical or physical processes, such as those related to the chemical, metallurgical, electronics, or specialized product manufacturing industries. Therefore, emissions from these activities are not included in Nicaragua's National Emissions and Gas Inventory (INGEI).

Despite this, the industrial sector has shown sustained growth, driven primarily by manufacturing and non-metallic mining. These activities have been key to productive diversification and economic expansion. Within manufacturing, subsectors such as food, beverages, and textiles stand out, having contributed significantly to both employment and exports. According to the Central Bank of Nicaragua (BCN), in 2022, this group of activities experienced 5.1% growth compared to 2021, reflecting an increasing capacity to meet national and international demand for goods.

However, there are activities that drive the national economy and are considered in the INGEI (National Statistics Index) such as non-metallic mining, specifically activities like the production of cement, lime, and the consumption of carbonates. The latter are characterized by artisanal production methods and open-pit operations, while cement production is carried out by two main industrial plants: CEMEX and HOLCIM, which are responsible for a significant portion of the country's industrial emissions.

Regarding the national manufacturing industry, which includes key activities such as food and beverage production, it registered a growth of 5.1% in 2022 compared to 2021. This performance underscores the strategic importance of the sector as an economic engine of Nicaragua, since it not only supplies the national demand for essential products, but also boosts exports, thus strengthening the trade balance and generating employment in the country (BCN, 2023).

On the other hand, the use of non-energy products, such as asphalt in road infrastructure, an activity also considered in the INGEI (National Energy and Gas Index), is a significant one. According to the Ministry of Transport and Infrastructure (MTI), in 2022, 2,344.34 kilometers of paved roads were rehabilitated and 40.69 kilometers of new roads were built, totaling 24,781.60 kilometers in the national road network, of which 2,581.35 kilometers are paved with asphalt. This sector, although not the largest emitter, illustrates the importance of non-energy industrial materials in the development of national infrastructure.

Another activity considered in this sector is the consumption of substances that replace ozone-depleting substances, also known as non-ODS substances. This activity is of particular interest to Nicaragua's NDC, which includes a target for reducing the consumption of non-ODS substances, in accordance with the Kigali Amendment. HFC consumption for 2022 was estimated at 279,409.60 ktCO2eq, the majority of which (99.78%) is consumed in refrigeration and air conditioning activities. The remaining 0.22% is consumed in fire protection and aerosols.

VIII. NDC Communication Strategy for Inclusive and Participatory Development

The Government of Nicaragua implements a Communication Policy based on three fundamental principles: the social right to information and freedom of expression, strict adherence to the Political Constitution of Nicaragua—in its latest reform—, and the citizens' right to participate actively in public decisions.

In the process of updating the NDCs, communication with the most vulnerable sectors was strengthened, expanding the dialogue with women, youth, indigenous peoples, and Afro-descendants at the national level. This process was developed with a multisectoral and multicultural approach, involving agricultural producers, universities, regional and national government institutions, media outlets, social organizations, and the general population.

The Communication Plan will continue to be implemented under the model of alliances, dialogue, and consensus, through the establishment of technical communication working groups with the participation of communicators from regional governments, representatives of the Indigenous Territorial Governments (GTI), and the Secretariat for the Development of the Caribbean Coast.

During the implementation of the NDC, the communication strategy will be guided by the Communication Policy of the Government of Reconciliation and National Unity, promoting national campaigns for values education and positioning families as key players in the process of fulfilling climate commitments.

Furthermore, inter-institutional collaboration will be encouraged to generate synergies that allow reaching a greater number of stakeholders, using both conventional media (radio, print media, television, digital portals) and social networks, to disseminate information on progress and challenges in the implementation of the NDCs in an agile and effective manner.

IX. Proposal for Updating the NDCs

According to the IPCC 1.5 report and the sense of urgency with which it was presented, which highlights the need for accelerated and immediate action to limit the increase in global temperature to 1.5°C, Nicaragua has developed a proposal to increase the ambition of its NDCs focused on climate change mitigation and adaptation.

According to the NDC submitted by Nicaragua to the UNFCCC in 2018, the main efforts are focused on increasing the energy matrix to 60% with renewable energy sources and reducing emissions from deforestation and forest degradation by 20% by 2030.

The Government of Nicaragua, in its strategic development plans and national policies, has directed actions that contribute to the protection, care, rational and sustainable use of the natural resources provided by Mother Earth. Therefore, progress has been made up to 2020 in increasing the share of renewable energy sources in the energy matrix to 59%; national capacities for monitoring, reporting, and verification of forest resources have been strengthened; reforestation targets have been increased; areas undergoing natural regeneration have been monitored and protected; surveillance systems within protected areas have been strengthened; strategic alliances with indigenous and Afro-descendant communities have been strengthened; among other achievements.

Considering the above, a proposal to increase the ambition of the NDC with respect to GHG mitigation from the country's productive sectors is described below.

9.1 Proposal for increased ambition in Mitigation

9.1.1 Energy Sector

✓ Conditional: Increase the energy mix to 65% with renewable energy sources by 2030.

  • Increased ambition will contribute to reducing energy sector emissions compared to the 2018 baseline.
  • Required activities:
  • National study to identify potential areas for expanding renewable energy production.
  • Analysis of financial, technical, and technological gaps for increasing energy generation by 65%.

9.1.2 Land Use, Land Use Change and Forestry Sector

✓ Conditional NDC in the forestry and land-use change sector: Reduce CO2 emissions from gross deforestation at the national level by 25% by 2030 compared to the country baseline.

During 2020, Nicaragua designed projects aligned with and focused on the restoration and protection of forests; the establishment of sustainable and environmentally friendly production systems; and the strengthening of local and institutional capacities that guarantee forest protection.

It is considered to implement interventions that contribute to supporting indigenous communities and producers with robust capacities, technical assistance, inputs, and financial and market incentives for the sustainable intensification of their livelihoods, oriented towards the restoration and conservation of natural resources and forests on their farms and territories. These interventions are: 1) establishment of agroforestry systems; 2) establishment of silvopastoral systems; 3) management of natural regeneration; 4) community forest management; 5) commercial reforestation; and 6) improvement of forest governance.

Considering the mitigation potential of the aforementioned interventions, Nicaragua will increase its ambition in the forestry sector on the condition of receiving international financing for the implementation of programs and projects that contribute to achieving the country's goal.

Required activities:

  • Capacity building plan for institutions responsible for monitoring forests and land-use change.
  • Reduction of extensive livestock farming practices and increase of forest cover in the livestock sector.
  • Formulation of a Nationally Appropriate Mitigation Action (NAMA) strategy for sustainable, low-carbon, and climate-resilient cattle farming.
  • National Plan for the Management of GHG Emissions from Enteric Fermentation and Livestock Manure Management through Biodigesters
  • Design of an electromobility strategy for public transport in the city of Managua.

9.1.3 Industrial Processes and Product Use Sector

✓ Reduction of consumption of fluorinated gases considered in the Kigali amendment

Required activities:

  • Development of a strategy for the use of natural or low global warming potential refrigerants, which would have an impact on the industrial processes sector, specifically the subsector dealing with emissions from fluorinated substitutes that deplete the ozone layer.
