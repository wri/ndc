Prologue

Faced with the global climate crisis, Peru is raising its voice and its commitment. Our country, highly vulnerable to The effects of climate change cannot be ignored; we cannot be indifferent to the impacts that are already altering our world. ecosystems and the lives of our communities. In this context, Peru has strengthened its commitment to management comprehensive approach to climate change over time, formally consolidated with Law No. 30754, Framework Law on Climate Change (LMCC), enacted in 2018. This legislation not only establishes the principles for management participatory and transparent adaptation and mitigation measures, but also seeks to reduce our vulnerability, seizing low-carbon growth opportunities and fulfilling our commitments international.

To strengthen this long-term vision, the country approved and published the National Strategy in November 2024 in the face of Climate Change by 2050 as a national policy (National Policy: ENCC 2050). This instrument proposes an ambitious goal, to reduce by 30% the damage, losses and disruptions caused by climate hazards and achieve net emissions equal to zero by the year 2050 In line with these objectives, this update to our Nationally Determined Contribution (NDC) towards 2035 represents a determined effort to strengthen climate action, Despite the social, economic, and political challenges we face, it also constitutes a renewed commitment. of the country towards the goals of the Paris Agreement, with a progressive ambition in relation to the previous NDC (2020).

Peru's updated NDC (NDC 3.0) is the result of a participatory, multi-actor and multi-level technical process. Between October 2024 and August 2025, 7 regional workshops and 1 Amazonian macro-regional workshop were held; and 6 workshops with non-state actors that included the active participation of civil society, academia, indigenous organizations, the business sector, and women's groups, whose valuable input and opinions were collected and addressed in this communication.

Regarding adaptation, Peru has included the importance of addressing loss and damage as a crucial component, and has added two new thematic areas of intervention: internal displacement forced by effects of climate change and the promotion of green jobs. In this way, it aligns its commitment with the Global Adaptation Goal (GGA), in order to improve adaptive planning with a territorial approach, increase resilience and safeguard livelihoods.

In mitigation, close coordination work was carried out with key sectors to define the Actions necessary to achieve the decarbonization of the economy by 2050, through a just and sustainable energy transition. Based on the results and the recommendations of the First Global Stocktaking (GST) presented in 2023, this NDC 3.0 ensure its content is aligned with global climate ambition.

The new Peruvian NDC delves deeper into adaptation and mitigation actions, integrating biodiversity as a pillar A key element of our climate response, it contributes to the fight against desertification and land degradation. As an essential part of sustainable ecosystem management, it incorporates commitments at the territorial level Through the updating of the Regional Climate Change Strategies (ERCC), it establishes tangible actions In terms of implementation, it promotes climate transparency and encourages stakeholder participation state and non-state actors through comprehensive climate change management.

We are convinced that collective climate action is the only real and effective alternative to accelerate a climate-resilient and low-carbon development, placing present and future generations at the center of our climate response.

Climate overview of the country

Peru maintains its firm commitment to the United Nations Framework Convention on Climate Change (UNFCCC) and the Agreement of Paris (AP), reaffirming its principles and objectives. In this five-year NDC update cycle, the country It projects a comprehensive vision that allows it to address the impacts and effects of climate change, promoting the progressive reduction of greenhouse gases (GHG). In this way, Peru contributes to the global effort to stabilize the temperature, in accordance with the principle of common responsibilities but differentiated and with respective capabilities.

Peru's NDC 3.0 builds on the achievements made since the second contribution cycle and defines a A roadmap to 2035 to strengthen national climate action. This commitment guides the country towards a low-carbon and climate-resilient development by 2050, through a just and inclusive transition with a a gender perspective that promotes equality and contributes to reducing structural inequalities, social gaps and economic.

This third NDC from Peru represents a significant advance over previous versions, by incorporating concrete actions in adaptation, loss and damage management, and mitigation; as well as in cross-cutting issues (means of implementation, governance, territory and transparency) that strengthen climate action.

In terms of adaptation, Peru will strengthen its resilience in nine priority thematic areas —water, agriculture, fishing and aquaculture, forestry, health, tourism, transport, forced internal displacement due to the effects of climate change and green jobs—, under a territorial and synergistic approach, integrating the action of the three levels of government (national, regional and local), together with non-state actors and indigenous peoples; likewise, recognizes the importance of addressing the losses and damages associated with climate change.

In terms of mitigation, Peru reaffirms its commitment to sustainably reduce its GHG emissions in all sectors of the economy, according to their national characteristics, for which it describes the actions necessary for achieving decarbonization, with the goal of reaching net-zero emissions by 2050, in in line with the National Policy: ENCC 2050, including a just and sustainable energy transition, system of Integrated public transport, sustainable waste management, promotion of the circular economy approach, implementation of programs aimed at reducing deforestation and promoting the sustainable management of forest ecosystems, among other actions.

The NDC also highlights the close interdependence between climate action and biodiversity, seeking the synergy with the objectives of the Convention on Diversity

Biological and the Kunming-Montreal Global Biodiversity Framework. This contributes directly to the National Biodiversity Strategy to 20501, whose objective is that by that year the country will have achieved a significant decrease in land-use change of its terrestrial and aquatic ecosystems, thanks to the reduction of 50% of deforestation in the regions and the restoration of 30% of degraded ecosystems.

Furthermore, the country recognizes that desertification, land degradation and drought (DDTS) processes represent One of the main challenges in the face of climate change. Within this framework, the national climate vision incorporates the commitment to move towards land degradation neutrality by 2030, consistent with the commitments of the United Nations Convention to Combat Desertification (UNCCD) and the Action Plan National Plan to Combat Desertification, Land Degradation and Drought by 2030 (PLANLCDDTS 2030), integrating actions for both adaptation and mitigation.

Peru's commitment in its NDC 3.0 is aligned and consistent with the objectives of the Strategic Plan of National Development Plan 2050 (PEDN 2050), where climate change is a priority to guarantee well-being, the dignity and sustainable development of the population, in conjunction with poverty reduction, equal opportunities, sustainable ecosystem management and the promotion of decent and competitive employment.

To achieve these objectives, the NDC considers six interrelated components: adaptation, mitigation, means of implementation, climate transparency, governance and territory; which together represent the vision national climate towards 2035 and 2050.

National Circumstances

Our country has climatic, physiographic, geological and ecological characteristics that give it a A significant variety of ecosystems and biological diversity. Featuring strategic landscapes with functions and services that allow the maintenance of ecosystem cycles and processes not only in Peruvian territory, but beyond of its borders, which defines its global importance3.

Thus, the country boasts a vast landscape of the Peruvian Amazon, with a tropical rainforest that produces and supplies large quantities of water to all of South America, through enormous rain clouds or “flying rivers”, influencing in the rainfall across the region and the stabilization of the global climate. In addition, there are the landscapes of mountains located in the Andes mountain range, related to 74% of the country's watersheds and concentrating 68% of the world's tropical glaciers. Meanwhile, the Peruvian sea, with 3,080 km of coastline and 200 The nautical miles comprise approximately 1,140,646.8 km2, with a high biodiversity that allows it to provide food and non-food services and products at the national and global level4.

On the other hand, the National Biodiversity Strategy to 2050 (NBS) identifies an increase in the degradation of biological diversity as the major problem to be faced, also highlighting its important Interrelation with cultural diversity. The variation of climatic patterns is one of the direct causes. identified for this problem, encompassing variables such as the increase in GHG emissions and the alteration of the hydrological cycles that affect the functions of ecosystems.

The close connection between climate change and biodiversity loss, coupled with persistent Social inequalities make Peru particularly vulnerable to climate change. In fact, the country It presents seven of the nine characteristics of developing countries that, according to the UNFCCC, warrant specific needs due to the adverse effects of the phenomenon.

In addition, the National Strategy to Combat Desertification and Drought (ENLCDS), which is implemented through the National Action Plan to Combat Desertification, Land Degradation and Drought by 2030 (PLANLCDDTS 2030), identifies land degradation and drought events as critical factors that They affect ecosystem productivity and rural livelihoods. This instrument is linked to the National Policy: ENCC 2050 and the NDCs, promoting actions for restoration, sustainable land use and management of the risk from drought.

Dangers, scenarios and risks associated with climate change

Dangers associated with climate change and its impacts

The increase in greenhouse gas concentrations has altered the natural variability of the climate. in Peru, causing extreme weather events to occur more frequently and with greater intensity, even in areas where they were not common before. Among them are the dangers of sudden development, such as rain intense events, floods and heat waves; and those slow-developing hazards such as prolonged droughts, the glacial retreat, among others. Both types of hazards generate increasing impacts on the population, the ecosystems and productive activities.

The sustained increase in global temperature is also reflected in the national territory. In 2024, Peru It recorded its warmest year on record, with an average annual temperature of 20.29 °C, representing a An anomaly of +1.18 °C compared to the normal average of 19.11 °C. This record surpassed the one reached in 2023 and was not directly linked to the El Niño phenomenon, evidencing the structural warming trend.

The effects of these climate changes are occurring with increasing frequency and intensity, through Events such as the 2016-2017 Coastal El Niño, which caused estimated infrastructure damage equivalent to 1.9% of the national GDP 26 Cyclone Yaku of 2023 or the forest fires that have intensified in recent years, having a A significant peak in 2024. These events have caused serious damage in critical sectors, coinciding with several of the priority thematic areas for adaptation in the country. The magnitude of these impacts demonstrates the need to strengthen adaptation actions and access climate finance mechanisms timely international cooperation that allows for reducing the country's vulnerability.

Regarding the thematic area of water, climatic phenomena have highlighted the vulnerability in relation to water resources and related infrastructure. During the 2017 Coastal El Niño, damage was recorded in more than 22,900 kilometers of irrigation canals, affecting the distribution of water for agricultural irrigation, in addition the destruction of more than 260,000 homes, which limited access to drinking water in various regions of the country 26. In turn, the intense rains linked to Cyclone Yaku in 2023 left more than 103,000 homes affected, of which of which more than 5,700 were destroyed, between January and April alone,27, hindering access to drinking water for extended periods.

Agriculture, a key thematic area for food security in rural communities, was also seen heavily affected by the events associated with the 2017 Coastal El Niño, which caused the loss or damage of more than 77,000 hectares of crops26, while in 2023 the rains associated with Cyclone Yaku affected more than 45,000 hectares of crops, of of which more than 25,000 were completely lost, in addition to causing the loss of more than 46,000 animals of parenting27.

Meanwhile, the impacts on public health have been severe and increasing. During Cyclone Yaku in 2023, As a result of the heavy rains, more than 300 health facilities were affected in regions such as Piura, Tumbes and Lambayeque, including the destruction of 10 of these establishments. Also, during 2023, 398 deaths from dengue were reported, with a total of 230,209 cases. confirmed and probable cases, representing a 378% increase in cases compared to the same period in 2022 and of 269% compared to 2017, the year of the Coastal El Niño that produced the largest dengue epidemic in the history of the country28.

Regarding the thematic area of forests, 2024 was a year in which Peru faced one of the worst seasons of the most critical forest fires in recent years. According to the National Information System for the Response and Rehabilitation (SINPAD), 1754 forest fires were recorded nationwide, being September The most affected month, with a peak of 840 incidents 29. The department of Cusco had the highest number of events with 402, followed by Huánuco (198), Cajamarca (184), Apurímac (151) and Áncash (143), while twenty other departments of the country concentrated an additional 676 fires; This resulted in an estimated destruction of 63,710 hectares of natural cover and the loss of 14,972 hectares of crops, in addition to 285 people injured and 35 dead29.

The 2017 Coastal El Niño generated changes in oceanographic conditions that altered the biological processes of pelagic species, causing a significant decrease in marine productivity off the northern coast and central Peruvian coastline, altering conditions for artisanal and industrial fishing. For example, the anchovy, a keystone species of the Peruvian marine ecosystem, has changed its population and distribution, even showing the absence of reproductive activity and spawning during the summer30.

The national transportation system also suffers recurring damage during extreme events, such as El Niño Costero. In 2017, more than 8,700 kilometers of roads were reported affected, as well as damage to 822 bridges nationwide. leading to an increase in logistical costs and hindering the humanitarian response to these events.

Climate risks

The NAP defined the conceptual framework for how the country addresses the risks associated with climate change. In accordance with the Intergovernmental Panel on Climate Change (IPCC), the analysis Climate risk assessment was structured according to the levels of hazard, exposure, and vulnerability for each subject and prioritized thematic area.

Under this conceptual framework, five key thematic areas for adaptation were identified - water, agriculture, forestry, fishing and aquaculture, and health—each with adaptation measures. Since then, the country has been implementing monitoring and evaluation processes, aimed at determining the real effectiveness of the measures and their contribution to risk reduction.

Within the framework of updating this NDC, and based on the inter-institutional dialogue between the entities Those responsible for generating information and implementing adaptation measures have been added. Scientific and technical evidence. In particular, the Climate Risk and Adaptation Measures Study for Hazards and priority sectors in Peru, prepared by MINAM and the IDB within the framework of the National Policy: ECC 2050 and the NAP allowed for the analysis of new hazards and additional thematic areas, expanding the scientific information for national adaptive action.

The aforementioned study worked on climate risk modeling for seven thematic areas, under a catastrophic approach from which the current level of risk was determined, as well as the risk that would exist by 2030 and 2050, under different greenhouse gas emissions trajectories, RCP 2.6 and RCP 8.5. The exposure was defined based on the sectoral characterization of elements at risk (productive, infrastructure and environment-forests-ecosystems), while vulnerability was modeled in a differentiated way according to the type of losses: economic in agriculture and livestock, physical in infrastructure (health, roads and sanitation) and ecosystem services in the environmental sector33.

According to the study, climate change can, in some cases, exacerbate the risk of losses from disasters. associated with hydrometeorological events significantly, and in other cases reduces it, depending on the hazard, exposure, vulnerability, and future climate that are taken into consideration; furthermore, even under the current climate, the Peru faces high levels of disaster risk, demonstrating the vulnerability of the territory, which may increase under the effect of climate change34.

The aim was to obtain risk assessments that could express the potential impact of climate change on in a more quantitative way (in terms of expected annual loss and probable maximum loss curves). With this, the The country seeks to generate more information for decision-making regarding adaptation and the effectiveness of the measures identified and the new prioritized areas.

In this context, climate change not only poses potential risks, but is already causing losses and Damages within the national territory. These losses and damages range from material damage to homes, infrastructure and production in various climate-sensitive economic sectors, up to and including irreversible loss of glaciers, biodiversity, livelihoods, as well as traditional practices and knowledge, particularly in rural communities and indigenous or native peoples, Afro-Peruvian population and other ethno-cultural groups in situation of exposure and vulnerability to the effects of climate change.

The scenario described reinforces the urgency of implementing comprehensive, multi-sectoral adaptation measures and multilevel, aligned with the NAP, the List35 of Adaptation Measures of the NDC and the commitments assumed by Peru within the framework of the UNFCCC and the Paris Agreement, including its connection to the global debate on loss and damage.

Climate change scenarios

Faced with changes in temperature and precipitation patterns, which generate a higher frequency and In the intensity of extreme events, the National Meteorology and Hydrology Service (SENAMHI) plays a role key in the generation and updating of climate change scenarios at the national, regional and by levels river basins. The country, through SENAMHI, strengthened its technical capacities through the incorporation of a high-performance computing (HPC) system called “NUNA”, which allows for the performance of climate simulations with greater speed, accuracy and spatial resolution.

Therefore, for the current year, the updated climate scenarios for Peru are made available. focused on the year 2050, developed from the most recent simulations of the CMIP6 climate models, with dynamic and statistical scaling down, under the high emissions scenario SSP5-8.5. These Projections include precipitation, maximum and minimum temperature variables, at a spatial resolution of 5 km. In addition, a Multi-Hazard Climate Index is presented that allows the identification of areas with greater exposure to the climate impacts.

The use of CMIP6 climate models in updating climate change scenarios represents a major advance in the representation of the climate system, since this latest version of the global models has enhanced the ability to quantify and attribute the factors behind climate change, thanks to the integration of cutting-edge science and technology that facilitates the creation and distribution of simulations advanced climate plans. Likewise, the application of current emission scenarios, called trajectories. Shared Socioeconomics (SSP) allows for the generation of plausible climate change scenarios that reflect socioeconomic and environmental conditions, as well as the challenges of implementing mitigation policies and adaptation.

Adaptation component

The Paris Agreement sets a long-term mitigation goal: to keep the increase in average temperature to a minimum. global “well below 2°C” above pre-industrial levels, and continue efforts to limit it to 1.5 °C. To achieve this goal, the Agreement urges countries (the Parties) to take measures based on equity and in the context of sustainable development, proposing: i) to reach a peak emissions point global GHG emissions as soon as possible; and ii) rapidly reduce emissions to achieve a balance between the emissions generated and absorption by sinks.

To make progress towards these goals, countries must submit their NDCs every five years, as stipulated in the Articles 4.2 and 4.9. In addition, the Agreement suggests that countries develop and communicate long-term strategies for low GHG development (Article 4.19), ensuring that each new NDC is more ambitious than the previous one (Article 4.3). This principle is based on common but differentiated responsibilities and the capabilities of each nation.

Additionally, the First GST reinforces the urgency of these actions, establishing the need for reductions deep, rapid and sustained reductions in GHG emissions to align with the 1.5°C trajectories.

Peru's commitment to reducing hydrofluorocarbon (HFC) substances

Peru has demonstrated its firm commitment to protecting the ozone layer and mitigating climate change. climate change by ratifying the Montreal Protocol in 1993. A global agreement that seeks to reduce and eliminate the use of ozone-depleting chemicals, primarily refrigerants found in equipment refrigeration, freezing, and air conditioning systems. In this context, Peru has exceeded its reduction targets, reaching 52% of a reduction by 2024, exceeding the 35% commitment. As a next commitment to eliminating the use of HCFCs, the country expects to achieve a 67.5% reduction by 2025-2029.

Through the Ministry of Production (PRODUCE), the country has implemented various regulations to comply with these commitments. However, the phase-out of HCFCs led to the mass adoption of HFCs, whose high impact on the Global warming forced new regulations. Therefore, in 2019 Peru ratified the Kigali Amendment, which expands the Protocol of Montreal to include the phase-down of HFCs.

Reducing these high-global-warming-potential gases has become a national priority. The country has incorporated this goal into its updated NDC, thus integrating the efforts of the Montreal Protocol into its national strategy against climate change and has issued a regulatory provision66 to reduce consumption of HFC by 30% by 2035 and by 80% by 2045.

It is important to note that the country is not a producer of these substances (HCFCs and HFCs), but rather consumes them through the import.

National Mitigation Commitment (NMC):

The Peruvian State commits to ensuring that its greenhouse gas emissions do not exceed 179 MtCO2eq in by 2035, seeking a progressive and fair reduction, adapted to national capacities and circumstances; and conditional upon international external financing under favorable conditions.

3. Scope and coverage

(a) General description of the goal.

In accordance with the principle of progress and non-regression established in the Paris Agreement, Peru's NDC 3.0 sets a new national mitigation target for 2035, establishing a goal of 179 MtCO₂eq. This goal, which in NDC 2.0 had an aspirational character, maintains its conditionality with respect to the availability of international climate finance, technical cooperation and technology transfer, essential elements to guarantee its implementation and sustainability.

Furthermore, this third communication incorporates specific sectoral commitments (CM1 to CM7), which They reflect the country's priority lines of action and the ongoing efforts of the various sectors in mitigation measures. These commitments strengthen the national goal, making it more ambitious, coherence and institutional support.

(b) Sectors, gases, categories and reservoirs covered by the nationally determined contribution, that, where appropriate, conform to the guidelines of the Intergovernmental Panel on Climate Change (IPCC).

NDC 3.0 maintains the same sectoral and GHG coverage as that presented in 2020 (Second NDC).

The GHG emission sectors included, consistent with the INGEI, are: i) Energy; ii) Processes Industrial and Product Use (PIUP); iii) Agriculture; iv) Land Use, Land Use Change and Forestry (UTCUTS); and, v) Waste.

The GHGs considered are: carbon dioxide (CO2), methane (CH4), nitrous oxide (N2O), hydrofluorocarbons (HFC), perfluorocarbons (PFC) and sulfur hexafluoride (SF6).

(g) The intention to resort to voluntary cooperation under Article 6 of the Paris Agreement, of correspond.

In accordance with its 2020 Nationally Determined Contribution (NDC), Peru reaffirms its commitment to resort to voluntary cooperation under Article 6 of the Paris Agreement.

This intention was formalized with the publication of Supreme Decree No. 010-2024-MINAM in November 2024, that approves the Provisions for the operation of the National Registry of Mitigation Measures (RENAMI). RENAMI is the main tool of the Peruvian State for monitoring the actions that comprise the NDC and projects linked to the carbon market, guaranteeing the environmental integrity and transparency in GHG mitigation results.

With this regulation, Peru establishes the necessary institutional arrangements for the authorization and monitoring of International Transfer Mitigation Outcomes (ITMOs), in compliance with the provisions of Article 6 of the Paris Agreement.

With the aim of strategically directing part of the climate finance towards Article 6, the RENAMI regulations empower sectoral authorities to approve a indicative list of types of priority activities in the carbon market (Eleventh Final Supplementary Provision). Currently, the Carbon Markets Working Group of the High-Level Commission on Climate Change It is developing agreed-upon criteria that will allow sectoral authorities to identify the types of activities corresponding to each sector, for subsequent publication in an indicative list.

Peru recognizes that the carbon market can boost national competitiveness and productivity through the mobilization of financial resources, the transfer of knowledge, and the incorporation of sustainable technologies in key sectors such as energy efficiency in industry and commerce, the sustainable ecosystem management, the circular economy, family farming, transport clean, among other things.

Within that framework, and as part of their voluntary participation in cooperative approaches in accordance with the According to RENAMI regulations, Peru estimates a minimum use of 9 million tCO2e as ITMO, in order to to promote greater ambition in the adaptation and mitigation measures that make up the NDC, as well as in the sustainable development and environmental integrity at the national level.

Regarding Non-Market Approaches (NMA), contemplated in paragraph 8 of Article 6, Peru has preparing the necessary institutional conditions and arrangements to identify and report the NMA activities through the UNFCCC Platform.

In this way, Peru continues to strengthen its national and multilateral processes, ensuring a robust, transparent and ambitious implementation of Article 6 of the Paris Agreement, consistent with the increased ambition of its climate goals.
