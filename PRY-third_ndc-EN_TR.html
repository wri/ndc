PRESENTATION

Paraguay presents its Third Nationally Determined Contribution (NDC 3.0) and its Second Adaptation Communication (2CA) as a reaffirmation of its commitment to climate action within the framework of the United Nations Framework Convention on Climate Change (CMNUCC) and the Paris Agreement.

Paraguay's Climate Plan (NDC), within the framework of the Paris Agreement, recognizes the climate crisis and projects a vision to 2035 aimed at building sustainable and resilient development, based on scientific evidence and consistent with the outcomes of the 2023 Global Stocktake (COP28). It also stresses that climate measures must be implemented in a fair and balanced manner, avoiding the creation of barriers to international trade. This NDC 3.0 reaffirms Paraguay's commitment to a sustainable, resilient and inclusive development model that integrates economic growth with ecosystem protection, productive innovation and climate justice, thereby strengthening its contribution to global climate action.

Recognizing its high vulnerability to extreme events such as droughts, forest fires and floods, Paraguay proposes to advance climate action while guaranteeing the fundamental rights of the population —health, education, decent employment and social protection—driving a transition towards a diversified economy based on knowledge, innovation and multilateral cooperation.

At the centre of this vision are ecosystems and natural resources, which constitute a strategic advantage for the country and are fundamental for people-centred climate action.

Mitigation

In this NDC 3.0, Paraguay reaffirms its commitment to promoting the consolidation of its sustainable climate contribution, reinforcing its role in global climate action with strengthened ambition aligned with its national capacities. This contribution is underpinned by the protection of strategic ecosystems, good production practices and the promotion of a sustainable and resilient economy. Paraguay aspires to make a net favourable contribution to the stabilization of the global climate, with a target equivalent to 10 % relative to the Business as Usual scenario (BaU) in 2030 and 2035 with its own resources, and an additional contribution of 10 % conditional on access to means of implementation such as finance, capacity-building and technology transfer.

With this, the country reaffirms that its climate action is based on fair ambition, strategic alliances and nature-based solutions, actively contributing to the collective global effort.

To achieve these objectives, Paraguay has defined 27 climate action measures in the sectors of Agriculture and Livestock (4 measures), Land Use, Land-Use Change and Forestry (7 measures), Energy and Transport (6 measures), Industrial Processes and Product Use (5 measures), Waste (4 measures) and Cross-cutting (1 measure).

These actions prioritize the conservation of natural sinks, productive efficiency, technological innovation and the strengthening of sustainable systems.

Adaptation

The Second Adaptation Communication (2CA) marks a milestone in Paraguay's national efforts by presenting a set of 25 objectives and 106 lines of action for the seven priority sectors: Resilient Cities and Communities; Health and Epidemiology; Ecosystems and Biodiversity; Energy; Agriculture and Livestock, Forestry and Food Security; Water Resources; and Transport.

This process introduces a more structured approach to adaptation planning, updating the commitments defined in the First Adaptation Communication under SMART criteria, with targets to be implemented in the 2026–2035 period.

In addition, national adaptation action is articulated in a coordinated manner with the three Rio Conventions, promoting an integrated and coherent vision.

Means of Implementation

Paraguay presents progress and challenges in capacity-building, climate finance and technology development and transfer. The country has promoted technical training, environmental education and citizen participation through initiatives driven by the Ministry of Environment and Sustainable Development (MADES) and the Ministry of Education and Sciences (MEC), strengthening institutional and community competencies in line with the Action for Climate Empowerment of the CMNUCC.

Likewise, the role of the Ministry of Economy and Finance (MEF) was consolidated as a key actor in the management of climate resources, steering efforts towards greater access to international finance, where the Finance Monitoring, Reporting and Verification system will be a key strategy.

In a complementary manner, Paraguay promotes technological cooperation through the Technology Action Plan (TAP). With this, the country reaffirms its commitment to a resilient and just development model.

1. INTRODUCTION

1.1. PARAGUAY'S VISION TO 2035

Paraguay reaffirms its commitment to the CMNUCC and the Paris Agreement by presenting its third Nationally Determined Contribution (NDC 3.0), with a country vision for the year 2035. A vision of sustainable and inclusive development that recognizes the urgency of acting in the face of the climate crisis, while building a resilient, low-emission and people-centred growth model. In this NDC, Paraguay projects its future as a cohesive nation, with a transparent and effective State, a competitive economy and an environmentally sustainable and climate-resilient territory.

Paraguay faces climate change on the basis of the National Constitution, which guarantees the preservation, conservation, restoration and improvement of the environment, as well as its reconciliation with integral human development, with the full enjoyment of fundamental rights and respect for the principles of human dignity.

The principle of common but differentiated responsibility (CBDR), enshrined in key international instruments such as the Rio Declaration (1992), the United Nations Framework Convention on Climate Change (UNFCCC, 1992) and the Paris Agreement (2015), is fundamental for guiding and mobilizing global climate policy.

Recent experience has highlighted the country's growing vulnerability to extreme climate phenomena such as droughts, forest fires and floods, which disproportionately affect the most vulnerable communities. These impacts reinforce the need to accelerate a structural transformation that allows progress towards a more just, resilient and inclusive society, in harmony with nature and aligned with the principles of sustainable development.

Paraguay's vision for 2035 is that of a country that guarantees the fundamental rights of its population, in particular access to health, education and decent employment, within a solid social protection system. This vision is based on the promotion of human capacities, the reduction of inequalities and the overcoming of poverty in all its forms, understanding that social well-being and climate justice are inseparable pillars of climate resilience.

With an integrated government, economy and society approach, Paraguay is driving a transition towards an innovative, diversified and competitive economy that generates added value on the basis of knowledge, digitalization, sustainable investment and respect for the environment.

Paraguay is moving towards a sustainable and resilient future

The country recognizes that climate action must be collective and transformative, incorporating both technical and scientific knowledge and traditional and ancestral knowledge, especially of indigenous peoples and rural communities.

At the centre of this transformation are our ecosystems and natural resources, which constitute a strategic advantage for development. Paraguay is moving towards a renewable energy model, sustainable agriculture, more resilient cities and transparent environmental governance.

The restoration of forests and landscapes, the protection of biodiversity and ecosystem-based adaptation are part of a new synergy between economy, nature and society.

Paraguay's NDC 3.0 represents a synthesis of the country's determination to contribute responsibly to the global effort against climate change.

It reflects Paraguay's confidence in its people, in their capacity to innovate, cooperate and lead solutions, and in its vocation as an open, supportive country committed to effective multilateralism.

From its active role in international negotiations and its commitment to the implementation of the Paris Agreement, Paraguay extends an invitation to the international community to revitalize cooperation, promote fair and equitable climate finance, and ensure that no one is left behind in building a common, resilient and sustainable, regenerative and circular future.

1.2. NATIONAL CIRCUMSTANCES

1.2.1. Government Structure

Paraguay is a democratic, representative, participatory and pluralist Republic, organized within the framework of the social State governed by the rule of law, as established in the National Constitution promulgated on 20 June 1992. The political organization of the country is based on the separation, independence and balance of the three Powers of the State: Executive, Legislative and Judicial.

Executive Power is exercised by the President of the Republic, who is elected by direct suffrage for a five-year term.

The President is accompanied in office by a Vice-President and is supported by the Executive Cabinet, composed of ministries and secretariats of the Executive Power.

Legislative Power resides in the National Congress, of bicameral structure, made up of the Chamber of Senators and the Chamber of Deputies. The Chamber of Senators is composed of 45 full members and 30 alternates, while the Chamber of Deputies is made up of 80 deputies and 80 alternates. All members of the National Congress are elected by direct vote for a five-year period.

Judicial Power is independent and is headed by the Supreme Court of Justice, composed of nine justices. The administration of justice is carried out through courts and tribunals established by the Constitution and the laws, with jurisdiction throughout the national territory.

1.2.2. Geographic and demographic profile

The Republic of Paraguay (in Guaraní, Tetã Paraguái) is a State without a maritime coastline, located in the south-centre of the South American continent. Its geographic location positions it strategically as a regional connection point, delimited between the parallels 19°18’ and 27°03’ south latitude and the meridians 54°15’ and 62°38’ west longitude1 . The Tropic of Capricorn crosses the country almost through its geographic centre. It shares borders with three countries: Argentina to the south, southeast and southwest; Brazil to the north and northeast; and Bolivia to the north and northwest. Its territorial boundaries total approximately 3.920 kilometres: 1.690 km with Argentina2 , 1.290 km with Brazil3 and 750 km with Bolivia.4

The national territory is divided by the Paraguay River into two large natural and administrative regions:

  • Eastern Region, which covers 39% of the territory, is characterized by its undulating topography, fertile soils and high water density. It is the most urbanized and developed area of the country, home to 14 of the 17 departments and the capital, Asunción. This region concentrates most of the population centres, health and educational infrastructure, transport networks and economic activity, in addition to remarkable ecological diversity.
  • Western Region or Chaco, which represents 61% of the national territory, is a vast alluvial plain with a semi-arid climate, lower population density and predominantly xerophytic vegetation cover. It comprises the departments of Presidente Hayes, Boquerón and Alto Paraguay. This region is key for biodiversity conservation and for resilient development strategies, due to its vulnerability to extreme climate phenomena.

The country is politically subdivided into 17 departments and a capital district, which are divided into 261 districts. This administrative structure facilitates the implementation of territorial public policies, including climate action adapted to regional conditions.

According to the most recent data available, Paraguay has an estimated population of 6.109.903 inhabitants, made up of 3.057.674 men and 3.052.229 women, reflecting a balanced distribution of men and women in the population. The population is mostly concentrated in the Eastern Region, especially in the metropolitan area of Asunción and the Central Department. The capital, Asunción, is the most populous city in the country, with approximately 462.246 inhabitants and more than 129.8065 registered dwellings, which evidences its role as a political, administrative, economic and cultural centre.

The Paraguayan population is predominantly young: approximately 60% is under 35 years of age, which represents a strategic opportunity for public policies oriented towards sustainable development, green employment and intergenerational climate empowerment.

1.2.3. Ethnic and cultural aspects

Paraguay is a multicultural and bilingual country, whose official languages are Spanish and Guaraní.

Paraguayan multiculturalism draws on native indigenous peoples, immigrants from different parts of the world, and a deep Hispanic-Guaraní heritage. The autochthonous populations belong to five linguistic families, the Guaraní being the most representative, as it accounts for 54% of the total indigenous population. This cultural richness is manifested in diverse social, spiritual and economic expressions.

According to official data, in Paraguay there are 140.2065 indigenous persons, distributed in 493 communities and belonging to 19 indigenous peoples. These communities are grouped into the following five linguistic families5:

  • Guaraní
  • Mataco-Mataguayo
  • Zamuco
  • Lengua-Maskoy
  • Guaicurú

Geographically, in the Eastern Region, the department with the largest indigenous population is Canindeyú (16.294 persons), while in the Western Region (Chaco), the department with the highest concentration is Presidente Hayes (29.814 persons)6.

1.2.4.Economic profile

Paraguay demonstrates solid macroeconomic performance, standing out for sustained economic growth above the regional average.

This evolution has been driven by good macroeconomic management and stable fiscal institutions. This combination has allowed the country to position itself as a relevant exporter in the agricultural, livestock and energy spheres, highlighting its participation in the international markets for soybeans, beef and renewable energy.

However, the concentration of the economy in primary sectors represents a structural challenge for macroeconomic resilience.

Currently, agricultural products, livestock and electricity account for about 80% of direct exports and approximately 17% of Gross Domestic Product (PIB)7, which could expose the country to vulnerabilities in the face of external shocks, extreme climate phenomena and fluctuations in international markets.

The combined impact of the COVID-19 pandemic and the prolonged droughts between 2020 and 2022 generated an economic slowdown. Even so, Paraguay managed to maintain important progress in social matters. Between 2003 and 2024, total poverty fell from 51,4% to 20,1%, and extreme poverty fell from 11,2% to 4,1%8.

Likewise, the Government of Paraguay has initiated a fiscal convergence strategy aimed at strengthening the sustainability of public finances. This strategy focuses on improving transparency, institutional consolidation and fiscal discipline. Between 2023 and 2024, the fiscal deficit was reduced, evidencing a firm commitment to macroeconomic stability.

Added to this are the agreements reached with the International Monetary Fund based on the Policy Coordination Instrument (PCI) and the Resilience and Sustainability Facility Programme (RSF), which strengthen the management of the country's macro-fiscal and climate risks.

During 2023, the Paraguayan economy experienced a robust recovery, with PIB growth9 that exceeded initial projections. This rebound was led by the good performance of agriculture, hydroelectric generation, commerce and the manufacturing industry. From the expenditure perspective, private consumption and net external demand stood out.

In the financial sphere, Paraguay has a solid banking system, with low levels of non-performing loans, adequate liquidity and high solvency ratios. Credit to the private sector showed significant growth, with historically low lending interest rates. Likewise, the current account of the balance of payments recorded a surplus in 2017, 2020 and 2023, largely due to the contributions of the agro-export sector and renewable energy generation10.

Foreign trade represented 83,1% of PIB in 2023. Agro-industrial products, led by the soybean complex (42,9%), beef (13,7%) and cereals (10,5%), make up more than 70% of exports. The manufacturing industry contributed 12,7% of exports, with items such as auto parts, chemicals and pharmaceutical products standing out11.

The Paraguayan State has initiated key institutional reforms to optimize the use of public resources and improve the efficiency of the state apparatus. In 2023, the creation of the Ministry of Economy and Finance (MEF) was finalized, integrating functions of the former Ministry of Finance, the Technical Secretariat for Economic and Social Development Planning (STP) and the Secretariat of Public Service (SFP).

In parallel, the National Directorate of Tax Revenue (DNIT) was established through Law No. 7.143/2023, unifying the National Customs Directorate and the Undersecretariat of Taxation, with the aim of strengthening revenue collection and modernizing fiscal controls.

The agricultural and livestock sector continues to be the cornerstone of the Paraguayan economy, directly contributing 10,5% of PIB and 20,5% if agro-industries are considered.

The sector represents 74% of total exports and employs 17,6% of the economically active population. According to the VI National Agricultural Census (CAN 2022), the area of agricultural farms amounts to 30,4 million hectares, which comprise about 291.497 agricultural farms, of which 89% are linked to family farming12.

The CAN 2022 highlights a growing participation of women producers, reaching 37%, and a notable dynamism of rural value chains. In addition, 93% of the cultivated area uses the direct seeding system, consolidating Paraguay as a benchmark in conservation agriculture and the reduction of greenhouse gas emissions.

In 2022, the cattle herd amounted to 13,5 million head, with more than 1,2 million registered in the national traceability system (SITRAP). In 2023, Paraguay ranked as the world's 8th largest beef exporter, generating about USD 1.500 million in foreign exchange. Thanks to its production model based on natural pastures and low chemical intervention, the country has the potential to become a global supplier of sustainable beef.

Paraguay is also a leader in hydroelectric power generation, with surpluses exported to Brazil and Argentina through the binational Itaipú and Yacyretá, and the national Acaray plant. In 2024, progress was made in the formulation of a National Green Hydrogen Strategy, as a key step towards energy diversification. In parallel, important steps have been taken in the promotion of electric mobility, with the design of public policies, tax incentives and regulatory frameworks aimed at facilitating the adoption of electric vehicles, as well as the progressive expansion of charging infrastructure, as part of the transition towards a cleaner and more efficient transport system.

Finally, Paraguay continues to bet on trade integration and the attraction of investment as engines of growth.

Economic policy is oriented towards diversifying the productive matrix, promoting free competition, strengthening technological innovation and ensuring environmental sustainability as axes of development.

1.2.5. Institutional arrangements

Paraguay has consolidated a robust legal and institutional architecture on environmental and climate matters, aligned with the international commitments undertaken by the country.

In 2000, through Law No. 1.561/00, the Secretariat of the Environment (SEAM) was created as the governing authority on environmental matters, responsible for applying the United Nations Framework Convention on Climate Change, approved by Law No. 251/93, whose instrument of ratification was submitted on 24 February 1994. This ratification formalised, from 1994, the country's international obligations on climate change, which were later joined by the ratification of the Kyoto Protocol (approved by Law No. 1.447/99) and of the Paris Agreement (approved by Law No. 5.681/16).

The institutionalisation of the climate approach was strengthened with Law No. 5.875/17, which defines the general regulatory framework for the response to climate change. This legislation creates the National Climate Change Commission (CNCC), an inter-institutional body with multisectoral representation, initially composed of 26 institutions, which has grown to 38 members and may incorporate new entities by majority decision of its members.

Likewise, this law creates the National Directorate of Climate Change (DNCC) as the executive body of the Ministry of Environment and Sustainable Development (MADES), which is responsible for coordinating the preparation of the national communications and the biennial transparency reports that must be reported to the United Nations Framework Convention on Climate Change (CMNUCC). Furthermore, the DNCC acts as the main coordinator of the process of building and updating the nationally determined contributions, as well as the other public policy instruments related to climate change, respecting the institutional mandates of the other State bodies.

In 2018, the Secretariat of the Environment (SEAM) was elevated to ministerial rank through Law No. 6.123/18, acquiring the new name of Ministry of Environment and Sustainable Development (MADES). Since then, its institutional structure has been oriented towards the effective implementation of environmental and climate policies, sectoral planning, decentralisation and technical coordination.

1.2.6.Emissions profile

Paraguay submitted its latest National Greenhouse Gas Inventory (INGEI) within the framework of the First Biennial Transparency Report (1BTR)13, covering the period 1990–2021. In 2021 the country's gross emissions reached a total of 79.585,1 kilotonnes of CO2 equivalent (kt CO2eq), while removals by sinks were 43.613,3 kt CO2eq, resulting in net emissions estimated at 35.971,8 kt CO2eq. This figure represents approximately 0,06 % of the global total14, placing Paraguay among the countries in the region with the lowest emissions in absolute terms.

On a per capita basis, the country's emissions were 4,9 tonnes of CO2eq, below the world average (6,5 t CO2eq) and well below the average of industrialised countries (G20: 7,9 t CO2eq). The country's performance shows that, although Paraguay contributes a minimal fraction of global emissions, it maintains a firm and growing commitment to climate action. 1.2.7. Climate vulnerability

Paraguay is a country vulnerable to the effects of climate change due to its status as a Landlocked Developing Country (PDSL) and its dependence on climate conditions for income generation, as well as the associated social and ecological impacts. Since 2015, the country has established adaptation as a national priority, a decision that has been confirmed through various national policies.

Historically, Paraguay has been affected by variations in climate patterns associated with the El Niño/Southern Oscillation phenomenon, which have caused droughts and floods with significant consequences for agricultural and livestock activities and river transport. Among the main climate risks identified are heatwaves, storms, forest fires and frosts, the disruption of river traffic, on which a large part of international trade depends, and the impacts on electricity generation, which comes almost 100 % from hydroelectric sources. All these risks are closely linked to water availability, productive capacity and the country's economic growth.

The 2019 Climate Study of Paraguay15 describes how climate impacts have intensified in recent decades. Over the last forty years, the frequency of extreme events associated with heatwaves has tripled, and droughts have become increasingly severe. 2019 was the warmest year on record in the country, with an average temperature 1,5 °C higher than the average for the period 1961–1990. In addition, eight of the ten warmest years have occurred in the 21st century, confirming a clear warming trend. This trend is also observed in the sustained increase in average temperatures by five-year period and decade, especially since the 1990s.

The 2022 drought represented a considerable difficulty for the national economy, given that soybean production fell by more than 60%. Despite this, GDP did not experience a significant contraction, achieving growth of 0,2%, according to data from the Central Bank of Paraguay.

In terms of vulnerability, the index developed in 2023 for the country's seven priority sectors, using the ND-GAIN index, identified the departments of Asunción, Cordillera and Ñeembucú as the most vulnerable in the ecosystems and biodiversity sector. In the health sector, Alto Paraguay presents the highest level of vulnerability, while in the agricultural and livestock sector the most vulnerable areas are found in the north of the Eastern region, covering San Pedro, Amambay, Concepción, Canindeyú, Caaguazú, Caazapá, Alto Paraná and Guairá. Regarding water vulnerability, determined by the use of the resource and its exposure to extreme events, the Tebicuary basin is the most critical, followed by those of the Salado river and Lake Ypacaraí, as well as those of the Apa, Tagatiyá and Jejuí Guazú rivers. In the cities and communities sector, the Department of Itapúa has been identified as the most vulnerable16.

These changes reflect a progressive intensification of extreme phenomena, with environmental, social and economic repercussions. In this context, climate change will have impacts on Paraguay, directly affecting its water resources, its economy and the well-being of its population. Rising temperatures and greater precipitation variability could make droughts up to four times longer by the end of the century, reducing soil moisture and affecting agricultural production, hydroelectric generation and river transport, on which 87% of exports and imports depend. This situation could increase logistics costs by between 15% and 58% per tonne, with losses of between 61 and 403 million dollars per year.

Extreme heat would increase the mortality rate by 22% by 2050, double hospitalisations and almost triple medical visits, while forest fires could increase by 46 % and deaths related to fine particulate pollution by more than 150%.

In addition, national GDP could decrease by between 0,5% and 3,1% by 2050 under high-emissions scenarios, and poverty would increase, with greater impacts in rural areas, especially in agricultural departments such as Caaguazú, San Pedro and Caazapá17.

In summary, climate trends for Paraguay project a future with higher temperatures, more unpredictable precipitation and more frequent extreme events, with economic, social and environmental impacts of great magnitude

Therefore, it is essential that, at both the national and subnational levels, vulnerabilities are identified, how and whom they affect, and that adaptation actions are planned, implemented and monitored as to how they increase resilience or reduce vulnerability.

1.2.8. Climate change public policies

Paraguay has developed a public policy framework oriented towards climate action based on the international commitments undertaken within the framework of the United Nations Framework Convention on Climate Change (CMNUCC) and the Paris Agreement. These policies provide strategic guidelines and multisectoral planning tools to advance towards a just, resilient and inclusive transition. All the public policies detailed below are fully aligned with Paraguay's Nationally Determined Contribution (NDC), which acts as the framework public policy on climate change and the main guidance for the country's climate action.

National Development Plan Paraguay 2030:

The PND 2030 steers public policies and Government actions towards the achievement of sustainable development, with long-term targets and with the active participation of all State institutions, civil society, the private sector and academia. For its part, the National Development Plan Paraguay 2050 (PND 2050) is in the final phase of formulating its strategic framework, which includes pillars, strategic and specific objectives, strategic actions and monitoring indicators. The projection of a new roadmap to 2050 is based on the fact that the PND 2030 is reaching its midterm, and that updated statistical information is now available at the national level (recent censuses) together with unified governance between planning and budgeting, integrated in the recently created Ministry of Economy and Finance (MEF).

National Climate Change Policy (PNCC):

1.2.9.Linkage with other international treaties, frameworks and agendas

The three Rio Conventions18—the United Nations Framework Convention on Climate Change19, the United Nations Convention to Combat Desertification20 and the Convention on Biological Diversity21— constitute the main multilateral instruments for addressing global environmental challenges in a coordinated manner. These conventions, although with specific mandates, share complementary objectives and constantly promote coordination and the creation of synergies as a means of strengthening their impacts, avoiding isolated efforts and maximising the environmental, social and economic benefits of their actions.

In this regard, within the framework of this update of the NDC 3.0 and the 2CA, an effort is being made to identify possible synergies and strategic linkages that can be worked on with a view to contributing to the achievement of the objectives and targets established in these international agendas.

Within the framework of the Rio Conventions, coordinated work is carried out among the MADES units responsible for implementing the CDB, the CNULD and the CMNUCC. This work aims to identify points of convergence, reinforce the synergy between these frameworks and ensure coherence in the planning and execution of national policies, promoting an integrated approach that maximises the impact of actions on climate action, biodiversity conservation, ecosystem restoration and combating desertification.

For the linkage with the CDB, the NDC 3.0 and the 2CA are articulated mainly through the ENPAB, which establishes national priorities on the conservation, sustainable use and restoration of biodiversity. For the linkage with the CNULD, the NDC 3.0 and the 2CA are articulated through the ENNDT, which guides national policies to prevent degradation, restore ecosystems and strengthen resilience to drought.

The three Rio Conventions drive climate action, biodiversity conservation and the fight against desertification.

1.3. CROSS-CUTTING AXES

Paraguay has defined a series of cross-cutting axes that will guide the planning and implementation of climate change mitigation and adaptation actions. These axes constitute guiding principles to ensure that the transition towards a sustainable development model is carried out in a just, inclusive and equitable manner, leaving no one behind. They represent a roadmap that integrates social, economic, environmental, cultural and technological dimensions, in recognition of the diversity of the territory and national capacities.

Just Transition.

Paraguay incorporates the just transition approach as a guiding principle of its climate action, recognising the need to advance towards a low-emission and resilient development model. This approach seeks to ensure that the processes of productive, technological and social transformation resulting from climate action are inclusive, equitable and participatory, protecting the rights of workers and of the most vulnerable communities. Within this framework, the creation of green, dignified and decent jobs is promoted, along with labour reconversion with access to technical and vocational training, and the inclusion of women, young people and Indigenous Peoples in strategic sectors of the economy. Likewise, priority is given to inter-institutional coordination and the alignment of public policies that integrate social protection, economic diversification and the strengthening of local capacities. The just transition in Paraguay is conceived as an opportunity to build a more inclusive and resilient economy that combines environmental sustainability with social and territorial justice.

Equal opportunities between men and women.

Paraguay incorporates equal opportunities between men and women in a cross-cutting manner in its NDC 3.0, recognising that climate change generates differentiated impacts and that women and girls face greater barriers to adapting and accessing the benefits of climate action. This approach seeks not only to avoid the reproduction of existing inequalities, but also to actively contribute to overcoming them through empowerment, effective participation and equitable access to resources, technologies, financing and decision-making processes. Climate action will be designed and implemented taking into account the needs, capacities and experiences of men and women, with special attention to rural and Indigenous contexts, following an approach of diversity, equity, inclusion and justice.

Community-led, prioritising the perspectives of young people and Indigenous communities.

Paraguay recognises that legitimate, effective and sustainable climate action must be deeply rooted in the voices and needs of communities. Therefore, the NDC is built on a society-led approach that promotes the active and meaningful participation of young people and Indigenous Peoples as key actors in the climate response. These populations not only face differentiated impacts of climate change, but also possess knowledge, traditional practices and innovative capacities that are fundamental for resilience and sustainability. Paraguay commits to creating spaces for intercultural and intergenerational dialogue, guaranteeing mechanisms for free, prior and informed consultation, and fostering inclusive decision-making from the local to the national level. By centring the perspectives of young people and Indigenous Peoples, the country drives a climate transformation rooted in social justice, intergenerational equity and respect for cultural and territorial diversity.

Risk management and reduction.

Risk management and reduction constitutes a key and cross-cutting axis in the implementation of Paraguay's NDC, with a central role in adaptation actions and a strategic contribution to mitigation. Recognising that adverse climate impacts can undermine progress in sustainable development, Paraguay promotes a preventive, systemic and evidence-based approach to identify threats, reduce vulnerabilities, increase ecological and socioeconomic resilience and strengthen institutional, community and technical capacities. In particular, priority is given to consolidating tools such as early warning systems, climate risk analyses and emergency response plans, which not only increase the resilience of communities and ecosystems, but also support proactive planning in sectors such as energy, agriculture and water resources. Risk management is integrated into national and local planning, fostering inter-institutional coordination, community participation and the use of climate information for coordinated, effective and knowledge-based action. By positioning itself as a cross-cutting component of climate strategies, this approach strengthens the country's adaptive capacities while contributing to a just transition.

Regulatory aspects.

Paraguay has a national legal and regulatory framework that enables multisectoral, multilevel and rights-based climate action. The National Climate Change Policy establishes the basis for the formulation, execution and evaluation of public policies on climate change, as well as the institutional and participation mechanisms required for their implementation. Through this policy, the importance of integrating climate change into development plans, territorial planning and sectoral policies is recognised, as well as of fostering inter-institutional cooperation and coordination with subnational governments. Complementarily, the National Environmental Policy, the National Development Plan, the Adaptation Communications, the National Climate Change Adaptation Plan, and other strategic frameworks reinforce the country's commitment to climate action consistent with the principles of the CMNUCC and the Paris Agreement. This regulatory framework is aligned with the international commitments undertaken by Paraguay and enables the consolidation of an institutional architecture oriented towards the effective, inclusive and transparent implementation of its NDC. In addition to the environmental legal framework, Paraguay has complementary public policies that strengthen climate action and contribute to a just transition, particularly from the labour and social dimension of sustainable development. The National Employment Plan and the Decent Work Programme, developed in coordination with tripartite actors and with the support of the International Labour Organization (OIT), integrate objectives linked to the creation of green jobs, training for new job skills, and the inclusion of vulnerable populations in strategic sectors of the green economy. These instruments offer concrete guidelines for linking climate policies with social protection and the formalisation of employment. Their alignment with the NDC reinforces institutional coherence and the rights-based approach that underpins national climate policy.

Action for Climate Empowerment (ACE).

Paraguay recognises that the empowerment of people and communities is fundamental to driving ambitious, sustained and transformative climate action. In this regard, capacity-building is promoted in the areas of education, technical training, access to information, public participation, awareness-raising and international cooperation, consistent with the six elements of ACE established by the CMNUCC. Through the implementation of national education and communication actions, the aim is to integrate climate change at all levels of the education system, raise awareness and strengthen community action. Likewise, equitable access to climate information and the participation of all sectors in the processes of formulating, implementing and monitoring climate policies are promoted. ACE is consolidated as a key enabling axis to strengthen social ownership of climate action and guarantee its legitimacy, effectiveness and inclusion. Paraguay reaffirms its commitment to the active participation of communities in the formulation of public policies that reduce climate vulnerability, foster sustainability and strengthen communities' capacity to respond to the challenges of climate change.

Technology and research.

The strengthening of national capacities in science, technology and innovation constitutes an enabling axis for ambitious, effective and sustainable climate action in Paraguay. The country recognises the importance of promoting the generation, transfer and adoption of technologies appropriate to the national context, with an emphasis on nature-based solutions, clean technologies, climate information systems and sustainable productive practices. Likewise, priority is given to scientific research as a basis for the formulation of evidence-based public policies, and to strengthening the capacities of national research institutions and agricultural and environmental extension systems. Coordination with academic centres, local communities and regional and international knowledge networks is key to promoting inclusive technological innovation processes that respond to the needs of the territory and contribute to a just and resilient transition.

Paraguay recognises that the empowerment of people and communities is fundamental to driving ambitious, sustained and transformative climate action.

Financing and innovation.

The mobilisation of climate finance and the promotion of innovation constitute fundamental enabling elements for the effective and sustained implementation of Paraguay's NDC. The country recognises the need to access adequate, predictable and equitable sources of financing, both national and international, that make it possible to scale up mitigation and adaptation solutions aligned with sustainable development priorities. Within this framework, the strengthening of regulatory and operational frameworks is promoted to channel public and private resources towards strategic climate investments, with an emphasis on sectors such as renewable energy, resilient infrastructure, sustainable agriculture and forest management.

Likewise, Paraguay is committed to financial innovation through mechanisms such as green bonds, blended finance schemes and results-based tools, which facilitate the participation of the private sector and public-private partnerships. The linkage between financing and technological and social innovation will make it possible to advance towards transformative, inclusive and resilient development models, ensuring a just climate transition consistent with the principles of the Paris Agreement.

Circular economy.

The circular economy is established as a cross-cutting axis in Paraguay's NDC, guiding both adaptation and mitigation strategies through an efficient productive approach.

This approach seeks to move from a linear model to one that promotes the prevention, reduction, reuse and recovery of waste. Along these lines, the Ministry of Industry and Commerce (MIC) leads a circular economy driving group, made up of public, private, academic and civil society institutions, with the aim of generating policies, regulations and capacities that accelerate the transition towards a more sustainable model. The circular economy is integrated into the NDC to advance towards inclusive, sustainable and resilient economic development.

Energy efficiency.

Energy efficiency constitutes a strategic cross-cutting axis in the implementation of the NDC, as a key enabler for reducing greenhouse gas emissions, promoting energy security and strengthening economic competitiveness. Its incorporation responds to the country's commitment to advance towards a more sustainable, resilient and technologically efficient development matrix, with full respect for the principles of equity, inclusion and co-responsibility.

1.4. BUILDING PARAGUAY'S NDC 3.0

The process of building Paraguay's NDC 3.0 and 2CA has been structured in the following stages:

Stage 1. Launch.

This phase marked the beginning of a strategic, technical and multisectoral process, whose objective is to update national commitments based on data and evidence through contributions from the different actors in society.

In May 2025 the Launch Workshop22 was held, officially starting the participatory construction process of the Third Nationally Determined Contribution (NDC 3.0) and the Second Adaptation Communication (2CA), attended by more than 150 people interested in contributing to the process, ensuring alignment with national priorities. In this space, the NDC 3.0 tracking portal was presented, as a component of the Climate Transparency Platform23.

Likewise, in June 2025, Mr. Rolando de Barros Barreto, Minister of Environment and Sustainable Development, through MADES Resolution No. 281/2025, ordered the start of the participatory and multisectoral construction of the NDC 3.0 and 2CA. This stage included the following activities:

  • Designation of technical focal points from government institutions, academia, trade associations and civil society organisations that will accompany the process.
  • Formation of adaptation and mitigation working groups among the different MADES units through ministerial resolutions.
  • Ordinary meeting with the National Climate Change Commission (CNCC).
Stage 2. Participatory construction process

During this phase, working spaces were developed to define the substantive content of the NDC 3.0 and the 2CA:

  • The mitigation measures of this NDC 3.0 were built through a participatory process in which more than 40 public and private institutions were consulted through bilateral meetings, working groups and territorial tours, making more than 550 contacts (40% men and 60% women) (See A1 of the Technical Annex of the NDC 3.0).
  • The adaptation objectives and lines of action (2CA) were updated through an iterative process of consultations with representatives of 71 public and private bodies, carried out through bilateral meetings.

In this update process approximately 215 contacts were made, of which 62% were women and 38% men, which ensured broad and diverse participation in defining sectoral adaptation priorities (See A17 of the Technical Annex of the NDC 3.0).

Stage 3. Public Consultation

The public consultation process on the draft of Paraguay's NDC 3.0 and 2CA was carried out from 25 August to 26 September 2025, through the Climate Transparency Platform24; interested people accessed the document (around 3.000 people) and shared their suggestions, comments and queries through predetermined forms.

In-person and virtual spaces for disseminating the document were also held, in which more than 1.770 people participated (See A19 of the Technical Annex of the NDC 3.0).

Likewise, different methodologies were used to reach the population, including podcasts and live broadcasts on MADES social media.

Stage 4. Submission of the final document

Based on the inputs received during the public consultation, the final document of the NDC 3.0 and the 2CA was consolidated. This stage included:

  • Systematisation and analysis of the comments received.
  • Preparation of a report on the handling of comments, detailing which were incorporated, modified or discarded, with due justification.
  • Final technical adjustments to the content.
  • High-level political validation and approval of the final document.
Stage 5. Submission to the CMNUCC

The final document of the NDC 3.0 was officially submitted to the Secretariat of the United Nations Framework Convention on Climate Change (CMNUCC), complying with the deadlines and formal requirements established in the Paris Agreement, marking a milestone for Paraguay's climate action. Stage 6. Dissemination

Subsequently, the process of disseminating the final document was initiated, through:

  • Production of accessible communication materials (videos, infographics, executive summaries, etc.).
  • Organisation of workshops and information sessions at the national and regional levels.

A BaU scenario of net GHG emissions refers to a projection of how net GHG emissions would evolve if the trends observed in the reference periods (years prior to the implementation of the NDC) continue without significant changes. In the context of NDCs, BaU scenarios reflect the estimates and projections of net GHG emissions without the implementation of mitigation measures.

This NDC 3.0 presents an update of the BaU scenario reported in the 2021 NDC and includes updated input data (annual net GHG emissions in kt CO2eq) from the INGEI (the one reported in the First Biennial Transparency Report (1BTR)) and methodological and unified improvements for the sectoral BaU scenarios.

3.2. CLIMATE CHANGE MITIGATION MEASURES TO 2035

3.2.1. Introduction

Climate change mitigation measures comprise the actions that the country, through its different stakeholders, commits to advancing in implementation in order to achieve the national goal of reducing greenhouse gas (GEI) emissions. The measures defined in this NDC 3.0 were determined through a participatory and multisectoral process that included bilateral meetings, workshops, territorial tours and data collection and processing (See A1 of the Technical Annex of the NDC 3.0 for details of the participatory process for the construction of the mitigation measures).

The mitigation measures are grouped into the sectors established by the Intergovernmental Panel on Climate Change (IPCC): 1) Agriculture and Livestock, 2) Land Use, Land-Use Change and Forestry (UTCUTS), 3) Energy and Transport, 4) Industrial Processes and Product Use (IPPU), and 5) Waste, and a measure cutting across all sectors is also added, 6) Cross-cutting. These measures have a time horizon to 2035 aligned with this update of the NDC.

The cross-cutting mitigation measure originates from the regulations in force relating to carbon credits (Law N°7190/2023 on Carbon Credits), whose Article 10 – Transfer of Carbon Credit Titles establishes that “In order to safeguard compliance with the Nationally Determined Contributions of the Republic of Paraguay, the Holders of Carbon Credits are obliged to retain and not transfer a percentage of said Carbon Credits derived from the same Project of 3% (three percent) and up to a maximum of 10% (ten percent), to be determined by the Enforcement Authority”.

The following sections present a summary of all the mitigation measures, while the annexes of the Technical Annex of the NDC 3.0 and 2CA present the full fact sheets with the details of each one. Likewise, the section on “Monitoring, Reporting and Verification (MRV) System for the Mitigation Measures” presents a summary of the monitoring, reporting and verification (MRV) system planned for each mitigation measure.

4. SECOND ADAPTATION COMMUNICATION

4.1. INTRODUCTION

The Second Adaptation Communication (2CA) of the Republic of Paraguay is submitted within the framework of Article 7 of the Paris Agreement, constituting the instrument for expressing national ambition on adaptation, in line with goal 2.1(c) set out in its Article 2: “Increasing the ability to adapt to the adverse impacts of climate change and foster climate resilience and low greenhouse gas emissions development, in a manner that does not threaten food production”.

The process of updating Paraguay’s 2CA is not an isolated event, but the result of a path travelled over a decade. It began in 2015 with the definition of national priorities on climate change adaptation, set out in Paraguay’s Intended Nationally Determined Contribution (INDC), which became the Nationally Determined Contribution in 2016 with the ratification of the Paris Agreement. Subsequently, in 2021, with the update of the Nationally Determined Contribution, the First Adaptation Communication was prepared, which defined sectoral objectives to guide adaptation action at the national and subnational levels.

Since then, the sectors have deepened their involvement, strengthened their capacities and consolidated their role as key actors, generating a working dynamic that, over time, gave rise to greater ambition, with concrete targets and an increasingly unified national discourse to address the multiple impacts of climate change. This 2CA is, therefore, the expression of that effort and of the collective will to move towards a sustainable and resilient Paraguay.

Paraguay submits its Adaptation Communications (CA) as part of its NDC, this being the main adaptation instrument established in the Paris Agreement and reflecting the importance of this subject as a national priority. This 2CA updates and expands on the progress of the 1CA, incorporating lessons learned, new approaches and results derived from a participatory process at the national level, with the aim of strengthening coherence between national and subnational policies, as well as international commitments on adaptation.

Paraguay’s Second Adaptation Communication (2CA) expresses the collective will to move towards a country that is sustainable and resilient to climate change.

The 2CA addresses seven priority sectors for adaptation, which together establish 25 objectives. For this update, these objectives were analysed using SMART criteria (Specific, Measurable, Achievable, Relevant and Time-bound), which made it possible to ensure the formulation of commitments that are concrete, measurable, achievable, pertinent and with a time horizon set between 2026 and 2035. This improvement with respect to previous processes represents a milestone at the national level.

During the process, the linkage of the sectoral adaptation objectives with the thematic targets of the GGA was also analysed, laying the foundations for reporting information in the next Global Stocktake of the Paris Agreement. In parallel, the linkage of the adaptation sectors with loss and damage issues was explored, generating inputs that will make it possible to take the first steps towards establishing national governance on this matter. The adaptation objectives and lines of action defined in this process respond to national priorities, and also contribute to other international treaties, including the Convention on Biological Diversity (CDB) and the United Nations Convention to Combat Desertification (CNULD).

Paraguay recognises the importance of adaptation at the national level and aims to achieve the 25 objectives established by 2035. Nevertheless, it must be recognised that such achievement is conditional on the availability of financial resources and on national capacities. As a Party to the CMNUCC and the Paris Agreement, the country will continue making efforts to access additional financing, from both multilateral and bilateral sources, that will enable it to advance effectively towards strengthening its adaptive capacity.

Paraguay reaffirms its commitment to climate change adaptation the through the update of its Second Adaptation Communication (2CA).

4.2. STRUCTURE OF THE SECOND ADAPTATION COMMUNICATION

Paraguay’s 2CA is structured in a way that facilitates a comprehensive understanding of its content, promoting the coherence, transparency and traceability of the information presented.

Its design responds to the provisions of the Paris Agreement and to the guidance agreed by the Parties in Decision 9/CMA.1, ensuring that the information is clear, complete and useful both for international assessment and for national planning and decision-making processes

4.2.1.Clarity, transparency and understanding

The 2CA begins with a component intended to facilitate the clarity, transparency and understanding of the information presented, in line with the elements set out in Decision 9/CMA.1 for adaptation communications.

This section incorporates key information that makes it possible to understand the approach and scope of the 2CA, addressing fundamental aspects such as the national circumstances, institutional arrangements and legal frameworks underpinning the country’s climate action; the impacts, risks and vulnerabilities identified in the different sectors; as well as the national adaptation priorities, strategies, policies, plans, targets and actions defined to strengthen long-term resilience.

Aspects linked to the element of the decision relating to implementation, progress, gaps and lessons learned will be addressed in complementary progress reporting documents, such as the National Communications and the Biennial Transparency Reports (see Chapter 2.2).

4.2.2.Main body: priority sectors, objectives and lines of action

The main body of the 2CA is organised around seven priority adaptation sectors defined at the national level and their lines of action. Each sector contains a set of objectives and their respective lines of action, which steer public policies and guide the implementation of concrete measures aimed at strengthening the country’s adaptive capacities.

The sectors are as follows:

  • Resilient Cities and Communities: 5 objectives and 28 lines of action. This is the sector with the largest number of initiatives, focused on integrating adaptation into subnational planning, restoring green areas as natural infrastructure, building resilient infrastructure, strengthening citizen and institutional capacities for risk management, and promoting adaptation in the tourism sector.
  • Health and Epidemiology: 4 objectives and 14 lines of action. It seeks to strengthen health response capacity to climate emergencies, consolidate epidemiological surveillance, promote research on climate-sensitive diseases and raise awareness among the population and health personnel.
  • Ecosystems and Biodiversity: 2 objectives and 15 lines of action. It focuses on increasing ecosystem resilience through Nature-based Solutions (SbN) and on strengthening the SINASIP, with attention to the conservation of threatened species and ecological connectivity.
  • Energy: 3 objectives and 13 lines of action. It includes actions to improve the supply of electricity in vulnerable communities, protect watersheds essential for hydroelectric generation and diversify the energy mix with renewable sources such as solar and wind.
  • Agriculture and Livestock, Forestry and Food Security: 5 objectives and 14 lines of action. It is aimed at fostering climate-adapted agricultural and livestock practices, strengthening food security, promoting research and innovation, and generating climate information for decision-making.
  • Water Resources: 4 objectives and 20 lines of action. It prioritises strengthening water management, access to safe water and sanitation, the promotion of efficient use and the protection of strategic ecosystems such as wetlands and aquifers.
  • Transport: 2 objectives and 2 lines of action. It focuses on planning and managing the navigability of transboundary rivers during droughts and on developing resilient road infrastructure that integrates climate risk assessments from the planning stage.

Each of these sectors articulates its objectives following SMART criteria (Specific, Measurable, Achievable, Relevant and Time-bound).

The lines of action represent the operational breakdown of these objectives, detailing the interventions needed to advance towards their achievement.

This chapter also reflects the continuous improvement of the national adaptation process, showing the evolution from the submission of the Initial Nationally Determined Contribution (INDC), through the First Adaptation Communication, to the present 2CA.

This process has made it possible to consolidate a more robust, coherent and participatory framework, incorporating new approaches, broadening the scope of actions and aligning national priorities with current and future climate challenges.

4.2.3.Technical annex: indicators, responsible parties and linkage with international processes

Complementing the main body, the 2CA includes a technical annex detailing key information linked to each objective and its lines of action (see Annexes of the Second Adaptation Communication - Technical Annex of the NDC 3.0). This annex incorporates:

  • Monitoring indicators, which make it possible to measure the direct result of the line of action.
  • Institutions responsible for implementation, in order to strengthen governance and ensure accountability.
  • Technical and documentary sources of information used as the basis for formulating and reviewing the objectives and lines of action.
  • Linkage with other CMNUCC processes, including the GGA under Article 7 and loss and damage under Article 8, both of the Paris Agreement, as well as with other international treaties such as the Convention on Biological Diversity (CDB) and the United Nations Convention to Combat Desertification (CNULD).

Additionally, the annex presents the methodological aspects used in the identification, prioritisation and formulation of the objectives, lines of action and indicators, as well as in the vulnerability analysis and adaptation planning processes.

These aspects were developed within the framework of a participatory construction and dissemination process with public institutions, private institutions, civil society organisations and subnational actors, ensuring the representation of the different sectors and strengthening the legitimacy, pertinence and relevance of the proposed actions.

5.2. CLIMATE FINANCE

Climate finance is a central pillar for the effective implementation of Paraguay's NDC 3.0, given that mitigation and adaptation actions require significant public and private investment. Despite national efforts to mobilise domestic resources, the country still faces structural limitations in accessing and channelling climate finance in a timely, sufficient and predictable manner. In this regard, the need is recognised for financial mechanisms aligned with national climate planning, integrating sustainability and transparency criteria.

In this context, the costing exercises carried out in Paraguay estimate that the financial needs for implementing mitigation measures amount to approximately USD 16.400 million50 to 2030, while adaptation actions will require around USD 6.500 million51 over the same period. These figures reflect the magnitude of the financial challenge and the urgency of articulating national and international mechanisms that guarantee adequate and balanced coverage of both dimensions of climate action. It should be noted that these calculations will need to be updated and adjusted in light of the commitments established in the NDC 3.0 and the guidelines contained in the Second Adaptation Communication.

As a significant advance, the Ministry of Economy and Finance (MEF) of Paraguay, created by Law No. 7158/202352 and regulated by Decree No. 1041/2024, has assumed a strategic role in the management of public resources, including those linked to climate action.

Within this framework, the MEF has developed a budget classifier53 that organises and codes the revenues, expenditures and funding sources of the PGN, allowing order and comparability at all stages of the budget cycle, both before and after its execution; and it has also developed green tagging54 as an analytical tool that, mainly ex post, indicates what part of the already approved or executed budget is allocated to public policies for climate change mitigation and adaptation.

This contributes to strengthening the traceability of public expenditure, improving the planning and allocation of resources, and facilitating accountability with respect to climate commitments. Its implementation constitutes a key tool for making visible the national budgetary effort in environmental matters and represents a technical basis for designing climate finance strategies that are more effective and coherent with the NDC.

In addition, Paraguay identifies that by improving the design of bankable proposals it will be able to move towards greater coordination with international climate funds. Although resources have already been channelled through funds such as the Green Climate Fund (GCF), the Adaptation Fund, the Global Environment Facility (GEF), and bilateral cooperation, technical challenges still persist in accessing climate finance in a more systematic and sustainable manner.

The country recognises that external financing (grants, concessional and non-concessional loans) will be necessary to fulfil part of the commitments of the NDC 3.0; it therefore proposes to consolidate a National Climate Finance Strategy that integrates green tagging, sectoral priorities, regulatory frameworks and international cooperation opportunities, within the framework of the CMNUCC decisions on long-term finance —Decision 6/CP.23 (para. 10) and its update in Decision 4/CP.28— which provide guidance on translating financing needs into action and strengthening developing countries' access to climate finance.

In this regard, the following axes of action to 2035 are proposed are:

  • Design and implement a national monitoring, reporting and verification (MRV) system for climate finance flows (tracking climate finance), which makes it possible to assess the alignment of mobilised resources with mitigation and adaptation targets, and to strengthen transparency and accountability to the international community.
  • Partnerships with business chambers or international platforms (e.g. IETA, VCMI) to position Paraguay as an exporter of high-integrity credits.
  • Develop a National Climate Finance Strategy.
  • Strengthen national mechanisms for accessing resources from multilateral (GCF, GEF, Adaptation Fund), bilateral and regional funds, through the development of technical and institutional capacities to prepare robust climate investment proposals, aligned with the priorities of the NDC 3.0
  • Develop technical capacities at the institutional, sectoral and subnational levels for the effective management of climate finance, including project formulation, financial structuring, environmental and social safeguards, and co-financing mechanisms.
  • Promote coordination spaces among the Ministry of Economy and Finance (MEF), MADES, the BCP, government institutions, subnational governments, the private sector and civil society organisations.

Paraguay recognises that the development, transfer and diffusion of environmentally sustainable technologies are fundamental pillars for achieving the objectives established in its Nationally Determined Contribution (NDC). In this context, the country has undertaken the commitment to strengthen the conditions for the adoption, generation and diffusion of climate technologies, in the field of both mitigation and adaptation.

In line with this commitment, Paraguay has developed its Technology Action Plan (TAP), within the framework of the global Technology Needs Assessment (TNA) initiative, led by the United Nations Environment Programme (PNUMA) and supported by the United Nations Framework Convention on Climate Change (CMNUCC). This Technology Action Plan identifies the priority technologies for the country in the sectors of energy, agriculture, water resources, forest management and land-use change, establishing specific roadmaps for their implementation, scaling-up and transfer.

Paraguay's TAP contemplates concrete actions to overcome the technical, institutional, financial and social barriers that limit the adoption of climate technologies. Likewise, it promotes international cooperation to facilitate the transfer of technologies from developed countries to Paraguay, fostering partnerships with research centres, cooperation agencies, the private sector and multilateral organisations.

In this regard, the country highlights the need to strengthen South-South technological cooperation mechanisms, and to generate enabling environments that incentivise local innovation, technological ownership and the development of national capacities for the management, operation and maintenance of climate technologies. The strengthening of the regulatory framework, access to financing for clean technologies, and the establishment of mechanisms for monitoring and evaluating technological impact are also identified as key.

  • Paraguay considers it a priority to strengthen institutional capacities for technology management, as well as to create instruments that facilitate the scaling-up of innovative technologies, particularly those adapted to rural, Indigenous and vulnerable contexts, with an emphasis on equity between men and women, and community participation.
  • In this regard, the following axes of action to 2035 are proposed:
  • The updating and institutionalisation of the TAP as a cross-cutting instrument.
  • The creation of a structured mechanism for monitoring and evaluating progress in the adoption of climate technologies.
  • The identification of indicators to measure the impact of technology transfer.
  • The active search for climate finance and private investment for key technologies.
  • The strengthening of local capacities for the development, adaptation and improvement of climate technologies.

5.4. ACCESS TO INFORMATION AND TRANSPARENCY

Paraguay has also initiated the process of establishing the Climate Transparency Platform to integrate information systems and the production of climate information, through the Environmental Information System (SIAM) of MADES, streamlining climate information management, planning, data collection, processing and analysis, publication and exchange of information, in addition to serving as an essential input for national decision-making, in line with the modalities, procedures and guidelines of the enhanced transparency framework under the Paris Agreement.

In addition, all public policy documents, reports and national processes related to climate change are built in a participatory manner, through events, workshops and meetings involving various actors from government, the private sector, academia, Indigenous Peoples, youth and civil society. These processes are documented and published transparently on the official MADES website and disseminated through its institutional social media. This practice strengthens public trust and guarantees access to climate information, promoting inclusive and evidence-based governance.
